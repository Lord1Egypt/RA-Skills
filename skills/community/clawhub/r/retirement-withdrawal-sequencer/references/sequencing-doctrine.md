# Sequencing Doctrine — which bucket, when, and why

## The default draw order (the engine's doctrine)
1. **Cash & interest inside taxable** — no transaction, tiny tax drag.
2. **Taxable-account long-term holdings** — realize gains, harvesting the 0% LTCG band first (up to ~$98.5k MFJ taxable income 2026 est.). Taxable accounts die with a basis step-up at death — spending them down *early* wastes that; spending them in the 0% band is free.
3. **Traditional (IRA/401k)** — pre-RMD years: withdraw/convert to *fill* the 12% bracket (or to an IRMAA-safe point). RMD years: the RMD is mandatory floor; anything above it is a choice.
4. **Roth last** — tax-free growth, no lifetime RMD, best inheritance asset (heirs get 10 years of continued shelter). The only reasons to touch Roth early: keeping MAGI under an IRMAA/ACA cliff in a specific year, or a big one-off expense.

## Why "taxable first" is wrong and "traditional first" is also wrong
- All-taxable-first drains the 0%-LTCG/step-up asset while leaving the traditional account to grow into forced RMDs that push 22–24%+ — the classic "RMD bracket spike."
- All-traditional-first wastes the low-bracket years: a retiree at $60k MAGI MFJ has ~$40k of 10–12% headroom that is *use-it-or-lose-it* annually.
- The optimum fills every low bracket every year — which is exactly what bracket-fill conversions do.

## Roth conversion windows
- **The window:** between retirement (income drops) and RMD start (73/75). Social Security claiming age shifts the math (delaying to 70 widens the window).
- **Convert to:** top of the 12% bracket by default; into 22% when projected RMDs + SS will land in 22–24% later anyway; NEVER across an IRMAA tier (unless the conversion is large enough to make the one-time tier crossing worth it — model both years).
- **Sequence risk bonus:** conversions in down markets buy more shares per tax dollar.
- **Irreversible:** recharacterization is gone (post-2017). Model first.
- **Watch state tax** (some states exempt conversion income, others don't) and **ACA cliffs if under 65** (sharper than IRMAA — different engine).

## IRMAA tactics
- Project MAGI two years ahead — that's the year whose premiums you're setting.
- A large one-off (Roth conversion, capital-gain harvest, home sale) can be split across December/January.
- QCD beats check-writing charity once RMDs begin: satisfies the RMD, excluded from MAGI entirely.
- Appeal paths: SSA-44 (work stoppage / marriage / death of spouse), or life-changing-event list; a one-time income spike alone does NOT qualify.

## Widow/er re-planning (the cruel year)
- Year of death: still MFJ. Next year: single brackets (~half the widths), single IRMAA tiers (~half the thresholds), one SS check.
- The survivor's RMDs suddenly loom larger — this is THE moment to have converted earlier. Re-run the sequencer with status S the year after a spouse's death; the "what if" run before that year arrives is worth thousands.

## Missed-RMD repair
1. Withdraw the shortfall now.
2. File Form 5329 Part IX requesting the waiver (reasonable cause: illness, bad custodian advice, error) — pay nothing up front if waiver is plausible, IRS has broadly granted 10% corrected-in-2-years relief.

## Sequence-of-returns guard
Hold 1–2 years of spending in cash/short Treasuries. In a drawdown year, spend cash and/or Roth (tax-free, no MAGI) rather than selling equities — selling stocks at −30% to fund a withdrawal is the loss you never recover. Refill the cash sleeve in strong years.

## Model limits (say them out loud)
- Ignores NIIT, state tax, SS benefit taxation interaction (85% rule), and the exact LTCG stacking interaction with bracket-fill withdrawals — it is a sequencer, not tax-prep software.
- Annual verify of brackets/IRMAA/RMD factors is part of the workflow, not optional.
