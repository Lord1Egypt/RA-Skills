# Check a virtual staging disclosure

Work from what the user is actually about to publish: the listing description, the photo caption, any staged-photo field the portal offers, and whether the published image itself carries a visible label. If they only describe the situation, ask for the exact wording and the portal or market. Answer in the user's language.

## Establish what was modified

- Which photos were edited, and how: an empty room furnished, a room restyled, objects removed, sky replaced, exposure corrected, or a 360° view assembled from several shots.
- Whether the edit adds or removes objects, surfaces, or a view that exists in the property.
- Whether exposure or colour work changed the apparent condition of the property.

Staging that adds furniture, a declutter that removes it, and a sky replacement are different claims to the buyer even when they share one photo gallery.

## Check the three places a disclosure goes missing

| Place | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| The image | A visible wording on or beside the photo. Roompano writes « mise en scène virtuelle » onto every generated image and adds an EXIF marker plus a C2PA manifest (content credentials). A label that exists only on the agency's own copy travels nowhere once the photo is re-uploaded or embedded. |
| The listing text | A line in the description or caption saying the image was digitally modified. With many photos, name the room or the photo number. |
| The portal | Some portals have a dedicated staged-photo or photo-credit field and require it. Check the portal's current rule instead of assuming it matches the last one you used. |

## Judge the wording

Adequate: it says the image was digitally modified, and it does not imply the furnishing is included.

- « Mise en scène virtuelle — mobilier ajouté numériquement, non inclus dans la vente. »
- « Photo retouchée par IA » when the edit is an exposure or colour correction rather than staging.
- "Virtually staged — furniture added digitally, not included in the sale."

Not adequate:

- Silence, or a credit that only names the photographer.
- Wording that turns the edit into a claim about the property: "furnished flat", "renovated", "open view" when the furnishing, the renovation or the view was generated.
- A disclaimer buried in the agency's general terms instead of on the listing.
- The label cropped, blurred or overlaid where it cannot be read on a phone.

## Rewrite it

Return one paste-ready sentence per language the user publishes in, then a short caption version and a fuller description version. Keep the caption within the portal's character limit.

Describe the obligation as a duty of transparency on the seller and the agent, and note that the exact requirement is set by the market and by the portal. Do not state a compliance verdict, do not promise that a listing is compliant, and do not cite an article number, a fine or a deadline unless the user supplies the text they are relying on. French consumer law treats a misleading omission as a deceptive practice, and the EU AI Act requires AI-generated or AI-manipulated image content to be identifiable; how that applies to one listing is a question for the portal and, where it matters, for a legal adviser.
