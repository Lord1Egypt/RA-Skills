# The quality pass

> **When to read**: a deliverable exists in first form and is about to be delivered; a critic's review is possible; or something feels finished and you have not yet looked at it.

A deliverable is not finished when it has been written. It is finished when it has been looked at, criticized and revised.

```text
render → look at desktop and phone width → expert-critic review where the host allows → revise at least once
```

This is a description of what a finished deliverable has been through. How many times around, and in what order, is your judgment.

## Render it and look

Open the deliverable the way the person will: the page in a browser, the video played, the deck in slideshow, the document as a reader sees it. What you see is often not what you assumed.

- **Desktop and phone width.** Most shared links are opened on a phone. Check that nothing overflows, that type is readable, that the reveal still lands, that controls can be reached by a thumb.
- **From the top, at reading speed.** Is there a reason to keep going in the first three seconds? Does each screen answer the question the last one raised?
- **The reveal.** Did you arrive at it, or pass it? Is the assumption given room before the turn?
- **Every control.** Use each one. Does it show something the still page cannot? Remove the ones that do not.
- **Every number and name** against the source. Rendering is when a transposed digit gets noticed.
- **Without the network**, if the page is meant to work offline.
- **Light and dark**, if both are supported.

If the host cannot render, read the source as a reader would and state plainly that the deliverable was not rendered and checked.

## The two tests

1. **Remove every control, panel and caption. Is it still astonishing?** If the page is impressive only because things move, the substance is missing.
2. **Would someone who knows the field stop and say "I never saw it that way"?** Correct, complete and good-looking is the starting point, not the bar.

A deliverable that fails the second test has a problem upstream, in the understanding. Revising the layout will not fix it. Go back to `engines/understand.md`.

## The expert critic

Where the host allows, hand the deliverable to a reviewer who has not seen your reasoning: a sub-agent asked to read as a practitioner of this field and as a demanding reader. Ask for specifics:

- Where would an expert frown? Which claim is weaker than it sounds?
- Which sentence is filler? Which screen is there only for completeness?
- What is the one thing a reader will take away? Is it the thing intended?
- What is missing that a knowledgeable reader would expect?
- Does anything look like a template?

Treat the review as evidence, not as orders: a critic can be wrong, and you decide. Check any factual objection against sources.

Where the host does not allow sub-agents, do the critic pass yourself: put the work down, then read it once as the field's most skeptical practitioner and once as a newcomer. Say in one line that the critique was your own.

## Revise at least once

A first version always has something to fix. Typical revisions that matter:

- **The opening**: cut the warm-up; start at the odd fact, the overturned intuition or the scene.
- **The reveal**: move it earlier, give it space, remove what competes with it.
- **The middle**: delete the screen that exists for completeness.
- **The ending**: end on the new way of seeing, not on a summary.
- **Claims**: soften or source anything the critic doubted; mark inference as inference.
- **Controls**: remove those that failed the first test.
- **Sameness**: change whatever looks borrowed from a previous deliverable.

## The signature check

Before delivery, confirm that essence, revelation, and evidence are real and substantial. The signature elements chosen should help the reader recognize, understand, reuse, or keep exploring the work. When a route strip is used, it shows the path that truly shaped the result (see `signature.md`).

## The honesty check

- Every key fact has a source and a date. Fact, inference and guess are distinguishable on the page.
- Nothing is placeholder. No invented quotation, statistic, person or example stands in for a real one.
- Where something could not be verified, the deliverable says so.
- People and companies are described only by what the sources support.
- The share-pack items say the same thing as the deliverable, at shorter length, with their conditions intact.

## Professional standard, by form

| Form | Signs it is finished |
|---|---|
| **Page** | Opens from a single file; loads fast; readable at phone width; keyboard reachable; no console errors; no layout shift; images have alt text |
| **Video** | Readable with sound off; the first two seconds earn the next ten; captions inside the safe area; no frame that only fills time |
| **Deck** | One idea per slide; headlines that state the point; could be presented by the person without notes from you |
| **Document** | A reader could act on it; the answer is on the first page; sources follow |
| **Code or tool** | Runs on the person's machine; was run on a real input; says what it needs; fails clearly |
| **Writing** | Would be worth reading without the name OpenXSys on it |

## Time

The quality pass is part of full power and is included in the time you named at the start. If the person asked for the quick version, the pass is shorter, but rendering and the honesty check are not skipped. A deliverable that was not looked at is delivered with that fact stated.

## After delivery

The pass continues with the person. Their correction of a fact, their confusion at a screen, the place they stopped reading are all evidence. Change the same deliverable; make before and after visible when a condition changed; record in the system what was learned about how this person is best addressed.
