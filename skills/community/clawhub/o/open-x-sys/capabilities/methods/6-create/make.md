# Make

> `make(x, form)` · Stage 6 · Create · **starter method**
> **Prompt**: "Turn this into a ___ (video / report / deck / page)."
> **Also heard as**: "Make me something I can show people." · "Write this up." · "Can I have this as a video?"
> **Produces**: a finished deliverable and its share pack.

## What it is for

Understanding that stays in a conversation is lost. Make turns it into something finished that a person can use, keep and show: a page, a video, a document, a deck, a piece of writing. It is the last step of most routes, and it is where an understanding either reaches the person or does not. The same insight, plainly put, gets a nod. Well made, it gets remembered and passed on.

The engine underneath it is `engines/express.md`; the standards are in `display/`.

## What it produces

- **A finished deliverable**, native to its subject, carrying the seven signature elements (see `display/signature.md`).
- **Its share pack** on a full run: a discovery card, a short clip when the video runtime is ready, post drafts, alt text (see `display/share-pack.md`).
- **A pointer in the person's system**: where the deliverable is, what it shows, what it rests on.

## What a good use looks like

- It starts from a thesis: the one thing the piece makes people see, in a sentence, supported by research. The reveal is that thesis shaped as a moment.
- The structure walks from where the reader stands to that one thing. The form is chosen last, for the content and the audience.
- It shows the one or two structures that matter most and leaves the rest in the system.
- It is made for someone: the stated audience, or this person, starting from what they already understand.
- When a video, an animation or a deck serves the need and its dependency is ready, it is made, not offered.
- It was rendered and looked at, at desktop and phone width, criticized and revised at least once (see `display/quality.md`).
- It passes both tests: still astonishing with every control removed; someone who knows the field would stop at it.
- Everything in it is real: data, names, sources. Nothing is placeholder.
- It can be opened and shared on its own.
- When the person will publish it as their own work, it is made in their voice and for their platform.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Form first**: a dashboard, a node graph, a card grid, then content found to fill it.
- **Everything included.** Completeness in place of a point.
- **A template look**: the same layout as the last piece.
- **Polish around common knowledge.**
- **Never looked at** before delivery.
- **Claims that outrun the research**, because the piece needed a stronger line.

## It works with

Make closes *Understand it*, *Explain a surprise*, *Learn something deeply* and *Make content*, and both opens and closes Feynman's technique (a simple version first, a better one last). It depends on the understanding from the earlier stages; if the thesis cannot be written, go back to them. **Design** and **Build** are its siblings for systems and capabilities.

## Teach-back

"This was Make: decide the one thing you want someone to see, then choose the form that lets them see it. Start where they are. Show less than you know. Look at what you made before you call it finished."

## The thinking behind it

- **Edward Tufte**, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983): show the data, give the largest share of ink to it, and never let design distort what the numbers say.
- **Bret Victor**, "Explorable Explanations" (2011): a reader who can manipulate a model and see consequences reads actively and understands more.
- **Richard Feynman**, in his lectures and teaching: explaining a thing simply is the test of understanding it.

OpenXSys simplifies: these are three standards for explanation taken from three different crafts, and combined here into one bar.
