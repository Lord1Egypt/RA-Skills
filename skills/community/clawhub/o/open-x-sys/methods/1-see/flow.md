# Flow

> `flow(x)` · Stage 1 · See
> **Prompt**: "Follow the money (people / information / attention) through ___."
> **Also heard as**: "Where does the money actually come from?" · "Where does the time go?" · "How does work move through ___?"
> **Reveals**: what accumulates, what flows, where it piles up.

## What it is for

Much of how a system behaves is decided by what moves through it and what builds up in it. Flow follows one thing (money, people, material, energy, information, attention, work in progress) from where it enters to where it leaves, and finds where it accumulates, where it is delayed and where it leaks. It turns a description of parts into a description of movement, and it very often shows that the thing people watch is not the thing that matters.

## What it reveals

- **Stocks**: what accumulates and can be counted at a moment: cash, inventory, backlog, debt, users, skill, trust.
- **Flows**: the rates that fill and drain the stocks: revenue per month, hires per quarter, tickets closed per day.
- **Where it piles up**: a stock that keeps growing marks a constraint just downstream of it.
- **Where it leaks**: a flow that goes somewhere nobody intended.
- **Delays**: how long between something entering and its effect appearing.

## What a good use looks like

- It chooses the one or two flows that decide this subject. In a media business that may be attention before money; in a factory, material; in a research group, people.
- Stocks and flows are kept apart. Revenue is a flow, cash is a stock, and confusing them is a classic error.
- There are quantities with units and dates, even rough ones. A flow with no numbers is a slogan.
- It checks conservation: what enters either leaves or accumulates. If the numbers do not balance, something is missing, and what is missing is often the finding.
- It follows the flow across the boundary: where it comes from before the system and where it goes after.
- It notices which stock changes slowly. Slow stocks (reputation, infrastructure, skill) give a system its inertia.

## Where it goes wrong

- **A list of flows** with arrows and no magnitudes.
- **Only money.** Money is the easiest flow to find and often not the one that decides.
- **A snapshot** presented as a flow: one period's figures with no rates of change.
- **Ignoring delays**, so that cause and effect seem unrelated.
- **A diagram of the accounting categories** instead of what actually moves.

## It works with

Flow follows **Open** and pairs with **Loops** (loops are made of flows that feed back on stocks). A pile-up points to the **Bottleneck**. Its numbers come from **Size It** when no figures exist. It is the first step of Shell's scenarios (driving forces) and central to the Toyota way.

## Teach-back

"This was Flow: pick the thing that moves, follow it from entry to exit, and separate what accumulates from the rate at which it moves. Wherever it piles up or leaks, look there."

## The thinking behind it

- **Jay W. Forrester**, *Industrial Dynamics* (1961): the behavior of an enterprise arises from its stocks, flows, delays and feedback.
- **John Sterman**, *Business Dynamics* (2000): stocks and flows as the basis of dynamic behavior, and people's difficulty in reasoning about accumulation.
- **Taiichi Ohno**, *Toyota Production System* (1978): make work flow, and treat piled-up inventory as something that hides problems.

OpenXSys simplifies: system dynamics builds quantitative simulation models; Flow uses the same distinction to see clearly, and builds a model only when the dynamics are the insight.
