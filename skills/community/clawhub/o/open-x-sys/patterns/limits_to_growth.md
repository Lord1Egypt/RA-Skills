# Limits to growth

> A reinforcing process meets a balancing one. Growth slows, stops or reverses as it approaches a limit, and pushing harder on what used to work stops helping.

## The structure

A reinforcing loop produces growth. As the thing grows, it draws nearer to a limit: a capacity, a market size, a resource, the attention of its managers, the quality it can sustain. A balancing loop tied to that limit strengthens with size and slows the growth. The limit is often inside the system and often invisible until it binds.

## How to recognize it

- A period of growth, then a slowdown **despite the same or greater effort**.
- The response is to push harder on the growth engine: more sales effort, more features, more hours. Results per unit of effort keep falling.
- Somewhere, something is saturating: delivery times lengthen, quality slips, key people are overloaded, the easy customers are gone.
- People explain the slowdown by motivation or execution.

## Where it usually breaks

The pattern is itself a description of how growth breaks. What matters is how it is handled:

- **The limit is misidentified.** Effort goes to the growth loop, which is not the problem.
- **The limit is internal and unwelcome**: the founder's time, the team's skill, the quality of the product.
- **Pushing harder makes it worse**, by pressing harder against the limit: more sales with the same delivery capacity produces more unhappy customers.
- **Overshoot.** With a delay between growth and the signal of the limit, the system runs past what it can sustain and falls back.

## Where to push

- **Work on the limit, not on the growth engine.** Find what is saturating and raise it, remove it, or change the system so it no longer applies.
- **Look for the next limit early.** Removing one reveals another (see [the moving bottleneck](moving_bottleneck.md)). Ask at each stage what will limit growth at the next size.
- **Decide whether to grow.** Sometimes the right response to a limit is to stay at a size that works.

## What to check before naming it

Identify the reinforcing loop, then find what is saturating and show the evidence. If nothing is saturating, the slowdown has another cause.

## Sources

- **Peter Senge**, *The Fifth Discipline* (1990): the "limits to growth" archetype and its principle, to remove the limiting factor instead of pushing on growth.
- **Donella Meadows, Dennis Meadows, Jørgen Randers and William Behrens**, *The Limits to Growth* (1972): a system dynamics study of growth against finite limits at world scale, from which the name comes.

The **Bottleneck** method finds the limit; the **Scale** lens helps anticipate it.
