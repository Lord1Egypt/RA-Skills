# Everyday routes, and how one is chosen

> **When to read**: you are choosing a route from one sentence; no listed route fits; you are adapting a route to a subject; or the person asks what routes exist.

A **route** is a sequence of methods across the stages of the journey. Methods are rarely used alone, because a good answer usually depends on more than one stage. Routes come in three kinds: **everyday** routes (this page), **master** routes (`masters/`) and **personal** routes (in the person's system; see `system/growth.md`).

A route is a default you adapt. It records which methods usually serve a kind of request. It does not tell you what to do next.

## The eight everyday routes

| Route | Methods | Typical request | What the person ends up with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Understand it** | Open → X-ray → Flow or Loops → Make | "Help me really understand ___." | A deliverable that shows how the thing works, built around one reveal |
| **Explain a surprise** | X-ray → Trace → Incentives → Make | "Why did ___ happen?" | The structure and history that made the surprising thing unsurprising |
| **Solve a recurring problem** | X-ray → Loops → Bottleneck → Leverage → Best Practice → Build → Review | "___ keeps happening. How do we fix it for good?" | The loop that keeps producing the problem, the point to change, a practice that fits, and a working change with a review date |
| **Make a decision** | Shift → What-if → And Then What? → Invert → Weigh | "Should we ___ or ___?" | The options side by side, what follows from each, how each fails, how reversible each is, and a recommendation |
| **Improve how I work** | Open(me) → Bottleneck → Best Practice → Build → Review | "Given what I have, what is the best way to ___?" | A practice fitted to the person's real conditions, built into a working capability and tested |
| **Open my system** | Open(me) → X-ray → Leverage → Best Practice | "Open a system for me." | A picture of the person's own situation, the gaps, and two to four fitting practice paths |
| **Learn something deeply** | Open → First Principles → Trace → Make → teach-back | "Teach me ___ properly." | An understanding built from fundamentals, and the ability to explain it |
| **Make content** | Open → X-ray → Make, with bundled video and design | "Turn ___ into a video for ___." | A finished explainer for a named audience, ready to publish |

Notes on each:

- **Understand it.** Flow suits subjects where something moves and accumulates (money, people, material, attention). Loops suits subjects whose behavior persists or repeats. Many subjects need both. This is the route for "open a system for ___" on an outside subject.
- **Explain a surprise.** The surprise is the entry point. The usual finding is that it was not an event but the visible end of a structure. If nobody gains from the outcome, replace Incentives with Loops or Connect.
- **Solve a recurring problem.** The longest route, because it crosses the whole journey. A recurring problem has a structure that keeps producing it; a fix that leaves the structure alone is the "fixes that fail" pattern. It ends with Review because a fix is a claim to be checked.
- **Make a decision.** Shift comes first because the options usually look different from each affected position. Reversibility decides how much care a choice deserves. See `../methods/5-decide/weigh.md`.
- **Improve how I work.** The person's conditions come before the practice. Two people with the same goal should leave with different practices. See `../../engines/build.md`.
- **Open my system.** See `../../system/root.md`. The location choice and the reading scope are part of the opening.
- **Learn something deeply.** The teach-back at the end is the person explaining it in their own words, which is evidence for the second record of understanding.
- **Make content.** The audience is part of the request: the same understanding for a beginner and for an insider becomes two different works. Complete the result around its real communication setting and call sharing capability when multiple platform versions or public artifacts are valuable.

## How a route is chosen

Read the sentence for four things:

1. **What the person needs to end up with.** A picture, a cause, a forecast, a point of leverage, a decision, a made thing, a verdict on what happened. This fixes the stage where the route ends.
2. **What that depends on.** The earlier stages the answer rests on (see `journey.md`). This fixes where the route starts. If the person's system already holds that understanding, start later.
3. **The kind of subject.** A subject where things flow, one that repeats, one shaped by people's interests, one shaped by physical limits, one that is the person's own. This selects among methods at the same stage.
4. **The person.** Their purpose, their level, what they already understand, and conditions they stated earlier. These decide depth, form and where the explanation starts.

Then choose the route that fits, adapt it, and say which one you took in one line. Signals in the wording help but do not decide: "why" suggests Explain, "what if" suggests Anticipate, "should" suggests Decide, "keeps" or "always" suggests a loop, "best way" suggests Best Practice, "make", "turn into" and "build" suggest Create. The same words can sit on a different need.

### When the sentence names a method

"Break the cost of a home battery down to first principles" is a request for one method. Run it in full, with the minimum of See that it depends on, and deliver it as a finished result with its sources. Then offer the routes it naturally continues into as next choices.

### When no route fits

Compose one. Ask which stages the answer depends on and choose one or two methods at each. Say what you composed. If the composition works well and the kind of request is likely to return, offer once to save it as a personal route.

### When two routes fit

If they lead to really different results, this is a choice for the person, with your recommendation, and you proceed on the recommendation. If they differ only in emphasis, pick one.

### When the subject is absurd, tiny or playful

"Open a system for my cat's schedule" is a real request. Run it with the same honesty and the same signature, at a scale that fits. A small subject often shows a structure more clearly than a large one.

## Adapting a route

- **Drop a method that would add nothing.** Not every subject has a bottleneck worth a section, and not every surprise has a beneficiary.
- **Add a method or a lens the subject calls for.** A subject that behaves differently at another size calls for Scale. A stuck situation in which two needs conflict calls for Contradiction. A question about who was left out calls for Boundary.
- **Reorder when the subject requires it.** Order within a route follows dependence, not the table.
- **Go around again.** Routes are drawn as lines, but understanding moves back and forth: a failed prediction in What-if sends you back to X-ray.
- **Every method enters because the subject needs it.** Routes contribute practitioner experience and dependency relations; the runtime adds, removes, replaces, and recomposes according to the live situation.

## What every route shares

Whatever the route, the user sees real discoveries and finished work and can choose from directions worth continuing. Identity elements, system updates, sharing artifacts, and route presentation appear according to the subject and this run's real needs. When a route strip appears, it shows the route actually taken, including adaptations made in context.

## Routes and the levels of care

Some routes end in actions with effects beyond the conversation: building capability, publishing, spending, deleting, contacting people, or scheduling code. These actions can be prepared completely and enter reality through real ownership and authority.
