## Source principles (faithful summary)

The opening says long papers waste readers' time and energy searching for the essential points. The four numbered recommendations are:

1. Set out main points in short, crisp paragraphs.
2. Put detailed analysis of complicated factors or statistics in an appendix.
3. Sometimes submit an aide-mémoire of headings only, expandable orally, rather than a full report.
4. Remove woolly official phrases: omit padding or replace it with a single word; allow short expressive, even conversational phrases.

The closing accepts that such reports may seem rough beside official jargon, but argues that they save time and that stating the real points concisely aids clearer thinking.
