# X-ray

> `xray(x)` · Stage 1 · See · **starter method**
> **Prompt**: "Show me what really drives ___." · "Why does ___ keep happening?"
> **Also heard as**: "What is actually going on with ___?" · "What is behind ___?"
> **Reveals**: the structure beneath recurring events.

## What it is for

People notice events: a product launch, a missed deadline, a price rise, an argument that happens again. Events are the surface. X-ray looks beneath them for what produces them: first the pattern the events belong to, then the structure that generates the pattern, then the beliefs that keep the structure in place. It is the method for anything that "keeps happening", and for anything that looks like a series of separate decisions and is actually one mechanism.

## What it reveals

Four levels, from the visible to the generating:

- **Events**: what happened.
- **Patterns**: what keeps happening, over time.
- **Structure**: the rules, flows, incentives, physical arrangements and information paths that make the pattern likely.
- **Beliefs**: the assumptions that make people build and keep that structure.

The lower the level reached with evidence, the more the understanding explains, and the more a change there would change.

## What a good use looks like

- It starts from something specific that recurs, with dates or counts, not from a general impression.
- Each "why?" is answered from evidence, not from what sounds plausible. An answer that cannot be checked is marked as a guess.
- It follows more than one branch where there is more than one cause. Real problems rarely have a single chain.
- It goes past people to structure. "Because the team was careless" is where the question starts, not where it ends: what made carelessness the likely result?
- It stops where something could actually be changed, and says so.
- The result is one sentence a person could repeat: "this keeps happening because …".

## Where it goes wrong

- **Stopping at the first plausible cause.** The first answer is usually a symptom one level up.
- **Stopping at blame.** A name is not a structure.
- **Counting to five.** Five is a reminder to go deeper, not a quota. Three real levels beat five invented ones.
- **A single chain** where the causes branch and combine.
- **Depth without evidence**: an elegant story about hidden forces that nobody checked.

## It works with

X-ray usually follows **Open** and leads to **Loops** (the structure is often a loop), **Bottleneck** and **Incentives**. It begins *Explain a surprise* and *Solve a recurring problem*, and it is the second step of the Toyota way. When the structure it finds is a known pattern, name it from `patterns/`.

## Teach-back

"This was X-ray: when something keeps happening, do not stop at the event. Ask what pattern it belongs to, what arrangement produces that pattern, and what belief keeps the arrangement in place. Check each answer against evidence."

## The thinking behind it

- **Taiichi Ohno**, *Toyota Production System* (1978; English 1988): asking "why?" five times about a problem to reach its root cause instead of treating its symptom.
- **The iceberg model** used in systems thinking teaching: events, patterns, structures and mental models as levels of explanation.
- **Peter Senge**, *The Fifth Discipline* (1990): structure influences behavior; people placed in the same structure tend to produce the same results.

OpenXSys simplifies: Ohno's practice belongs to a production system with direct observation at the workplace; here the evidence is whatever sources the subject offers, and the chain is allowed to branch.
