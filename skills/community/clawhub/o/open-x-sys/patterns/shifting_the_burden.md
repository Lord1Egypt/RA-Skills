# Shifting the burden

> A quick symptomatic solution is used in place of a slower fundamental one. Each use weakens the ability or the will to apply the fundamental solution, and dependence on the quick one grows.

## The structure

A problem can be handled in two ways: a symptomatic solution that works fast, and a fundamental solution that works on the cause and takes longer. Using the symptomatic one relieves the pressure that would have driven the fundamental one. It also has a side effect that erodes the capacity for the fundamental solution. Over time the system can only cope by means of the quick fix.

## How to recognize it

- Two possible responses, one fast and one slow, and **steadily increasing use of the fast one**.
- The underlying capability is **getting weaker**: skills not built, a process not fixed, a relationship not repaired.
- "We will do it properly once things calm down", said repeatedly.
- A helper who was brought in temporarily has become indispensable. This variant is called shifting the burden to the intervenor: the outside help solves the problem so well that the system never learns to.
- Typical forms: relying on a few heroes in place of fixing the process; consultants in place of building internal skill; a manager solving the team's problems for them; a tool that covers a lack of understanding.

## Where it usually breaks

- **The quick fix stops being available** (the hero leaves, the budget ends) and nothing stands behind it.
- **The problem outgrows the fix.**
- **The atrophy goes unseen**, because the symptoms are under control.

## Where to push

- **Strengthen the fundamental solution first**, then reduce the symptomatic one gradually. Removing the quick fix abruptly, before the real capability exists, causes a crisis.
- **When using the quick fix, pair it with a step on the fundamental one**, every time.
- **For outside help: help in a way that builds the system's own ability**, with an end date.
- **Make the erosion visible**: track the underlying capability, not only the symptom.

## What to check before naming it

Name both solutions. Show the growing reliance on the quick one and the weakening of the other. If the capacity for the fundamental solution is not eroding, it is a simpler case of [fixes that fail](fixes_that_fail.md) or just a reasonable temporary measure.

## Source

- **Peter Senge**, *The Fifth Discipline* (1990): the "shifting the burden" archetype and its variant, shifting the burden to the intervenor.

This pattern is directly relevant to how AI is used. An AI that only hands over answers can shift the burden away from the person's own understanding. It is the reason OpenXSys includes teach-backs, the route strip and learn mode.
