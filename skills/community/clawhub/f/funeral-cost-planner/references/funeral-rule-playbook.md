## The FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453) — what to say, exactly

- "Please send/e-mail me your **General Price List**." — Required to be provided; by phone
  they must read prices (16 CFR 453.2(b)).
- "I want to see your **casket price list** and your **outer-burial-container price list**."
  (453.2(g), 453.3)
- "**I'm declining embalming**; please use refrigeration." — No funeral home may claim
  embalming is required by law; only a few states require it for specific cases (public
  viewing in some, transcontinental transport). (453.4(f))
- "**I will provide my own casket/urn** [Costco/Amazon]." — They cannot refuse, cannot
  charge a handling fee, cannot make you be present for delivery. (453.4(b)(2))
- "Charge me only for the items I selected — **no packages**." (453.4(d))
- "Show **cash advances itemized at cost** with no markup." (453.4(e))
- Complaints: FTC ReportFraud.ftc.gov + state funeral board (licensing).

## Cemetery costs (the hidden half of burial)

Priced separately from the funeral home, rarely disclosed until the plot is chosen:
- Plot/grave: $1,500 (rural) - $4,000+ (urban) - $10k+ (historic cemeteries)
- Opening & closing: $1,200-2,500 (weekend +$500)
- Vault/liner: required by MOST cemeteries (their rule, not law): $1,000-3,500
- Headstone/marker: $1,000-3,500 + setting fee $200-500; military marker free via VA
- Perpetual care: often bundled or 5-10% of plot
- Green/natural burial cemeteries: vault-free, shroud or plain-wood casket, often
  $2,000-4,000 all-in; find via greenburialcouncil.org

Alternatives worth pricing when cost is the binding constraint:
- Direct cremation + memorial service at church/home/park ($0-500 venue) = $2-3k total
- Body donation to a medical school: typically no cost (they cremate and return remains
  after 1-2 years); transport sometimes charged
- Burial on private land: legal in most rural counties with a permit; check local health
  department

## Prepaid funerals / funeral insurance (review mode)

Traps:
1. **Non-portable trust funds** — money held by THE funeral home; move = penalty or forfeit
   of growth. Portability is the #1 question.
2. **Item-lock inflation** — price "guaranteed" only for items listed; cash advances
   (certificates, obituary) NOT covered and billed at then-current price.
3. **Insurance vs trust**: preneed insurance (unit-linked) is more portable; trust locks
   to the home. If the family might relocate or the home might be sold (they do get
   acquired), insurance wins.
4. **Medicaid spend-down**: prepaid funeral is an exempt asset in most states — that's the
   legitimate use case; do it with the funeral home's insurance vehicle, itemized.
5. Cancellation: insurance has free-look (~30 days); trust refunds may deduct admin fees.

Review checklist for an existing contract: who holds funds, item list vs current GPL,
portability clause, inflation guarantee scope, cash-advance coverage, cancellation terms.

## Phone script (3 homes, 15 minutes)

"Hi, I'm arranging a cremation and collecting prices per the FTC Funeral Rule.
1. What's your charge for **direct cremation, including the non-declinable basic services
   fee**? 
2. Does that include an alternative container, or is that extra?
3. If we wanted a **viewing first**, what would that add — staff, prep, rental casket?
4. What are your **least expensive** casket options?
5. Are death certificates passed through at cost, and what's the state fee?
6. Can you e-mail me the GPL?
Thank you — I'll call back today either way."

Write answers on the comparison sheet; the deltas between homes for IDENTICAL line items
are your negotiation leverage.

## Negotiation dynamics

- Funeral homes compete; the GPL made prices transparent for a reason. "Home B quoted
  $1,495 all-in for direct cremation" is a sentence they answer.
- End-of-month/quarter pacing exists in the industry like anywhere else.
- NEVER negotiate the person's dignity — negotiate line items: container, printing,
  flowers, obituary placement, staff hours for viewing.
- If pressured: "I need the itemized GPL to decide; the Funeral Rule requires it and I'll
  come back this afternoon." Nobody reasonable loses a sale over that sentence.

## Outside-US notes

- UK: Funeral Rule equivalent does not exist; use Funeral Consumer Association / CMA
  2021 market investigation findings as leverage; direct cremation ~£1,500-2,000.
- Canada: provincial regulation varies; basic direct cremation CAD 1,200-2,500 urban;
  Consumer Protection Acts cover misrepresentation.
