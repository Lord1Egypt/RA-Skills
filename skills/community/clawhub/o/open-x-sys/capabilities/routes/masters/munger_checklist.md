# Munger's checklist

> **Master route.** Invert → follow the incentives → use many models → think a level deeper → decide.
> **Run it with**: "Run Munger's checklist on ___." · "Look at this the way Munger would."
> **Fits**: a judgment under uncertainty where being wrong is costly: an investment, a hire, a partnership, a strategy, a large purchase, a plan one is already fond of.
> **Sources**: Charlie Munger, speech at the Harvard School (1986) and "The Psychology of Human Misjudgment" (1995); Howard Marks, *The Most Important Thing* (2011).

## The five steps, in their own order

| # | Step | What it asks | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Invert** | What would guarantee that this fails? | Seeing only the path to success |
| 2 | **Follow the incentives** | Who is rewarded for what here, including the people advising me? | Taking stated motives at face value |
| 3 | **Use many models** | What do several different disciplines each say about this? | Seeing every problem through one favourite idea |
| 4 | **Think a level deeper** | After the obvious consequence, what happens next, and what does everyone already expect? | Stopping where everyone else stops |
| 5 | **Decide** | Given all this, what do I do, and how reversible is it? | Analysis with no conclusion |

## As OpenXSys methods

| Step | Method | How it is used here |
|---|---|---|
| Invert | **Invert** | A premortem specific to this situation, ranked by likelihood and damage |
| Follow the incentives | **Incentives** | Each party's rewards, costs and time horizon, including sellers, advisers and the person themselves |
| Use many models | **Connect** | Look at the same situation through several fields (for example economics, psychology, statistics, engineering, biology) and note where they agree and where they conflict |
| Think a level deeper | **And Then What?** | Second and third rounds of consequences, and how the person's view differs from what is generally expected |
| Decide | **Weigh** | Options side by side, with reversibility, a recommendation and its conditions |

## What a good run looks like

- It begins by inverting, before the case in favour has been admired any further.
- The incentives step includes the incentives of whoever is recommending the action, and the person's own: what do they want to be true?
- The models are really different and are actually applied. Naming five disciplines is not using them. Where independent models point the same way, confidence rises; where they conflict, that conflict is examined.
- It checks for the common errors of judgment that matter here: over-weighting recent or vivid evidence, following the crowd, commitment to a past position, liking the person who proposes it.
- It stays within what the person can actually know. Recognizing that something lies outside one's competence, and passing on it, is a legitimate result.
- It ends in a decision or a clear "not yet, until …", with reversibility stated.
- For financial subjects, it makes the decision logic, evidence, alternatives, exposures, and conditions for changing course explicit; the person retains the decision.

## Where OpenXSys simplifies

- **This five-item list is OpenXSys's arrangement.** Munger argued for inversion, for attention to incentives, for a range of models from many disciplines and for the use of checklists in judgment, in several talks over many years. He did not publish this list in this form.
- "Think a level deeper" comes from **Howard Marks's** second-level thinking, not from Munger. It is included because the two approaches are close and are often used together.
- Munger's talk on misjudgment lists many psychological tendencies; this route uses a handful.
- No words are quoted as Munger's here. Sayings widely attributed to him are repeated only when traced to a source.

## Next choices that usually fit

**Shift**, to see the decision from the other party's side. **Watch**, on the signal that would show the thesis is failing. **Review**, later, to compare the decision's reasoning with what happened.
