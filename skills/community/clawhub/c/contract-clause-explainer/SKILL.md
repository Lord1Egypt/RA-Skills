# Contract Clause Explainer

## Overview

Contract language hides its meaning in structure. This workflow translates clause by clause: the obligation, the permission, the trigger, and the exit — and flags the clauses whose plain meaning surprises most signers. It explains; it does not give legal advice.

## When to use

When signing a freelance agreement, SaaS terms, partnership docs, or any contract you did not draft.

## Workflow

1. **Isolate the clause** — quote it exactly; never explain a paraphrase
2. **Translate the mechanics** — who must do what, by when, triggered by what, in plain sentences
3. **Find the permissions** — what the clause lets the other party do (change terms, terminate, use your work/data) and on what notice
4. **Find the asymmetries** — obligations that run one way, caps that protect one side, auto-renewals and notice windows
5. **List the negotiation candidates** — the two or three changes a reasonable counterparty would accept, in order of importance
6. **Summarize the risk in one line** — what is the worst realistic outcome this clause enables?

## Guardrails

- This is explanation, not legal advice — anything high-stakes goes to a real lawyer, and the workflow must say so
- Never summarize away a number, date, or notice period — quote them exactly
- If a term is defined elsewhere in the document, use the document's definition, not the dictionary's

## Output format

Per clause: exact quote, plain-language mechanics, permissions, asymmetries, negotiation candidates, one-line risk.
