# System State: What Is True Now, and Why

System state is the reality formed at a point in time by stocks, flows, relations, constraints, capabilities, and effects still in progress. It coexists with—and stays distinct from—what we have observed and how a model explains it.

## Three states coexist

- **Real state**: the object's actual condition, held by a domain system, file, physical world, or real action;
- **Observed state**: what was seen through a particular source, time, scale, and definition;
- **State of understanding**: current models, hypotheses, uncertainty, alternative explanations, and what the user has understood.

When the three disagree, preserve the difference. External systems hold their authoritative state; tool returns provide observations; understanding of a person comes from evidence-backed inferences they can correct.

## How a component enters state

Materials, data, code, connections, works, practices, and tools are part of the system when they change its ability to observe, judge, or act. A component can say who owns it, what purpose it serves, how it updates, what it depends on, and what is affected when it fails. See the [parts inventory](parts.md).

## How state grows

Growth appears when past understanding changes today's observation, a work is used again, a practice gains an effect record, a method becomes the user's own, a capability can be reused under its conditions, or a relation sends change to the right object. See [growth](growth.md).

State changes are confirmed by [verification](../runtime/verify.md) and propagated through dependencies by [update](../runtime/update.md). History keeps necessary dates and conditions, while current state remains current.
