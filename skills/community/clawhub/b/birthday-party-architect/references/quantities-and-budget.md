# Quantities & Budget Anatomy

## Cake servings chart (party slices, ~5 × 7 cm)
| Item | Servings |
|---|---|
| 6″ round | 8 |
| 8″ round, single layer | 12 |
| 8″ round, double layer | 20 |
| Quarter sheet (9×13) | 24 |
| Half sheet (11×15) | 36 |
| 10″ + 8″ tier | 48 |
| Full sheet (18×24) | 72 |

Rule: order the chart entry ≥ kids + adults, one step up if you want next-day family cake (you do). Ice cream: 1 scoop × 60% of kids (some refuse) unless sundae bar is the activity — then budget 2 scoops + toppings per child.

## Food math
- **Pizza:** 3 slices per child 5+, 2 per child under 5, 1 pizza per 3 adults. Large pizza = 8 slices. Round up, not down — cold pizza is tomorrow's win, missing pizza is a party failure.
- **Toddler table (under-5):** fruit, crackers, cheese cubes, veggie straws. Nothing hard-round (whole grapes — halve them; popcorn — no).
- **Adults at a preschool party** are the majority guest count and the supervision plan: one substantial platter + coffee.
- **Drinks:** 2 servings/child (juice box + water), 2/adult. Water dispenser beats bottles for cost.
- **Tween/teen food:** multiply pizza by 1.5; add a "second wave" item (nachos, sliders). Running out of food at a teen party is remembered forever.

## Favor doctrine
- Budget **$3–5/child, one quality item**: mini plant, paperback book, craft kit, sidewalk chalk set, s'mores kit. A bag of six plastic trinkets costs the same, dies in a day, and becomes landfill before bedtime.
- Exclude: slime (carpet war), whistles (parents will curse you), anything battery+noise combined, gum for under-5.
- Label with names — doubles as the seating/place marker for tweens.

## Budget shape
### Home party (budget splits used by the script)
- Food 40% · Cake 18% · Decor 12% · Activity supplies 10% · Favors (fixed ~$4/child) · Buffer 5%
- The invisible line item is **your labor**: a 12-kid home party is 6–8 hours of a parent's weekend (prep + run + reset). That's fine when it's your craft; if you'd pay $25/hr to avoid it, the venue math changes.

### Venue package anatomy (read the contract for these)
- **Per-child price × realistic headcount** (use expected, not invited).
- **Time cap is usually 90 min** — overtime $50–150 per 30 min; cake/exit ceremonies routinely push you over.
- **Cake-cutting fee** $1–2/slice at some venues, *or* mandatory house cake.
- **Outside-food ban** (pizza delivery surcharge or prohibition).
- **Party host tip** $20–40 standard at activity venues.
- **Socks/waivers** at trampoline parks (paid socks!), waiver forms signed by every parent BEFORE the party.
- Deposit & cancellation window; headcount final-change deadline (usually 72h).

### Home vs venue — the honest compare
- **Home wins** under ~10 kids, ages 1–5 (toddlers need caregivers anyway, venues add nothing), or when you have a yard + a signature activity (water play, petting zoo).
- **Venue wins** 10+ kids at 6–12 when the activity is the point (you cannot build a trampoline park), small apartments, winter birthdays with high-energy kids.
- Break-even rule of thumb: venue ≈ $25–45/child × expected count + $75 hidden fees; home ≈ $150–250 food/cake/decor + 7 hrs labor. At 8 kids they're close; at 15 the venue is often *cheaper* per unit of parental sanity.

## Week-of checklist
- RSVP deadline 5 days out; confirm final headcount to venue 72h ahead.
- Order cake 1–2 weeks out (bakeries cap weekend orders Thursday).
- Charge camera, assign the ONE photographer, buy candles + lighter (the lighter is always missing).
- Prep a "party box" the night before: tape, scissors, markers, trash bags, paper towels, first-aid kit, phone charger.
