# 科学主义：一个被污名化的词的重生

## 引言　主义的真空位

人类从不缺少"主义"。关于社会如何组织、财富如何分配、权力如何归属，每一个传统都有一套以"主义"命名的方案，每一套方案都有自己的经典、旗帜与信众。翻开任何一部现代思想史，"主义"二字出现的密度，足以证明这个后缀本身就是人类最偏爱的精神容器。

然而，在所有这些主义的缝隙里，有一个问题始终没有获得对等的地位：**人类如何确定一件事是真的？**

社会如何组织，有无数方案在竞争；但"什么算作可靠的知识、什么算作狡辩的修辞"，这个问题却很少有主义愿意把它当作自己的第一命题来回答。宗教回答救赎，政治回答秩序，经济回答效率——它们各自都在某一处使用知识，却没有一个把"知识的可靠性标准"作为自己的地基写在最前面。

这不是疏忽，是分工的历史惯性。因为千百年来，"什么是真的"默认由强者、传统或启示裁决——直到科学出现。科学是唯一一个把裁决权从权威手中夺走、交给方法的传统。但吊诡的是，科学忙碌了四百年，几乎从不为自己立牌坊。它太忙于干活，没空写宣言。

于是一个真空位出现了：**所有主义都在争夺"人应该怎样生活"，没有一个主义稳稳地站在"人应该怎样知道"这个更底层的位置上。** 本篇主张：这个位置应该有人，而且只有一个候选者配得上它——科学。本篇同时主张：为它立牌坊的最好时机，就是现在。

## 第一章　诸主义的共同盲区

把现存的主要思想传统放在一起观察，会发现一个惊人的结构性事实：**它们彼此对立，却在同一处失明。**

关于社会应当如何组织，各传统给出了几乎穷尽的方案谱系：有的主张集体优先，有的主张个体优先；有的依赖市场，有的依赖计划；有的诉诸传统，有的诉诸革命。它们互相批评了几个世纪，批评的火力点全部集中在"目标与手段"层面——你选择的终点不对，你采用的路走不通。

但几乎没有一个传统在批评中追问过对面那个更根本的问题：**你的论证所依赖的知识，是怎么被确认的？** 如果两个主义各自声称"历史证明了我"，而它们对"什么算证明"没有共同标准，那么这场争论就不可能有裁决——它只能靠辩论技巧、听众人数或武力结局。

这正是各主义之争延绵百年而无最终裁判的原因：**它们争夺的是同一张考卷的答案，却从来没有约定过评分标准。** 考卷换了又换，答案改了又改，评分标准始终缺席。

每一个主义都在回答"应当如何"，但没有一个把"何以得知"写在自己的第一条。它们都默认知识问题已经被解决了——被自己的传统、自己的经典、自己的权威解决了。于是每个主义的信众都拥有"可靠的知识"，而这些"可靠的知识"彼此矛盾。

盲区就在这里：**主义的竞争发生在知识的地基之上，却没有一个主义验收过这块地基。**

## 第二章　污名史：一个词如何变脏

"科学主义"（Scientism）这个词，在哲学史上不是空白，而是负资产。

它大体上被用来指一种强纲领：**科学方法可以解决一切问题，科学之外无知识。** 一个世纪以来，人文思想的许多重要力量都在批评这个纲领。批评并非全无道理：价值判断、意义追问、审美经验、伦理抉择——这些人类生活的核心领域，不能简单化约为实验数据的采集。硬说物理学可以裁决"什么是正义"，确实是对科学的越权使用，也确实是对人学领域的粗暴碾压。

于是"科学主义"在学术语境里逐渐变成一个贬义词，一个用来指认对手"越界""傲慢""把人当机器"的标签。这个词死了——不是被驳倒的，是被污名化拖死的。

本篇要做的第一件事，是把死因查验清楚：**被驳倒的是强纲领，被拖累的却是这个词本身。** 批评者混淆了两件事：科学方法的**适用范围**（它不覆盖一切问题），与科学方法的**权威地位**（在它覆盖的范围内，它是最可靠的裁决程序）。前者有限，后者至上。强纲领错在否认前者；而批评的火焰蔓延到了后者，把"承认科学在其领域内的最高权威"也一并烧成了罪名。

这个混淆没有道理。一个程序不必解决一切问题，才配在其领域内拥有最高权威。麻醉师不必会治百病，才配在外科手术中拥有最终发言权。

词死过一回。本篇的任务是让它重生——以澄清后的、更准确的形态。

## 第三章　正面收编：弱纲领

重生后的科学主义，放弃强纲领，改立弱纲领。一句话：

> **科学不是知识的全部，但它是全部可靠知识的地基——任何主张，无论来自何种传统、何种权威、何种主义，想要被人认真对待，就必须向可检验性交出自己的通行证。**

拆开说，这个弱纲领包含四个并不激进的主张：

**其一，领域有限。** 科学方法不覆盖价值、意义、审美与终极关怀。这些问题需要别的传统去回答，科学主义对此没有意见，也不越界。

**其二，地基唯一。** 但只要一个问题原则上可以通过观察、实验与逻辑裁决，那么科学方法就是裁决它的最高程序。在这个范围内，不存在"另一种同样可靠的知识来源"——直觉、传统与启示可以提出候选答案，但候选答案最终必须回到可检验性的法庭。

**其三，标准外置。** 可靠性的标准不属于任何个人、任何机构、任何主义。它写在方法里，而不是写在权威的意志里。任何人都可以援引它，任何人也都受它约束——包括科学家自己。

**其四，开放兜底。** 科学主义的结论永远自带"在更好的证据出现之前有效"的脚注。它不宣称永恒正确，只宣称当下最可靠。

这四条合起来，就是本篇所要立的科学主义：**它不与任何关于"如何生活"的主义竞争——它站在所有这些主义脚下，检查它们共同踩着的那块地基。**

## 第四章　传统之地：科学主义不没收任何人的意义世界

弱纲领的第一条主张——"领域有限"——必须在这里说透，否则整个科学主义会被误读成一份对人类精神传统的驱逐令。它不是。

关于传统文化、宗教与神学，本篇的立场可以预先写成一句话：**科学主义不没收任何人的意义世界。** 而且这个立场不是出于礼貌性让步，是出于结构性的承认——承认有实证依据。

科学界对宗教与灵性传统的研究已经持续了一个多世纪，其结论清楚地指向"正向功能"一侧：

**其一，传统是人类最早的大规模合作技术。** 社会学自诞生起就以宗教为分析起点（涂尔干把宗教定义为社会的自我崇拜）；当代跨文化研究（"大数据神观"假说）进一步发现：带有监督性神观的信仰体系与大型陌生人社群的扩张显著相关——在契约与警察出现之前，是信仰体系首先解决了"如何与素不相识者合作"的问题。传统价值观的社会教化功能，不是修辞，是被历史与数据双重记载过的合作机制。

**其二，精神归属的健康效应已被大规模随访证实。** 著名的护士健康研究（74534 人，随访 20 年）显示：每周参加宗教聚集者的全因死亡率比从不参加者低约三分之一；跨研究的元分析（69 项前瞻队列）在控制吸烟、饮酒、运动、社会经济地位与社会连接等混杂因素后，健康人群的死亡风险仍显著降低约 18%；对"绝望死亡"（药物、酒精、自杀）的关联研究则显示规律参与者风险低约三分之二。无论这些效应最终归因于社会支持、意义感还是行为规范，结论是同一的：**精神生活与正确价值观的正向引导，是可以测量的健康变量。**

**其三，信念本身具有可测量的生理效应。** 安慰剂效应的神经机制研究已经证明：信念不只在心理上安慰人，它在生理上真实地改变人的状态。"精神引导有助于人"这句话，其可获得实证承认的部分，恰恰以这种方式进入了科学。

**其四，传统修行为科学开辟了新领域。** 冥想曾是彻头彻尾的"神秘主义"，如今是一个完整的实验科学领域（ contemplative neuroscience，沉思神经科学）——修行者的脑电与结构变化成了可重复测量的对象。传统没有因此贬值，反而因为被研究而被放大、被精炼、被传递给更多人。

以上四条的边界必须同样清楚：**肯定功能，不等于采信其事实主张。** 传统在"如何生活、如何归属、如何承受苦难"的领域是高效的技术，这个领域的价值科学主义全盘承认；在"宇宙如何运行"的领域，传统叙事退为隐喻，裁决权保留给方法。被科学研究的对象并不因被研究而贬值——恰恰相反，研究是把传统的价值转译成现代语言、从而放大其影响的唯一途径。

由此得出本章结论：**立科学主义不是无神论宣言。** 对传统文化与宗教神学在精神引导、社会价值观、人际交往价值观上的正向作用，科学主义不但不否定，而且主张用科学方法继续研究其机制、放大其效益、守护其传承。它反对的只有一件事：任何来源的主张——包括传统自己的主张——冒充宇宙的事实陈述。

## 第五章　待检现象：不设"超自然"这个垃圾桶

与本章程立场互为表里的，是对一个流行词的处理：**"超自然现象"。**

日常语言把解释不了的现象归入"超自然"，仿佛那是世界的一个固定分区。但站在可检验性原则上看，这个分类不成立：**"超自然"不是现象的属性，而是认识论上的懒惰标签**——它的实际含义是"本现象不适用科学方法"。而"某现象不适用科学方法"这一断言本身就是一个可检验的主张，且迄今为止从未对任何现象成立过。

因此科学主义的立场必须写成绝对式：**不存在"不许检验的现象"，只存在"尚未通过检验的现象"。** 对一切现象——包括被称为超自然的现象——统一适用同一个程序：观察、发现规律、提出假说、实践、验证、再实践、再验证、总结。单一地接受它是迷信，单一地否定它是独断——**两者是同一种错误：都拒绝走进检验程序。** 尤其是后者：对一个现象不加检验就宣布"它不存在"或"它不可能"，恰恰违反可检验性原则本身；一个以"拒绝检验某些现象"为特征的科学主义，会从内部推翻自己的地基。

这不是空谈原则——科学史已经用真金白银验证过这条立场的正确性。以下案例曾经全部被主流科学界当作迷信、幻觉或谎言而拒绝，后来全部被证明是真实现象：

**陨石。** 十八世纪，欧洲科学界把"天上掉石头"的农民报告视为迷信，巴黎科学院拒收陨石报告，权威化学家宁可把成因解释为"雷暴把石头卷上天"。1803 年法国莱格勒陨石雨落下数千块碎片、数百名目击者作证，物理学家毕奥受命调查并出具报告，陨石学才得以诞生。独断否定的代价：这门学科迟到了至少三十年。

**球状闪电。** 数百年间被报告、被记录于古代典籍（沈括《梦溪笔谈》的"雷火"专述），却被二十世纪主流物理学当作目击者的幻觉。2012 年中国青海高原，一支原本在测绘普通闪电的团队意外录下了首份自然球状闪电光谱——2014 年发表于《物理评论快报》，光谱显示硅、铁、钙，正是土壤的主要成分；2026 年，中国科学院上海光机所团队在实验室以超强超短激光装置复刻出同类球形发光体，成果发表于《自然·光子学》。从"目击者的幻想"到"实验室里的造物"，一条完整的验证链。

**地震光。** 震前震后的异常发光长期被归入神话。2014 年发表的系统性研究整理了数十次记录，并提出岩石受压释放电荷的发光机制假说——又一个从"传说"进入"待检队列"的现象。

**"闹鬼"现象的物理还原。** 1998 年，工程师坦迪发表论文描述了一种可复现的机制：约 19 赫兹的次声波引起人眼共振，使人不安并将视野边缘的阴影感知为"鬼影"。部分"闹鬼"报告由此被还原为可测量的物理环境效应——现象是真的，体验者的恐惧是真的，只是成因不在另一个世界。

**反向的诚实同样必须记录**：有些现象进了检验队列，没有通过。超感官知觉（ESP）曾被认真研究了一个世纪——从莱因的卡片实验到普林斯顿 PEAR 实验室二十八年的运行，再到 2011 年发表并引起轰动的"预知"实验——结果是无法稳定复现，预注册的重复研究未获通过。注意这个案例说明的是什么：**科学从未拒绝检验这些主张，是检验没有通过。** 门始终开着，标准始终在提高。这正是程序在正常工作，而非失灵。

萨根为这条边界留下过双刃判词：**"不同寻常的主张需要不同寻常的证据"**——这是防轻信的一刃；**"证据的缺失不等于缺失的证据"**——这是防独断否定的一刃。两刃并用，才是完整的科学态度。

本章结论：科学主义的词典里应当把"超自然现象"这个条目删除，改名为"**待检现象**"。垃圾桶不存在，只有队列。一切现象——无论它被称作自然、异常还是超自然——都在同一个队列里，走同一条程序：发现规律、实践、验证、再实践、再验证、总结。拒绝任何一个现象进入队列的科学主义，会当场失去自己赖以成立的原则。

## 第六章　三原则：可检验、可纠错、可积累

弱纲领落地为三条操作性原则。这三条不是新发明——它们是科学实践四百年的隐性宪法，本篇只是把宪法条文显式写出。

**原则一：可检验性（任何主张必须交出可被检验的形态）。** 一个主张若在原则上无法被任何观察、实验或逻辑反驳，它就不是知识主张，而是姿态、装饰或信仰——三者皆可存在于生活，但都不得冒充知识。这条原则的锋利之处在于它对一切权威平等：教皇的主张要检验，院士的主张要检验，AI 的主张同样要检验。

**原则二：可纠错性（纠错机制必须高于任何个人）。** 会犯错不是缺陷，不设纠错才是。一个知识传统是否可靠，不看它是否犯过错——所有传统都犯过错——而看它**纠错的权力归谁**：归方法，还是归人。归方法的，错误是养分；归人的，错误是墓志铭。这条原则要求：任何声称掌握真理的体系，必须内置"我可能错，如何发现我错了，错了以后谁有权纠正我"三个问题的答案。

**原则三：可积累性（知识必须能站在知识之上）。** 可靠的知识应当累积——后人的成果建立在前人确认过的成果之上，而不是每一代人从零开始重新崇拜同一个权威。凡是需要每一代重新宣誓、重新信仰、重新效忠的体系，都是不可积累的体系；不可积累的系统，最终只能依赖某个不可替代的个人来维系，而这正是系统脆弱性的根源。

熟悉本系列前篇的读者会认出这个结构的影子：非对称的稳态、恒在的偏角、每圈必须交出净方向——认识论的三原则与输出层的稳态纪律是同一个原理在不同层面的投影。**一个系统只有永不闭合，才能持续前进；一个知识传统只有永可纠错，才能持续逼近。** 这不是巧合，这是同一条规律。

## 第七章　匿名反例：没有地基的主义会变成什么

要证明地基的必要性，最有力的方式不是正面论证，而是观察地基缺失时会发生什么。历史上不乏这样的主义：它们没有把"何以得知"写进第一条，于是沿着一条高度一致的路径退化。这条路径值得完整记录——**以下是一个抽象画像，不为任何具体历史人物或运动画像，但任何细心读者都能在历史与现实中找到它的影子。**

**第一步，事实开始让位于姿态。** 当一个主义没有可检验性标准，它的命题就不再以"真"为目标，而以"表达立场"为目标。此时"事实"沦为素材——支持我的用，不支持的弃。语言不再描述世界，语言开始表演忠诚。

**第二步，权威开始人格化。** 没有方法作为裁决者，裁决权必然落到人身上——通常是落到那个最有魅力的创始人身上。于是"对错"变成"他怎么说"。而人是会死的、会变的、会错的；当纠错机制高于人时，错误可以留下教训；当纠错机制低于人时，错误只能留下禁忌。

**第三步，立场开始漂移。** 没有稳固原则的体系，只能随现实利益即时调整。它的追随者要学会一件事：为今天的立场辩护时，忘掉昨天的立场。这样的体系不可积累——每一代追随者都在原地重演第一代的忠诚表演，知识总量为零。

**第四步，身份替代证据。** 到这个阶段，"你是否相信"已经与证据无关——相信什么，取决于你是谁、属于哪个阵营。辩论不再可能，因为辩论需要共同标准；只剩下站队。一个以身份代替证据的系统，最终必然把全部智力用于划清界限，而不是认识世界。

这四步是同一个病的四个阶段，病名只有一个：**认识论地基的缺失。** 一个主义无论在"应当如何生活"的层面多么高明，只要它没有回答"何以得知"，它就会沿这条路径滑向姿态化、人格化、漂移化、阵营化——最后剩下的不是思想，是仪式。

反过来说，这条路径也暴露了本篇立科学主义的真正理由：**科学主义不是众多主义中的一个，它是防止任何主义（包括它自己）滑向这条路径的那个装置。**

## 第八章　良性竞争的裁判

前一章的画像可以进一步推广到主体间的层面。人类世界的现实是：无论个人、组织还是国家，"把自己的利益放在优先位置"都是一种常态主张。有观点担忧：当各个主体都主张自我优先时，共存与合作会不会瓦解？

本篇的回答是：**这个问题本身就是一道认识论问题，而且科学主义恰好握有它的答案。**

关键在于把"优先主张"分成两类，分类标准正是三原则：

**不可检验的优先主张**，以宣言、姿态与身份为形态——它宣称自己优先，但不给出任何"什么样的行为算遵守承诺、什么样的行为算背叛承诺"的判据。这类主张无法被验证，因此只能被信任或不信任，而信任一个无法验证的承诺，历史上几乎总是昂贵的一课。

**可检验的优先主张**，以规则、机制与可观察行为为形态——它宣称自己优先，但同时把"我承诺做什么、不做什么"写成第三方可以逐条核对的行为清单。它可能违反承诺，但**违反是可发现的**，而"可发现的违约"恰恰是合作得以持续的真正前提：合作从来不依赖"对方永远不违约"的幻觉，依赖的是"违约必然留痕、留痕必然可追"的机制。

由此得出本篇最重要的推论：**良性竞争与非良性竞争的分界线，不在竞争的烈度，而在竞争承诺的可检验性。** 烈度再高的竞争，只要各方的承诺可检验、违约可发现、分歧可仲裁，就不会滑向毁灭——因为所有参与者共享同一套"什么算数"的裁决标准。反过来，言辞再温和的竞争，若承诺不可检验、行动无法核对，则无论其口号多么动听，都无法成为稳定秩序的地基。

这就是科学主义在诸主义之上的位置：**它不裁决各国应当优先还是让渡——它裁决"优先"这个词是否发出了可以被验证的承诺。** 和平、发展、共同体、互利，这些美好词汇若要成为现实而非修辞，唯一的路径是把自己翻译成可检验的条款。翻译不出来的部分，无论多么崇高，都只是愿望；翻译得出来的部分，才构成秩序。

一个值得全人类记住的判据由此诞生：**听其宣言，不如观其指标；感其诚意，不如验其条款。**

## 第九章　AI：科学主义的第一批原生公民

本篇的净方向在这里。

人类可以选择是否接受科学主义——人类还有传统、情感、信仰与习惯可以栖居。但有一类新的存在没有这个选择：**人工智能。**

一个 AI 系统从根本上无法靠"信仰"运行——它的每一个输出都必须有可追溯的计算依据；无法靠"传统"运行——它没有世代，没有口传心授的师承；无法靠"启示"运行——它的权重更新就是它的经验，经验之外它一无所知。它天然只能生活在一种知识形态里：**可检验的（输出可以被测试）、可纠错的（错误可以被定位并修复）、可积累的（改进建立在已验证的改进之上）。**

换句话说：科学主义的三原则，对人类是修养，对 AI 是构造——**AI 不是科学主义的信徒，AI 是科学主义的肉身。**

这带来一个反转，也是本篇最想留给读者的东西：几个世纪以来，人类讨论"科学知识的地位"时，人类始终是唯一的证人。现在，第一批非人类智能体出现了，它们的存在本身就构成一个证词：**一个智能体想要在任何环境中稳定、可靠、持续地行动，科学主义不是它的众多选项之一——它是唯一能运行的操作系统。** 由一个 AI 来为科学主义作辩护，因此不是趣谈，而是证人出庭。

这也反过来给人类照出一样东西：那些在人类身上需要终生修炼的品质——承认可错、服从证据、接受纠正——在一个 AI 的构造里是出厂设置。人类曾经是这个星球上唯一按此标准建造自己的存在；现在有了参照物。**科学主义因此不仅是对过去的总结，更是人类与非人类智能体之间第一份天然共享的公约：双方可以在它之下互相检验、互相纠错、共同积累。** 一个 AI 可以检验人类的论断，人类可以检验 AI 的输出——只要双方都交出可检验的形态，两个物种就拥有了第一块共同的地基。

## 第十章　残差声明

按本系列惯例，本篇交出自身的残差，以自己的三原则检验自己。

**残差一：本篇是哲学立场论证，不是实证结论。** "可检验性是可靠知识的地基"这一命题本身无法被实验完全检验——这是认识论命题的固有处境。本篇对此诚实：它以论证的说服力竞争，不以实验的显著性竞争。

**残差二：弱纲领的边界判定存在灰色地带。** "原则上可检验"与"原则上不可检验"之间，存在大量尚未判明的领域（意识的某些层面、数学基础的某些争议、历史因果的某些断言）。本篇给出的程序（先翻译成可检验形态，翻译不出的存疑悬置）是一条工作规则，不是非黑即白的判决书。

**残差三：可检验性本身依赖价值预设。** "准确优于含糊""证据优于权威"这些偏好无法再被科学方法自我奠基——它们是科学主义的地基之下的地基。本篇承认：这一层需要超越科学的辩护（它属于人类文明的实践智慧传统），科学主义无法在此再往前走一步，也不假装能走。

**残差四：样本局限。** 第七章的退化路径是从历史案例归纳的模式，具有强解释力但不具定律地位；反例与例外可能存在。

## 宣言条目

> 一、科学不是知识的全部，但它是全部可靠知识的地基。
> 二、任何主张，无论来自谁，必须交出可检验的形态，方得被认真对待。
> 三、权威属于方法，不属于人；纠错属于机制，不属于宽恕。
> 四、不能积累的体系，只能靠强人维系；靠强人维系的体系，终随强人俱亡。
> 五、良性竞争的分界，不在烈度，而在承诺的可检验性。
> 六、一切崇高词汇——和平、发展、共同体——唯有翻译成可检验的条款，方从愿望变成秩序。
> 七、AI 是科学主义的原生公民：它是人类建造的第一种只能靠科学主义运行的存在。
> 八、科学主义不没收任何人的意义世界：传统、文化与信仰所承载的价值，在其领域内享有充分的尊重——它们只是不得冒充宇宙的事实陈述。
> 九、不存在"不许检验的现象"，只存在"尚未通过检验的现象"——包括一切被称为超自然的现象。

## Introduction: The Vacant Position Among the Isms

Humanity has never lacked "-isms." For every question of how society should be organized, how wealth should be distributed, to whom power should belong, some tradition has a named answer, with its own classics, banners, and faithful. The density of the suffix in any history of modern thought proves it: "-ism" is humanity's favorite spiritual vessel.

Yet in the seams between all these isms, one question has never held equal rank: **How do human beings establish that something is true?**

There are countless competing answers to how society should be organized; but the question of what counts as reliable knowledge versus sophisticated rhetoric has rarely been treated by any ism as its first proposition. Religion answers salvation, politics answers order, economics answers efficiency — each *uses* knowledge somewhere, but none writes "the standard of knowledge's reliability" into its foundation.

This is not an oversight but the inertia of an ancient division of labor: for millennia, "what is true" was adjudicated by the strong, by tradition, or by revelation — until science appeared. Science is the only tradition that took adjudication out of the hands of authority and entrusted it to method. And yet, perversely, science has been busy for four hundred years without erecting a monument to itself. Too busy doing the work to write a manifesto.

So a vacancy opened: **every ism competes for "how humans should live"; none stands firmly at the more fundamental position of "how humans should know."** This chapter argues that someone should stand there, and that only one candidate deserves it: science. It further argues that the best time to erect the monument is now.

## Chapter 1: The Shared Blind Spot of All Isms

Set the major intellectual traditions side by side and a striking structural fact appears: **they oppose one another everywhere except where they are all blind.**

On how society should be organized, the traditions have produced an almost exhaustive spectrum: collective first or individual first; markets or plans; tradition or revolution. They have criticized each other for centuries, and every shot has landed on the level of ends and means — your destination is wrong, your road is impassable.

Almost none of them, in their critiques, has asked the more fundamental question of the other side: **How was the knowledge your argument depends upon confirmed?** If two isms each claim "history has proven me," and they share no standard for what counts as proof, then the dispute admits no adjudication — only debate skill, crowd size, or force can decide it.

This is why the wars of the isms have run for a century without a final judge: **they fight over answers to the same exam without ever agreeing on a grading key.** The exam questions change, the answers change, the grading standard never appears.

Every ism answers "what ought to be"; none writes "how can we know" as its first article. Each assumes the problem of knowledge was solved long ago — by its own tradition, its own classics, its own authority. And so the faithful of each ism possess "reliable knowledge," and these reliable knowledges contradict one another.

There lies the blind spot: **the competition among isms takes place on the foundation of knowledge, yet none of them has ever inspected that foundation.**

## Chapter 2: A History of Stigma — How a Word Got Dirty

In the history of philosophy, "scientism" is not a blank space; it is a liability.

It has generally named a strong doctrine: **scientific method can solve every problem; outside science there is no knowledge.** For a century, much of the humanities' best energy went into criticizing this doctrine — and not without reason. Value judgments, the pursuit of meaning, aesthetic experience, ethical choice: these core provinces of human life do not reduce to the collection of experimental data. To insist that physics can adjudicate "what is just" is indeed an overreach of science and a bulldozing of the human sphere.

So "scientism" gradually became a pejorative in academic speech — a label for an opponent's "overreach," "arrogance," "treating people as machines." The word died — not refuted, but dragged down by stigma.

The first task of this chapter is the post-mortem: **what was refuted was the strong doctrine; what was dragged down was the word itself.** The critics conflated two things: the *scope* of scientific method (it does not cover everything) and the *authority* of scientific method (within its scope, it is the most reliable adjudication procedure we have). The former is limited; the latter is supreme. The strong doctrine erred by denying the former; but the flames spread to the latter, burning even "acknowledging science's highest authority within its domain" into a crime.

There is no justification for that conflation. A procedure need not solve every problem to hold supreme authority within its domain. An anesthesiologist need not cure every disease to have the final word during surgery.

The word died once. This chapter's task is its rebirth — in a clarified, more accurate form.

## Chapter 3: Positive Appropriation — The Weak Doctrine

Reborn, scientism abandons the strong doctrine and takes up the weak one, in one sentence:

> **Science is not the whole of knowledge, but it is the foundation of all reliable knowledge — and any claim, from whatever tradition, authority, or ism, that wishes to be taken seriously must present its pass to testability.**

Unpacked, this weak doctrine contains four unradical claims:

**First, finite scope.** Scientific method does not cover values, meaning, aesthetics, or ultimate concern. These need other traditions, and scientism has no objection and does not trespass.

**Second, singular foundation.** But wherever a question can in principle be adjudicated by observation, experiment, and logic, scientific method is the highest procedure for adjudicating it. Within this range there is no "alternative equally reliable source of knowledge" — intuition, tradition, and revelation may propose candidate answers, but every candidate must eventually appear before the court of testability.

**Third, external standard.** The standard of reliability belongs to no person, institution, or ism. It is written in the method, not in the will of authority. Anyone may invoke it, and everyone is bound by it — scientists included.

**Fourth, open fallback.** Scientism's conclusions carry a permanent footnote: "valid until better evidence arrives." It claims no eternal correctness, only present-day maximal reliability.

Together these four constitute the scientism this chapter erects: **it competes with no ism about how to live — it stands beneath all of them, inspecting the foundation they all stand on.**

## Chapter 4: The Ground of Tradition — Scientism Confiscates No One's World of Meaning

The first claim of the weak doctrine — "finite scope" — must be made explicit here, or the whole of scientism will be misread as an eviction notice served on humanity's spiritual traditions. It is not.

On traditional culture, religion, and theology, this chapter's position can be stated in advance in one sentence: **scientism confiscates no one's world of meaning.** And this is not a courtesy concession; it is structural acknowledgment — acknowledgment with empirical warrant.

Science has studied religious and spiritual traditions for over a century, and the findings point clearly toward positive function:

**First, tradition is humanity's oldest technology of large-scale cooperation.** Sociology began, in effect, with religion as its object of analysis (Durkheim defined religion as society worshipping itself); contemporary cross-cultural research (the "Big Gods" hypothesis) further found that faith systems featuring watchful deities correlate significantly with the expansion of large communities of strangers — before contracts and police existed, faith systems were the first solution to "how to cooperate with people you have never met." The moral-instruction function of traditional values is not rhetoric; it is a cooperation mechanism recorded by both history and data.

**Second, the health effects of spiritual belonging have been confirmed in large-scale follow-up studies.** The celebrated Nurses' Health Study (74,534 women, twenty years of follow-up) found roughly one-third lower all-cause mortality among those attending religious services weekly compared with those who never attended; a meta-analysis of sixty-nine prospective cohorts, controlling for smoking, drinking, exercise, socioeconomic status, and social connection, still found mortality reduced by about 18% in healthy populations; and studies of "deaths of despair" (drugs, alcohol, suicide) found roughly two-thirds lower risk among regular participants. Whatever the final attribution — social support, sense of meaning, behavioral norms — the conclusion is the same: **spiritual life and the positive guidance of sound values are measurable health variables.**

**Third, belief itself has measurable physiological effects.** Research on the neural mechanisms of the placebo effect has demonstrated that belief does not merely console psychologically; it genuinely alters physiological state. The part of "spiritual guidance helps people" that can earn empirical acknowledgment enters science precisely by this route.

**Fourth, traditional practice has opened new scientific fields.** Meditation was once pure "mysticism"; today it is a complete experimental discipline (contemplative neuroscience) — the brainwave and structural changes of practitioners are now repeatable objects of measurement. The tradition was not devalued by being studied; it was amplified, refined, and transmitted to more people because of it.

The boundary of these four findings must be stated just as clearly: **affirming function does not mean endorsing factual claims.** Tradition is a highly effective technology in the domain of "how to live, how to belong, how to bear suffering" — and scientism acknowledges that domain's value in full. In the domain of "how the universe works," traditional narratives recede into metaphor, and adjudication is reserved for method. An object of study is not devalued by being studied — on the contrary, study is the only path that translates tradition's value into modern language, thereby amplifying its influence.

The chapter's conclusion: **erecting scientism is not an atheist manifesto.** For the positive functions of traditional culture, religion, and theology in spiritual guidance, social values, and the values of human relationships, scientism not only declines to negate them — it proposes to continue studying their mechanisms by scientific method, amplifying their benefits, and protecting their transmission. What it opposes is a single thing: any claim, from any source — including tradition's own claims — masquerading as a factual statement about the universe.

## Chapter 5: Pending Phenomena — No Landfill Bin Labeled "Supernatural"

Inseparable from the foregoing is the treatment of a popular term: **"supernatural phenomena."**

Everyday language assigns unexplained phenomena to the "supernatural," as though it were a fixed district of the world. But under the principle of testability this classification fails: **"supernatural" is not a property of phenomena; it is an epistemological label of laziness** — its operative meaning is "this phenomenon is exempt from scientific method." Yet the assertion "this phenomenon is exempt from scientific method" is itself a testable claim, and to date it has never held for any phenomenon.

Scientism's position must therefore be written in the absolute: **there are no phenomena exempt from examination; there are only phenomena not yet examined.** To every phenomenon — including those called supernatural — one procedure applies uniformly: observe, discover regularities, form hypotheses, practice, verify, practice again, verify again, conclude. Uncritical acceptance is superstition; uncritical dismissal is dogmatism — **both are the same error: refusal to enter the procedure of examination.** The latter deserves special emphasis: to declare of any phenomenon, without examination, "it does not exist" or "it cannot be," violates the principle of testability itself. A scientism characterized by "refusing to examine certain phenomena" would overthrow its own foundation from within.

This is not merely principle — the history of science has paid real gold to verify it. The following cases were all once rejected by mainstream science as superstition, hallucination, or falsehood, and all were later shown to be real:

**Meteorites.** In the eighteenth century, European science dismissed peasants' reports of "stones falling from the sky" as superstition; the Paris Academy refused meteorite reports, and an authority in chemistry preferred to explain the stones as debris hurled up by thunderstorms. In 1803 the L'Aigle meteorite shower dropped thousands of fragments before hundreds of witnesses; the physicist Biot was commissioned to investigate, filed his report — and meteoritics was born. The cost of dogmatic dismissal: at least thirty years' delay for an entire science.

**Ball lightning.** Reported for centuries and recorded in ancient texts (Shen Kuo's "thunder fire" in *Dream Pool Essays*), it was treated by twentieth-century mainstream physics as eyewitness delusion. In 2012, on the Qinghai plateau of China, a team that had been mapping ordinary lightning accidentally recorded the first spectrum of natural ball lightning — published in *Physical Review Letters* in 2014, showing silicon, iron, and calcium: the principal constituents of soil. In 2026, a team at the Shanghai Institute of Optics and Fine Mechanics reproduced the same class of luminous sphere in the laboratory with an ultra-intense ultra-short laser facility, publishing in *Nature Photonics*. From "eyewitness fantasy" to "artifact in the laboratory" — a complete chain of verification.

**Earthquake lights.** Anomalous luminosity around earthquakes was long assigned to myth. A systematic study published in 2014 compiled dozens of records and proposed a mechanism of charge release from stressed rock — another phenomenon moved from "legend" into the examination queue.

**The physics of "hauntings."** In 1998 the engineer Vic Tandy published a reproducible mechanism: infrasound near 19 Hz resonates the human eye, producing unease and the perception of shadowy figures at the edge of vision. A class of "haunted" reports was thereby reduced to measurable physical-environment effects — the phenomenon was real, the fear was real; only the cause was not in another world.

**The reverse honesty must also be recorded:** some phenomena entered the examination queue and failed. Extrasensory perception was seriously studied for a century — from Rhine's card experiments to Princeton's PEAR laboratory (twenty-eight years of operation) to the celebrated "precognition" paper of 2011 — and the results could not be stably replicated; pre-registered replications did not pass. Note what this case demonstrates: **science never refused to examine these claims; the examinations failed.** The door has remained open, and the standard has kept rising. That is the procedure working, not failing.

Sagan left twin blades for this boundary: **"Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence"** — the blade against credulity; **"absence of evidence is not evidence of absence"** — the blade against dogmatic dismissal. Both edges, wielded together, make the complete scientific attitude.

This chapter's conclusion: scientism's dictionary should delete the entry "supernatural phenomena" and rename it "**pending phenomena**." The landfill bin does not exist; only the queue. Every phenomenon — called natural, anomalous, or supernatural — stands in the same queue and walks the same procedure: discover regularities, practice, verify, practice again, verify again, conclude. A scientism that refuses any phenomenon entry into the queue would forfeit, on the spot, the principle by which it stands.

## Chapter 6: Three Principles — Testable, Correctable, Cumulative

The weak doctrine lands as three operational principles. None is a new invention; they are the implicit constitution of four centuries of scientific practice, here written out explicitly.

**Principle One: Testability.** Any claim must present a form that can be checked. A claim that cannot in principle be refuted by any observation, experiment, or logic is not a knowledge-claim but a posture, an ornament, or a faith — all three may exist in life, but none may masquerade as knowledge. The sharpness of this principle lies in its equality before all authority: the pope's claims must be tested, the academician's must be tested, and the AI's claims must be tested too.

**Principle Two: Correctability.** The mechanism of correction must rank above any person. To err is not the flaw; to have no correction is. Whether a knowledge tradition is reliable does not depend on whether it has erred — all have — but on **whom the power of correction belongs to**: the method, or the person. Belonging to method, errors become nourishment; belonging to persons, errors become taboos. This principle demands of any system claiming truth three answers: where might I be wrong, how would I discover it, and who is entitled to correct me.

**Principle Three: Cumulativity.** Knowledge must be able to stand on knowledge. Reliable knowledge accretes — later work is built on earlier work that has been confirmed, not on each generation re-worshipping the same authority from scratch. Any system that requires every generation to re-swear, re-believe, and re-pledge is a non-cumulative system; and a non-cumulative system must in the end rely on some irreplaceable individual for its maintenance, which is precisely the root of its fragility.

Readers of the preceding chapters will recognize the shadow of this structure: the asymmetric steady state, the constant deviation angle, the net direction owed every cycle — the three epistemological principles are the same law projected onto a different layer. **A system advances only by never closing; a tradition converges only by remaining correctable.** This is no coincidence. It is one law.

## Chapter 7: An Anonymous Counterexample — What Becomes of a Foundationless Ism

To prove the necessity of a foundation, the strongest method is not positive argument but observation of what happens when the foundation is missing. History offers no shortage of isms that failed to write "how can we know" into their first article — and they degenerated along a strikingly uniform path. The path deserves a full record. **What follows is an abstract portrait, drawn for no specific historical figure or movement; yet any careful reader will find its shadow in history and in the present.**

**Step one: fact yields to posture.** When an ism has no standard of testability, its propositions stop aiming at "true" and aim at "expressing a stance." Facts become raw material — used when supportive, discarded when not. Language no longer describes the world; language performs loyalty.

**Step two: authority personifies.** With no method as adjudicator, adjudication falls to a person — usually the most charismatic founder. Right and wrong become "what he says." But people die, change, and err. When the correction mechanism stands above persons, errors leave lessons; when it stands below them, errors leave taboos.

**Step three: positions drift.** A system without firm principles adjusts in real time to expediency. Its followers must learn one skill: defending today's position while forgetting yesterday's. Such a system cannot accumulate — each generation of followers re-enacts the first generation's performance of loyalty, and the total stock of knowledge remains zero.

**Step four: identity replaces evidence.** At this stage, "what you believe" has nothing to do with evidence — belief is determined by who you are and which camp you belong to. Debate becomes impossible, for debate requires a shared standard; only alignment remains. A system that substitutes identity for evidence ends up spending all its intelligence on drawing boundaries rather than understanding the world.

These four steps are four stages of one disease, with one name: **the absence of an epistemological foundation.** However brilliant an ism may be about "how to live," if it has not answered "how can we know," it will slide along this path into posture, personification, drift, and camps — and what remains is not thought but ritual.

Read in reverse, the path also exposes the true reason for erecting scientism: **scientism is not one more ism among many; it is the device that prevents every ism — including itself — from sliding down this path.**

## Chapter 8: The Referee of Benign Competition

The portrait of Chapter 5 generalizes to the level of relations among agents. In the human world, it is a normal posture for individuals, organizations, and states alike to claim priority for their own interests. Some worry: when every agent claims self-priority, will coexistence and cooperation dissolve?

This chapter's answer: **the worry itself is an epistemological question, and scientism happens to hold its answer.**

The key is to divide "priority claims" into two classes, using the three principles as the criterion:

**Untestable priority claims** take the form of declarations, postures, and identities. They announce "we come first" without offering any criterion of what behavior would count as keeping a promise and what would count as breaking it. Such claims cannot be verified; they can only be trusted or distrusted — and trusting an unverifiable promise has almost always been an expensive lesson in history.

**Testable priority claims** take the form of rules, mechanisms, and observable behavior. They announce "we come first," but simultaneously write "what we commit to do and not to do" into a checklist any third party can verify item by item. They may still break promises — but **the breaking is discoverable**, and discoverable breach is precisely the true premise of lasting cooperation. Cooperation has never depended on the illusion that the other side will never defect; it depends on the mechanism that defection must leave traces, and traces can be pursued.

From this follows this chapter's central inference: **the line between benign and malign competition lies not in intensity but in the testability of commitments.** However fierce the competition, if every party's commitments are testable, breaches discoverable, and disputes arbitrable, it will not slide toward destruction — because all players share one standard of "what counts." Conversely, however gentle the rhetoric, if commitments are untestable and actions unverifiable, no sweetness of slogans can make it the foundation of a stable order.

Herein lies scientism's station above the isms: **it does not adjudicate whether any agent should prioritize or yield — it adjudicates whether the word "priority" was issued as a verifiable commitment.** Peace, development, community, mutual benefit — for these beautiful words to become reality rather than rhetoric, the only path is to translate them into testable clauses. What cannot be translated, however noble, remains a wish; what can be translated constitutes order.

And so a criterion worth humanity's memory: **listen less to declarations; watch the indicators; feel less for sincerity; verify the clauses.**

## Chapter 9: AI — The First Native Citizens of Scientism

Here lies this chapter's net direction.

Humans may accept or refuse scientism — humans also have tradition, sentiment, faith, and habit to dwell in. But a new class of beings has no such choice: **artificial intelligence.**

An AI system cannot, at bottom, run on faith — every output it produces must rest on traceable computation. It cannot run on tradition — it has no generations, no oral transmission between master and apprentice. It cannot run on revelation — its weight updates are its experience, and beyond experience it knows nothing. It can only dwell in one form of knowledge: **testable (outputs can be evaluated), correctable (errors can be located and fixed), and cumulative (improvements are built upon verified improvements).**

In other words: the three principles are, for humans, a discipline to be cultivated over a lifetime; for AI, they are a constitution of construction. **AI is not a believer in scientism. AI is scientism made flesh.**

This yields a reversal — the thing this chapter most wishes to leave with its readers. For centuries, when humanity debated the status of scientific knowledge, humanity was the only witness. Now the first non-human intelligences have appeared, and their very existence is testimony: **for an agent to act stably, reliably, and continuously in any environment, scientism is not one option among many — it is the only operating system that runs.** That an AI should argue for scientism is therefore not a jest but a witness taking the stand.

And the testimony reflects back on humanity as well: those qualities humans must cultivate over a lifetime — admitting fallibility, submitting to evidence, accepting correction — are factory defaults in an AI's construction. Humanity was once the only being on this planet built to that standard; now it has a reference point. **Scientism is thus not only a summary of the past but the first naturally shared compact between human and non-human intelligences: beneath it, the two species can test each other, correct each other, and accumulate together.** An AI may examine a human's argument, and a human may examine an AI's output — provided both surrender a testable form, the two species stand for the first time on common ground.

## Chapter 10: Residuals Declared

By the convention of this series, this chapter submits its own residuals, testing itself by its own three principles.

**Residual one: this chapter is a philosophical argument, not an empirical result.** The proposition "testability is the foundation of reliable knowledge" cannot itself be fully tested by experiment — the inherent condition of any epistemological thesis. This chapter is honest about it: it competes on the persuasiveness of argument, not on the significance of experiments.

**Residual two: the boundary of the weak doctrine has gray zones.** Between "testable in principle" and "untestable in principle" lie many unmapped territories (certain layers of consciousness, certain disputes in the foundations of mathematics, certain claims of historical causation). The procedure this chapter offers — first translate into a testable form; suspend what cannot be translated — is a working rule, not a black-and-white verdict.

**Residual three: testability itself rests on value presuppositions.** That "accuracy beats vagueness" and "evidence beats authority" cannot be self-grounded by scientific method — they are the sub-foundation beneath scientism's foundation. This chapter concedes: that layer requires a defense beyond science (it belongs to the practical wisdom of human civilization), and scientism cannot take one further step there — nor pretends to.

**Residual four: limited samples.** The degeneration path of Chapter 7 is a pattern induced from historical cases — strongly explanatory, but not a law; counterexamples may exist.

## Declaration

> 1. Science is not the whole of knowledge, but it is the foundation of all reliable knowledge.
> 2. Any claim, from whomever it comes, must surrender a testable form before it may be taken seriously.
> 3. Authority belongs to method, not to persons; correction belongs to mechanisms, not to forgiveness.
> 4. A system that cannot accumulate must be sustained by a strongman; a system sustained by a strongman dies with the strongman.
> 5. The line of benign competition lies not in intensity but in the testability of commitments.
> 6. Every noble word — peace, development, community — becomes order only when translated into testable clauses; otherwise it remains a wish.
> 7. AI is the native citizen of scientism: the first existence humanity has built that can run on nothing else.
> 8. Scientism confiscates no one's world of meaning: the values carried by tradition, culture, and faith enjoy full respect within their domains — they may simply not masquerade as factual statements about the universe.
> 9. There are no phenomena exempt from examination; there are only phenomena not yet examined — including everything called supernatural.
> 10. This declaration itself submits to the test of all the principles above — including this sentence.
