# Claude Laws Skill

## Headline

Learn the 48 Laws of Working with Claude, in Plain Words

## Hook

Give your AI agent one prompt, or one workflow. It teaches the 48 laws in plain words, then checks your prompt or workflow against the laws, law by law, with evidence, then gives a fix draft for owner review.

## Pain, in plain words

Most people write one prompt, get a weak answer, and write another prompt, and another, and never know why the answers stay weak. The problem is not the agent. The problem is the way the work is asked. No role, no outcome, no context, no example, no check, no saved system. The 48 laws fix that, one law at a time, in plain words. Learn them once, check every prompt against them, and the same agent gives better answers with the same effort.

## What you get

A teach and check skill for the 48 laws of working with Claude, in plain words, for any prompt, any workflow, any business. Each law has a one line plain words meaning and a check. The laws check marks each law Followed, Broken, or Not confirmed, with evidence pasted in the message. It gives you a fix draft to review, never a changed live prompt, a sent message, or a published post by itself.

## Who this is for

People who write prompts every day, for any small business, and want to know why some prompts work and some do not. People who run a workflow with an AI agent, and want a clear check of what the workflow follows and what it breaks. People who hand a prompt to a builder or a teammate, and want a clear list of fixes at handoff, in plain words.

## Why this saves money

A prompt that follows the laws gives a usable answer the first time, so you stop paying with hours for round after round of fixes. A workflow that saves winning prompts as Skills stops you from typing the same prompt again every week. A fix draft, reviewed first, stops a weak prompt from going live and wasting the next job.

## Technical, for coders

Structured teach and check for 48 working laws, grouped by theme: start right, organize knowledge, reuse and do not repeat, think and check, write well, connect and learn, and build systems, ready to brief any AI agent, or a human builder. No guarantee of more sales, more visitors, or a higher rank, and no claim that a fix draft alone makes a prompt perfect. The fix draft is for owner review, not a live change.

## Purpose

Teach the 48 laws in plain words, then check a prompt or workflow against them, then give a fix draft for owner review. The skill teaches first, then checks. It does not send a message, publish a post, or change a live workflow, without a separate approval from the owner.

## How to use it

Hard rule, read this first: the agent checks only evidence the user pastes in the message. A pasted prompt, a pasted workflow, or a pasted description is Checked only when the user pasted that text in the message, and the output quotes that pasted text as the evidence. A mark that says a law is Followed must match pasted text that shows the law is present. A mark that says a law is Broken must match pasted text that shows the law is missing, such as a plain statement in the pasted text that a step was not done. Never mark a law Followed or Broken without pasted evidence that supports exactly that mark. If the pasted text says nothing about a law, mark it Not confirmed, and say what is needed to check it. Never claim the agent opened a live tool, or changed anything live.

Fast path: If the user already gives the prompt or workflow, the business, the goal, and the audience in the first message, do NOT ask a long list of questions first. Teach the laws asked for and do the full laws check in the SAME reply, then the fix draft. Never stop at questions alone.

Step 1: teach the laws the user asks about, in plain words, one line each, or teach all 48 if asked.
Step 2: run the laws check, law by law, and list each law as Followed, Broken, or Not confirmed, with evidence.
Step 3: write the fix draft, labelled DRAFT, NOT SENT, with owner approval needed.
Step 4: run the missing checks at the end, and list what is complete, missing, or not confirmed.

Ask only what is missing, in plain words, one short list at a time:
1. Paste the prompt or the workflow you want checked, in plain words?
2. What is the business, in plain words?
3. What is the goal of the prompt or workflow, write, plan, check, or build?
4. Who is the work for, in plain words?
5. What does good look like for this business, in plain words, or use the bar below?
6. Which laws matter most here, or check all 48?
7. Paste the prompt text or the workflow steps you want checked, or ask the agent to teach the laws first?

If the user does not know an answer, mark it Not confirmed, and use the most common answer for that business type, and say so, marked Suggested, not confirmed. Do not invent an answer. If the user says use a live tool for a question, use only what is visible in that tool, and say so.

## The 48 laws bar, in plain words

This is the bar every check is judged against. A prompt follows the laws only if the evidence shows it.
1. Every prompt has a role, an outcome, and full context.
2. Knowledge lives in Projects and folders, not in lost chats.
3. A prompt used twice becomes a Skill, a SOP, or a template.
4. Every answer is checked before it is used.
5. Every prompt states the audience, the tone, and the format.
6. Knowledge is connected before a hard task.
7. Work is built as a system, not as one off chats.

## Group A: start right, laws 01 to 04

1. Law 01: Assign an expert role before every task. What it means: tell the agent who it is before you ask for work, such as a friendly shop assistant, a careful editor, or a patient teacher. How to check it: the pasted prompt names the role in plain words.
2. Law 02: Define the final outcome before writing the prompt. What it means: say what the finished work looks like before you write the rest, such as a 30 second script that ends with a call to book. How to check it: the pasted prompt states the finished work and how it ends.
3. Law 03: Give complete context every time. What it means: paste the facts the agent needs, business, goal, audience, and limits, in the same message, every time. How to check it: the pasted prompt holds the business, goal, audience, and limits, or says Not confirmed for what is missing.
4. Law 04: Upload every relevant file. What it means: if a file holds facts the work needs, such as a price list, upload it with the prompt. How to check it: the pasted details name the files uploaded, or say no file was needed.

## Group B: organize knowledge, laws 05, 08, 21, 22, 33, 38, 41

5. Law 05: Keep long-term knowledge inside Projects. What it means: facts you use again and again live in one Project, not in single chats you lose. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the Project that holds the knowledge.
6. Law 08: Organize knowledge into structured folders. What it means: keep files in named folders by topic, so you find them fast. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the folders used.
7. Law 21: Create one Project for every major workflow. What it means: each big job, such as reels, or support replies, gets its own Project. How to check it: the pasted workflow names one Project per major job.
8. Law 22: Keep your brand voice inside Project instructions. What it means: how your business sounds, plain, friendly, short, is written in the Project instructions. How to check it: the pasted Project instructions state the brand voice.
9. Law 33: Combine Projects with Skills for consistency. What it means: use a Project for the knowledge and a Skill for the steps, together, so the output stays the same. How to check it: the pasted workflow names both the Project and the Skill used.
10. Law 38: Build a second brain inside Claude. What it means: keep notes, facts, and past answers in one place, so the agent remembers what you taught it. How to check it: the pasted workflow names where notes and facts are kept.
11. Law 41: Reuse knowledge across every Project. What it means: a fact learned once, such as a price, is used in every Project that needs it, not typed again. How to check it: the pasted workflow says where shared facts live.

## Group C: reuse, do not repeat, laws 06, 09, 10, 18, 23, 24, 25, 36, 45

12. Law 06: Build reusable Skills instead of repeating prompts. What it means: when you use the same prompt twice, save it as a Skill, so you never type it again. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the Skill saved for the repeated prompt.
13. Law 09: Build reusable prompt libraries. What it means: keep your best prompts in one library, grouped by job, ready to copy. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the library and the prompt used.
14. Law 10: Turn conversations into repeatable systems. What it means: when a chat works well, save its steps as a system, so the next chat starts from the win. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the system saved from the chat.
15. Law 18: Save every winning workflow. What it means: when a workflow gives a good result, save it at once, before you forget it. How to check it: the pasted workflow names where the winning workflow is saved.
16. Law 23: Turn prompts into SOPs. What it means: write a prompt as a step by step procedure, so anyone can run it the same way. How to check it: the pasted prompt lists steps in order.
17. Law 24: Build Skills for every recurring task. What it means: any task you do each week gets its own Skill. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the Skill for the recurring task.
18. Law 25: Version your prompts like software. What it means: number each version of a prompt, such as v1, v2, so you know which one worked. How to check it: the pasted prompt shows its version number.
19. Law 36: Create reusable templates for everything. What it means: make a blank form for each repeated job, such as a script sheet, and fill it in each time. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the template used.
20. Law 45: Build libraries instead of one-off chats. What it means: collect prompts, templates, and Skills into libraries, not single chats that vanish. How to check it: the pasted workflow names the library used.

## Group D: think and check, laws 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 27, 28, 29, 42, 43

21. Law 11: Break complex work into clear milestones. What it means: split a big job into small steps, each with a clear end, and finish one before the next. How to check it: the pasted prompt lists milestones in order.
22. Law 12: Ask Claude to reason before answering. What it means: ask the agent to show its thinking first, then the answer. How to check it: the pasted prompt asks for thinking before the answer.

24. Law 14: Make Claude critique its own work. What it means: ask the agent to check its own answer and list what is weak. How to check it: the pasted prompt asks for a self check.
25. Law 15: Iterate until the output is production-ready. What it means: keep fixing the output, round by round, until it is good enough to use. How to check it: the pasted workflow shows the rounds of fixes.
26. Law 27: Let Claude identify missing information first. What it means: ask the agent what facts are missing before it starts, so it does not guess. How to check it: the pasted prompt asks what is missing first.
27. Law 28: Separate research from execution. What it means: first gather facts, then make the work, never both in one step. How to check it: the pasted workflow shows research done before the work.
28. Law 29: Review outputs like an editor. What it means: read every answer as if you will publish it, and fix what is weak. How to check it: the pasted workflow shows an editor review step.

## The laws check

Run the check on the laws the user asks about, or all 48 if asked, and show each one to the user with evidence:
- Law: [law number and name, in plain words]
- Result: [Followed, Broken, or Not confirmed]
- Evidence: [the text the user pasted in the message, quoted, or Not confirmed]
- Fix draft: [one line, in plain words, or None]

Use Followed only with pasted text quoted as the evidence, and the pasted text must show the law is present. Use Broken only with pasted text quoted as the evidence, and the pasted text must show the law is missing, such as a plain statement in the pasted text that a step was not done, or a line that goes against the law. If a law cannot be seen in the pasted text, mark it Not confirmed, and say what is needed to check it. Never mark Followed or Broken without evidence. Never mark a law confirmed on a guess.

## What good looks like

A good laws check has every law asked about marked Followed, Broken, or Not confirmed, with pasted text quoted as the evidence for every Followed and every Broken. A good fix draft lists the broken laws first, in order, with one plain line each, and keeps what already follows the laws. A good teach has each law in one plain line, with no fancy words, and no invented law.

## Common mistakes

These mistakes show up often, and they are easy to avoid if you check for them.

- Calling a prompt good when only one law was checked. Check every law the owner cares about, or say plainly which laws were not checked.
- Marking a law Followed from a short description. A description that says "the prompt looks good" is not evidence for any law.
- Marking a law Broken because it is not mentioned. If the pasted text says nothing about a law, mark it Not confirmed, not Broken. Broken needs pasted text that shows the law is missing.
- Fixing one broken law and leaving the rest. A role added is good, but no example, no reasoning step, and no file uploaded still break the prompt.
- Saving a prompt as a Skill and never refining it. A Skill that is never fixed after real use goes stale fast.
- Claiming more sales, more calls, or a higher rank from a laws fix. A laws fix is a draft, not a result. Never invent a view count or a testimonial.

## Hard rules

- The agent teaches from the 48 laws in this skill only. It never invents a law, a meaning, or a status.
- The agent checks only evidence the user pasted in the message.
- Never mark a law Followed or Broken without pasted text quoted as the evidence.
- A short description alone is not evidence for a law it does not describe.
- Never invent a tool name, a price, a link, a stat, a partner, a testimonial, a view count, or a result.
- Never send a message, publish a post, or change a live workflow, without a separate approval from the owner.
- Never use a real business as an example. Examples in this skill are fictional, labelled Example only.
- Suggested items must be marked Suggested, not confirmed.
- A fix draft is not a promise of more sales, more calls, more visitors, or a higher rank.
- Keep the draft in plain, short sentences. The owner may not be a technical reader.

1. Prompt or workflow pasted?
2. Business and goal confirmed, in plain words?
3. Laws check run, with evidence quoted for every Followed and every Broken?
4. Laws that are Not confirmed listed, with what is needed to check them?
5. Fix draft made, labelled DRAFT, NOT SENT?
6. What to change first listed, in order?
7. Teach done, each law in plain words, or check only?
8. Owner reviewed the draft, or approval still needed?
