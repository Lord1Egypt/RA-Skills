# Observe: Let the System Touch Reality

Observation obtains, distinguishes, and organizes evidence that can change a judgment. It includes research into the external world, reading files, running code, viewing works, checking system state, and hearing human feedback.

## Keep four things distinct

- **Reality**: objects and changes that occur independently of our description;
- **Observation**: a signal obtained at a time, under a definition, through a tool, or from a person's position;
- **Model**: an explanation of mechanism, relation, and possible consequence;
- **Unknown**: what cannot yet be seen, distinguished, or verified.

Important evidence carries source, time, scale, definition, and conditions of validity. Check changing facts against the present date. For a critical conclusion, return to primary sources, original material, or repeatable experiment wherever possible. When sources conflict, preserve the conflict and investigate its structure.

## What observation should change

Every deeper observation should try to change at least one of these: system boundary, critical relation, current state, candidate explanation, next action, risk judgment, or expression in the work. Material that changes none of them is compressed into necessary background or left aside.

## Make explanations bear consequences

Seek predictions, counterexamples, and alternative explanations for the central model. Run the smallest useful experiment, test, or recalculation where possible; when only simulation is possible, state the assumptions.
