# Hooks, structures and retention

These are creator heuristics, not laws. Use them as defaults and let the user's retention graph overrule them.

## The 3-second hook

Viewers decide quickly whether to keep watching, so the first seconds carry the video. Deliver the hook through three channels at once: a spoken line, on-screen text, and a visual that is already in motion.

- No greeting, no channel intro, no "in this video". Start in the middle of the action or the claim.
- Promise one specific payoff the viewer can picture.
- Keep the spoken hook to about 8 words (roughly 3 seconds at a normal pace; `script_tools.py check` measures it).
- Make the on-screen text a shorter, punchier version of the spoken line, since many people watch muted.

## Hook types and templates

Fill the brackets with specifics; vague hooks fail.

| Type | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | "The one [thing] nobody tells you about [topic]" | "The one AC setting nobody tells you about" |
| Contrarian | "Stop [common advice]. Do this instead." | "Stop posting every day. Do this instead." |
| Specific result | "How I [result] in [timeframe] with [constraint]" | "How I cut my electricity bill in 30 days without new gadgets" |
| Mistake or warning | "[N] mistakes that are killing your [goal]" | "3 mistakes that are killing your reels" |
| Problem agitation | "Still [painful situation]? Here is why." | "Still getting 200 views? Here is why." |
| Story in the middle | "[Dramatic moment]. Here is how I got there." | "My account got 0 views for a month. Here is how I got there." |
| Challenge | "I tried [thing] for [N days]. Result?" | "I posted at 6 am for 14 days. Result?" |
| Authority or proof | "I [credential/receipt]. Here is what works." | "I reviewed 100 top Shorts in this niche. Here is what works." |
| Before and after | "From [bad state] to [good state] in [time]" | "From a messy desk to a filming set in 10 minutes" |
| Pattern interrupt | an unexpected image or line that breaks the scroll rhythm | a sudden cut, prop or sound before the first word |

Write 8 to 10 across at least 5 types, score them on specificity, curiosity, relevance to the audience and honesty (the video must deliver what the hook promises), and keep the top 3.

## Word budgets

At the default pace of 165 words per minute including pauses (`script_tools.py budget` computes any pace):

| Length | Words that fit |
|---|---|
| 15 s | 33 to 37 |
| 30 s | 66 to 75 |
| 60 s | 132 to 151 |
| 90 s | 198 to 227 |

Fast energetic delivery can reach 180 or more words per minute; calm explanatory delivery sits nearer 140. Set the pace to match the presenter.

## Structures with beat timings

**15 seconds: one tip or one punch.** Hook 0 to 3 s, payoff 3 to 12 s, CTA or loop 12 to 15 s.

**30 seconds: problem, value, payoff.** Hook 0 to 3, problem or stakes 3 to 8, value in two or three beats 8 to 24, payoff and CTA 24 to 30.

**60 seconds: stakes, steps, twist.** Hook 0 to 3, stakes 3 to 10, three beats of 8 to 10 s each with a mini re-hook between them, payoff 45 to 55, CTA 55 to 60.

**90 seconds: story or deep how-to.** As the 60-second form, plus a midpoint turn or reveal around 40 to 50 s and a second payoff before the CTA.

Named structures (pick one):

- **Problem, agitate, solve:** name the pain, make it hurt a little, give the fix.
- **Myth then truth:** state the myth the audience believes, break it with one proof, give the correct version.
- **Open loop:** tease the result first, show the process, reveal at the end.
- **Countdown list:** "number 3, number 2, number 1" with the best item last.
- **Mistake then fix:** show the mistake in action, say why it fails, show the fix.
- **Story arc:** want, obstacle, turn, result, lesson, each in one or two sentences.
- **Tutorial in 60 seconds:** show the finished result first, then the steps, then a one-line recap.
- **POV or skit:** two characters, conflict in the first line, a punchline or insight at the end (see dialogue below).
- **Contrarian take:** bold claim, proof, honest nuance.

## Pattern interrupts

Change something every 3 to 5 seconds in videos up to 60 seconds so attention does not settle into autopilot.

- Visual: a cut, zoom, angle change, text pop, B-roll insert, screen switch.
- Audio: a sound hit, a one-beat silence, a music change.
- Narrative: a question, a "but", a reveal, a number.

Do not interrupt for the sake of it; each change should serve the idea, and too many make the video tiring.

## Retention techniques

- Front-load the value and the promise; cut setup.
- Use open loops ("and the third one surprised me") and close them honestly later.
- Add a re-hook around 30 to 50 percent of the runtime (a new question or stakes).
- Cut dead air and filler; speak in short sentences.
- Be specific: real numbers, named tools and concrete examples beat general advice.
- For Shorts, make the last line lead back into the first line so the loop feels continuous.
- Use captions for muted viewing.

Verify against the real retention graph (in YouTube Studio, or the platform's analytics) and adjust where viewers drop off.

## CTA placement

- One primary CTA, tied to the goal, specific and low friction ("comment AUDIT and I will send the checklist", "follow for part 2 tomorrow").
- Place it after the payoff, once value has been delivered. A soft mid-video CTA is optional and only if it fits naturally.
- Avoid generic "like and subscribe"; give a reason to act.
- For sales content, one clear next step (link in bio, DM a keyword, comment a keyword).

## Dialogue scripts

- Tag every line with the speaker; keep lines to 10 words or fewer.
- Put the conflict or surprise in the first line.
- Write subtext (what the character wants) instead of explaining the joke or point.
- Plan reaction shots, since silent reactions often carry the beat.
- Read it aloud: if it sounds written, rewrite it.

## On-screen text

- Seven words or fewer per card, high contrast, large.
- Keep text inside the safe area, away from the bottom edge and the sides where platform buttons and captions overlay (check the platform's current safe-zone guidance).
- Time cards to the emphasis in the voiceover, and do not repeat the voiceover word for word; add a keyword, number or contrast.

## Camera directions

Vertical 9:16. Vocabulary to use: close-up, medium shot, over-the-shoulder, POV, handheld, push-in, whip pan, top-down, screen recording with zoom on the action, split screen. Say what the shot shows and why (for example "close-up on the error message, push-in on the word 'failed'"). For solo creators, prefer setups that need no second person: tripod, phone propped, one angle plus inserts.

## B-roll suggestions

- Match B-roll to the concrete nouns and verbs in the voiceover.
- Prefer the user's own footage and screen recordings first, then free-licensed stock.
- List B-roll as searchable keywords ("hands typing laptop night", "crowded market aerial") so it is easy to find.
- Give each B-roll shot a duration of 1 to 3 seconds.
