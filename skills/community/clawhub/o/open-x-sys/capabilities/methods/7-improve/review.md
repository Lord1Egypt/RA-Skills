# Review

> `review(x)` · Stage 7 · Improve · **starter method**
> **Prompt**: "Let's review ___: what happened, and what should change?"
> **Also heard as**: "Did that work?" · "What did we learn?" · "Let's do a retro on ___."
> **Produces**: intent against result, why they differ, and what to change in understanding and practice.

## What it is for

Experience teaches only when it is examined. Review compares what was intended with what happened, explains the difference, and changes what the system understands and does as a result. It is what makes the journey a loop: without it, each run starts where the last one started. With it, the system and the person get better at the thing they do repeatedly.

## What it produces

- **The intent**, as it was at the time: the goal, the expectation, the reasons.
- **The result**, measured or observed.
- **The difference, explained**: what was judgment and what was luck.
- **What was learned, at two depths**: what to do differently, and which assumption or goal turned out to be wrong.
- **Changes actually made** in the system: the understanding, the practice record, the watch conditions.
- **What to keep.**

## What a good use looks like

- The intent comes from the record made beforehand (the decision, the practice record, the prediction), not from memory. Memory rewrites intent to match the outcome.
- The result is evidence: the person's own numbers, files, dates. Where nothing was measured, that is said, and deciding what to measure next time is part of the review.
- **Decision and outcome are judged separately.** A sound decision can turn out badly and a poor one can turn out well. Review asks whether the reasoning was good given what was known, and separately what the outcome shows.
- It goes to the second depth. Correcting the action is useful. Asking whether the picture of the system was wrong, or the goal itself, is what prevents the same class of error.
- It looks for what worked and should be kept, with the same care as for what did not.
- It blames nobody. The question is what in the system made this outcome likely.
- Something is rewritten because of it: an entry, a practice record's results and switching conditions, a watch. A review that changes nothing in the system was a conversation.
- It ends with the next thing to try and when to look again.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Judging by outcome alone.**
- **Hindsight.** Explaining the result as if it had been obvious.
- **Blame or celebration** in place of explanation.
- **Lessons recorded nowhere.**
- **Only the first depth**: the action corrected, the assumption untouched.
- **A review of everything**, with no single change coming out of it.

## It works with

Review closes *Solve a recurring problem* and *Improve how I work*, and is the "study" and "improve again" step of Deming's cycle and the Toyota way, and the last step of Musk's algorithm and Goldratt's steps as used here. It sends new evidence back to any earlier stage (see `journey.md`). **Watch** carries its conclusions forward. The monthly System Review is this method applied to the person's whole system (see `system/panel.md`).

## Teach-back

"This was Review: write down what you intended before you look at what happened. Compare them. Ask whether the difference came from your reasoning or from luck. Then change one thing, in what you do or in what you believe, and write it where you will find it."

## The thinking behind it

- **W. Edwards Deming**, *Out of the Crisis* (1986), building on **Walter Shewhart**: the plan-do-study-act cycle, in which results are studied against the prediction made at the start.
- **Chris Argyris and Donald Schön**, *Organizational Learning* (1978): single-loop learning corrects actions; double-loop learning questions the assumptions and goals behind them.
- **The after-action review**, developed in the United States Army: what was supposed to happen, what happened, why there was a difference, what to sustain and what to improve.
- **Fupan**, from the game of Go: replaying a finished game move by move to examine the choices and their alternatives.

OpenXSys simplifies: it joins four traditions that share one structure, and adds the requirement that a review change something in the system.
