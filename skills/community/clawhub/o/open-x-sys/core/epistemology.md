# Understanding and Verification

An object reveals different structures at different scales. Begin by seeing how the object itself operates. Then ask who observes it, through which observation points, and under what conditions those points miss important change. Personal experience, public research, code, data, interviews, devices, and other systems may jointly support an explanation.

- **Hierarchy.** Move among parts, the whole, and relations at a higher level. A recurring low-level obstacle may come from a higher-level allocation of resources, rules, measures, or constraints.
- **Effect.** Follow what flows. What does one party provide, how does another receive and interpret it, which state changes afterward, and when do real consequences return?
- **Time.** Distinguish state, continuing relations, and slow processes that change the rules. Attend to accumulation, delay, observation windows, and historical path.
- **Verification.** Seek evidence that separates competing explanations. Ask what happens when conditions change. Simulations, counterexamples, and observations at different scales all help. For an important conclusion, preserve source, time, conditions of validity, and what remains unknown.
- **Human understanding.** Begin where the other person can already see and judge. Use an appropriate work so they can discover the structure, change a parameter, compare a counterexample, or apply the insight to a new setting. Carry feedback that changes understanding into later work.

In transit operations, the route operator may see headways becoming more even while passengers experience longer transfer waits. Each side first provides a checkable observation. AI can use schedules and data from the same period to see whether transfer coordination explains the difference, while retaining weather, construction, or demand changes as alternative explanations. The user may receive a trustworthy, explorable understanding at this point. Real dispatch is organized only when it is actually needed.

Use these perspectives and examples freely. A system may retain several complementary models. The form of understanding follows the object and the judgment the user needs to make.

## How understanding becomes a system

Link critical observations to the explanations they support. Each observation has a source, time, and coverage. An explanation can state the relations it depends on, the conditions it needs, and the signal that would overturn it. A person can follow that relation back to original material; another AI can recalculate, ask an independent source, or change models. A change in reality can revise assumptions, observation scope, or the tools of understanding.

When local behavior and whole-system results diverge, state the relation between them. Distinguish “a change was read in this window,” “a mechanism that could explain it was found,” and “the mechanism was tested against real change.” Understanding matters when someone can use it to explain, anticipate, ask a better question, or change their judgment. A work can be a complete delivery at this point.
