# Open

> `open(x)` · Stage 1 · See
> **Prompt**: "Open a system for ___."
> **Also heard as**: "Help me really understand ___." · "What is ___, as a whole?" · "Open a system for me." (`open(me)`)
> **Reveals**: the whole: parts, relations, boundary, what matters.

## What it is for

Open is the first look at something as a system. It answers "what is this, taken whole?" before any question about why or what to do. Most misunderstandings of a company, a technology or a city come from seeing one part and taking it for the thing. Open replaces the part with the whole: what it consists of, how the parts act on each other, where it ends, and which few things about it actually matter.

## What it reveals

- **The parts**, named by the role each plays in the whole, not by the labels on an organization chart.
- **The relations** that carry the behavior: what gives what to what, what depends on what.
- **The boundary and the scale**: what is inside, what is outside but acts on it, and at what size it is this thing.
- **What matters**: of everything that could be said, the two or three structures that make it behave the way it does.
- **The essence in one sentence**, which is not the sentence everybody already says.

## What a good use looks like

- The boundary and the scale were chosen for this person's purpose. The same company opened for an investor and for a student is two different systems.
- All six zero-level questions were asked, and the two or three that grip this subject were pursued. They do not appear as six sections.
- The whole can be held in the head after reading: a reader could draw it from memory.
- What was left out is stated, and so is what is still unknown.
- It rests on evidence with sources and dates, and the understanding was tested at least once before being shown.
- It ends with a way of looking that the person can reuse on similar things.

## Where it goes wrong

- **An inventory.** Every part listed, nothing about how they act on each other.
- **An encyclopedia.** History, products, people, numbers, all true, no essence.
- **A boundary drawn too wide**, so that everything is included and nothing stands out.
- **The official description repeated.** How something presents itself is evidence, not the answer.
- **A node-link diagram** standing in for an understanding.

## It works with

Open is where most routes begin: *Understand it*, *Learn something deeply*, *Make content*, *Open my system*. It leads naturally to **X-ray** (what drives it), **Flow** (what moves through it) and **Loops** (what keeps it going). The **Boundary** and **Scale** lenses refine it when the edge or the size is itself in question. For the person's own work and life it is `open(me)`: see `system/root.md`.

## Teach-back

"This was Open: before asking why or what to do, say what the thing is as a whole. Name its parts by what they do, find how they act on each other, decide where it ends, and state its essence in one sentence."

## The thinking behind it

- **Ludwig von Bertalanffy**, *General System Theory* (1968): wholes have properties their parts do not; open systems exist through exchange with their environment.
- **Jane Jacobs**, *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* (1961), drawing on **Warren Weaver**, "Science and Complexity" (1948): a city is a problem of organized complexity, many factors interrelated into a whole, and cannot be understood as either a few variables or an average.
- **Qian Xuesen, Yu Jingyuan, Dai Ruwei**, "A new scientific field: open complex giant systems and their methodology" (1990): such systems need qualitative and quantitative knowledge integrated, from many sources and many experts.

OpenXSys simplifies: these are large bodies of work, used here for one habit, which is to look at the whole first.
