# Extracting a voice from samples

## What to collect

- 10 to 30 pieces the user is proud of and 5 they dislike (the contrast is informative). Mix formats: captions, scripts, emails, replies, website copy.
- Real audience reactions if available (comments, saves, replies): which pieces sound most like "us" to them.
- Spoken material (voice notes, video transcripts) shows how the person actually talks; written posts often drift formal.
- Exclude text written by other people or generic AI drafts the user never edited, or the profile will describe them.

## Interview questions (ask only what the samples do not answer)

1. Who are you talking to, and what do they worry about?
2. If the brand were a person at a party, who would they be and how would they talk?
3. Three words you want people to use about the brand; three you never want.
4. Which brands or creators do you like how they sound, and what exactly do you like?
5. Words, phrases, jokes or emoji you always or never use.
6. How do you handle complaints, mistakes, competitors, controversial topics?
7. Which language mix feels natural for your audience, and which feels forced?
8. What claims must you never make (legal, medical, financial, guarantees)?

## Reading the profile numbers

| Number | Reads as |
|---|---|
| avg_sentence_words under about 10, short_sentence_share high | punchy, conversational |
| avg_sentence_words above about 20, long_sentence_share high | formal, explanatory |
| exclaim_per_sentence high | enthusiastic (check it is not habit noise) |
| question_per_sentence high | engages the reader, uses hooks |
| first_person high | personal, founder-led; second_person high | reader-focused |
| contractions low | more formal (English only) |
| emoji_per_post, hashtags_per_post | platform habit; compare per platform, not overall |
| signature_phrases | recurring hooks or sign-offs worth keeping (or retiring if stale) |
| top_openers | patterns that may be overused |
| roman_urdu_share | the language mix; compare with posts' performance before changing it |

Profile per platform if the habits differ (one file per platform). The numbers are habits, not quality: a profile of weak posts describes weak habits.

## Audit scoring

For each of 10 to 20 recent posts: score each trait 1 (off) to 5 (clearly on) with a short quote, note vocabulary slips and tone mismatches, and mark the three most and least on-voice posts. Summarise the drift (for example "LinkedIn posts are more formal than the guide; replies are on voice") and give 3 specific fixes. Do not average scores into a single "brand score"; it hides the useful detail.

## Pitfalls

- A voice built from one great post is a snapshot, not a voice.
- Team voices differ from founder voices; ask whose voice the brand account uses.
- Avoid describing the voice only in adjectives ("friendly, professional"); use contrasts and examples so others can imitate it.
- Update the guide when the audience, product or platform mix changes.
