Opened 3 November 2026 for you, after you said that entropy "means disorder, and everything tends to get messier". It is one file because the understanding is short.

**The essence.** Entropy counts how many different microscopic arrangements look the same from the outside. A state that can be made in many ways has high entropy. Things drift toward such states for no deeper reason than that there are overwhelmingly more of them.

**The misunderstanding we corrected.** "Disorder" is a description of how a state looks to us. Entropy is a count. The two often agree, which is why the word survives, and they sometimes do not: oil and water separating into two tidy layers is an increase in total entropy, not a decrease. (Boltzmann's relation S = k log W is the count written as a formula; on the weakness of "disorder" as an explanation, see Frank Lambert, "Disorder: A Cracked Crutch for Supporting Entropy Discussions", Journal of Chemical Education, 2002.)

**The distinction you now make.** You can tell apart "this particular arrangement" (a microstate) and "what it looks like from outside" (a macrostate). You put it in your own words on 5 November: "a shuffled deck is not special, it is just that almost every order of the cards counts as shuffled, and only a few count as sorted". That comparison is yours and it is a good one. It stops working in one place: cards do not have energy, and in physics the count is over arrangements that share the same total energy.

**Where you are still unsure.** Why entropy can go down in one place (a refrigerator, a living cell) without breaking the rule. You suspect the answer has to do with "somewhere else", which is right, and you have not yet worked through an example.

**How sure this is.** The physics is settled textbook material. What I have recorded about your understanding rests on two conversations and your own sentence above.
