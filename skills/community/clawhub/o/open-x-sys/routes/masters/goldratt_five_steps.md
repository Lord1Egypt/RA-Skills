# Goldratt's five focusing steps

> **Master route.** Identify the constraint → exploit it → subordinate everything to it → elevate it → repeat.
> **Run it with**: "Run Goldratt's five steps on ___." · "What is the constraint in ___, and what do we do about it?"
> **Fits**: anything with a goal and a throughput: a production line, a sales pipeline, a content schedule, a hiring process, a development team, one person's week.
> **Source**: Eliyahu Goldratt, *The Goal* (1984), and his later explicit statements of the five focusing steps.

## The five steps, in their own order

| # | Step | What it asks | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identify** the constraint | What one thing sets the pace of the whole? | Improving everything a little |
| 2 | **Exploit** it | How do we get the most from the constraint as it is, without spending? | Buying capacity before using what exists |
| 3 | **Subordinate** everything else to it | How should every other part work so that the constraint is never idle and never swamped? | Each part running at its own maximum |
| 4 | **Elevate** it | If it still limits us, how do we raise its capacity? | Investing first, in the wrong place |
| 5 | **Repeat** | The constraint has moved. Where is it now? | Running by rules built for the old constraint |

The order matters: steps 2 and 3 cost little and often release more than expected, so step 4, which costs money, comes after them.

## As OpenXSys methods

| Step | Method | How it is used here |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | **Bottleneck** | State the goal, then find the one constraint, with evidence: where work waits, what is always busy |
| Exploit, subordinate | **Best Practice** | Change how the constraint is used and how everything else is scheduled around it, for this system's conditions |
| Elevate | **Build** | Add real capacity at the constraint: a tool, a skill, a person, a capability |
| Repeat | **Review** | Check the result against the goal, find where the constraint went, and retire rules made for the old one |

## What a good run looks like

- The goal is stated in a measurable form before anything else.
- One constraint is named, with the evidence that distinguishes it from the other candidates.
- **Exploit** is concrete: the constraint does only work that only it can do; it never waits for input; nothing defective reaches it; its time is not spent on low-value work.
- **Subordinate** changes what the non-constraints do, and this is usually the hardest step to accept: parts that could go faster are asked to work at the constraint's pace, and their local efficiency measures are set aside.
- **Elevate** is proposed only if the first two were not enough, and with its cost.
- It predicts where the constraint will move, and checks whether the constraint is a policy, a habit or the market, because a different kind of constraint needs different work.
- When the constraint is a person, the route is applied with care: the aim is to protect that person's time, not to press them harder.

## Where OpenXSys simplifies

- *The Goal* is a novel about a manufacturing plant, in which the steps appear through the story; Goldratt set them out explicitly in later writing. The Theory of Constraints also includes its own accounting measures and scheduling methods, which this route does not use.
- The route is applied to any system with a goal. In knowledge work the "constraint" is often attention or a decision, and "inventory" is unfinished work; these are analogies with real force and real limits, and the run says where they hold.
- **Review** stands for the fifth step and adds a comparison with the original intent.
