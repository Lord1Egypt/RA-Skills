# Feynman's technique

> **Master route.** Explain it simply → find the gaps → go back to the source → simplify again.
> **Run it with**: "Use Feynman's technique on ___." · "Help me understand ___ well enough to explain it to anyone."
> **Fits**: learning a concept, a theory, a technology or a field; preparing to teach or present; checking whether an understanding is real.
> **Source**: named after Richard Feynman, whose lectures and teaching are a model of explaining difficult ideas plainly. See "Where OpenXSys simplifies" for what this attribution does and does not mean.

## The four steps, in their own order

| # | Step | What it asks | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Explain it simply** | Can I explain this, in plain words, to someone new to it? | Mistaking familiarity with the words for understanding |
| 2 | **Find the gaps** | Where did my explanation go vague, lean on a term I cannot unpack, or skip a step? | Covering a hole with vocabulary |
| 3 | **Go back to the source** | What do the original material and the evidence actually say about exactly that point? | Repairing a gap with a guess |
| 4 | **Simplify again** | Can I now explain it more simply and more truly? | Stopping at "I looked it up" |

## As OpenXSys methods

| Step | Method | How it is used here |
|---|---|---|
| Explain it simply | **Make** (a simple version) | A first plain explanation, short, with no jargon |
| Find the gaps | **X-ray** | Look beneath the explanation: where does it only describe, and where does it explain why? |
| Go back to the source | **Trace** | Return to the primary sources and to how the idea was originally established |
| Simplify again | **Make** | The final deliverable: simple, and correct at the points where the first version was not |

## What a good run looks like

- In learn mode, or whenever the person wants it, **the person makes the first explanation**. The run then helps find the gaps. This is the technique as it is meant to be used, and the person's explanation is strong evidence for the second record of understanding.
- Otherwise the run produces the first simple version itself and criticizes it honestly: here it says "energy flows" without saying how, here an analogy is carrying an argument it cannot bear.
- The gaps are specific. "I do not really know why the second step follows" is a gap. "It could be deeper" is not.
- Step 3 goes to primary sources: the original paper, the textbook derivation, the data. The repair is made from them.
- The final explanation is simpler than the first and more accurate. It uses an analogy only where the structure really corresponds, and says where the analogy stops.
- Simple does not mean false. Where a simplification leaves something out, the explanation says that something was left out.
- It ends with a teach-back from the person where possible, and an exercise: explain it to someone, or apply it to a case of their own.

## Where OpenXSys simplifies

- **The four-step "Feynman technique" is a later popularization.** It is named for the way Feynman learned and taught, as shown in his published lectures and in accounts of his teaching. He did not publish these four steps as a method, and OpenXSys does not present them as his own formulation.
- The widely repeated saying that if you cannot explain something simply you do not understand it has no verified source in Feynman's writing and is attributed to several people. OpenXSys uses the idea and does not attribute the sentence.
- The mapping to **X-ray** and **Trace** is OpenXSys's own.
