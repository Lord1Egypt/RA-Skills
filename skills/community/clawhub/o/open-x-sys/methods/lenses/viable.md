# Viability

> `viable(x)` · Advanced lens
> **Prompt**: "What keeps ___ alive, and what is missing?"
> **Use it when**: an organization, a team, a project or a one-person business keeps running into the same organizational trouble; something works day to day and has no future; growth has made things chaotic; a part acts against the whole.

## What it shows

For something to keep existing in a changing environment, certain functions have to be performed by someone, whatever the organization chart says. Viability checks whether each is present, who or what performs it, and how well they are connected.

| Function | It answers | Sign that it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Operations** | What does the thing actually do for its environment? | Much activity, no clear output |
| **Coordination** | How do the operating parts avoid getting in each other's way? | Collisions, duplicated work, oscillation |
| **Management of the present** | Who allocates resources and checks that the whole performs, including by looking directly? | Parts optimize themselves; nobody knows what really happens |
| **Attention to the outside and the future** | Who looks at what is changing and what is coming? | Efficient at yesterday's task; surprised by the obvious |
| **Identity and purpose** | Who decides what the thing is for, and balances today against tomorrow? | Constant swings between firefighting and reinvention |

The same five functions recur inside each operating part: a viable whole is made of viable parts.

## What a good use looks like

- It identifies who or what performs each function in practice, with evidence, and not which box carries the title.
- It finds the missing or weak function, which is usually the reason for a trouble that keeps being blamed on individuals.
- It checks the balance between managing the present and attending to the future, the two that most often crowd each other out.
- It applies to very small systems too. A freelancer performs all five; the one neglected is usually the fourth.
- It looks at the channels: does information about what is really happening reach the function that needs it, in time?

## Where it goes wrong

- Used to redraw the organization chart.
- Treating the functions as departments.
- Finding everything "missing" without saying which gap matters now.

## It works with

Viability deepens **X-ray** for organizations and `open(me)` for a person's working life. It tests a **Design** and pairs with **Variety** (can each function cope with what it faces?). A missing function is frequently the **Bottleneck**.

## Teach-back

"This was Viability: anything that lasts needs someone doing the work, someone keeping the parts from colliding, someone running today, someone watching tomorrow, and someone deciding what it is all for. Find which of the five nobody is doing."

## The thinking behind it

- **Stafford Beer**, *Brain of the Firm* (1972): the Viable System Model, five interacting subsystems that any organization capable of independent existence must have, repeated at every level.

OpenXSys simplifies: Beer's model is detailed and recursive, with specific channels including direct audit; this lens uses its five functions as questions.
