# Age-Band Doctrine — the developmental logic

## Band 1–2 (toddler)
- **Duration 60 min, morning only (9:30–10:30).** Afternoon collides with nap; the birthday child will not compensate.
- **Guests ≈ age in years.** A 2-year-old's party is a family-and-godparents event with 1–2 peer guests. The script enforces the cap.
- **No games.** Free play, bubbles, balloons kept ON the floor (flying balloons get bitten → pop → choke hazard for toddler siblings present).
- Caregiver per child stays. Smash cake is the centerpiece. Photos before food.
- Favors: a board book or bubble tube — nothing with parts under ~4 cm.

## Band 3–5 (preschool)
- **90 minutes is the wall.** At minute ~60 attention collapses into hunger — that's why food sits mid-party, not at the end.
- **Parallel-play stations beat group games.** 3 stations × 10 min rotation (playdough, beanbag toss, simple craft) + ONE short group moment (parachute, freeze dance, bubbles).
- **Elimination games are banned**: musical chairs at 4 = five crying children. Team/everyone-wins variants only.
- **Caregivers stay** (1 adult : 2–3 kids). Count adult chairs, serve coffee at morning parties — the adults are your supervision plan.
- Schedule: 10:00–11:30 or 15:30–17:00. Never 12:30.

## Band 6–8 (early elementary)
- **120 minutes.** Organized games finally work: relays, treasure hunts, obstacle courses. 2–3 games max + one calm craft station as a decompressor.
- **Competition OK as teams.** Individual elimination still produces tears at 6; by 8 they can take it — know your group.
- Drop-off begins (~half the parents will). 2 adult hosts per 10 kids.
- **Do not open presents live:** envy chaos, viral-video risk, and no 7-year-old can perform gracious gratitude for 40 minutes. Open at home; do a group "THANK YOU [name]!" cheer per gift-giver instead.

## Band 9–12 (tween)
- **150 minutes; the marquee activity IS the party.** Trampoline park, laser tag, NERF arena, movie, pottery workshop. Stacking 5 party games on top of a marquee activity is the classic over-programming error — unstructured hangout time is the feature, not a bug.
- Drop-off standard. Host visible but not leading. Music they pick (screen the playlist).
- **Named seating matters**: cliques are real; an accidental enemy pairing poisons 30 minutes.
- Ask allergies at RSVP; tweens have strong preferences and some have epi-pens.

## Band 13+ (teen)
- **~3 hours, evening OK.** Food + music + one anchor. Budget shifts heavily to food (teenagers eat like adults squared).
- Parents absent by design; ONE adult on-premise, low-visibility. That adult still counts: liability, gatekeeping crashes, alcohol-adjacent risks at 16+.
- Dim lighting will be requested — comply. A photo wall replaces organized photos.

## Cross-band rules
- **Show-rate math:** class-list invites get 70–85% yes; close-friend invites 90–100%. Siblings tag along +15%. Plan consumables for expected+2, confirm at the RSVP deadline.
- **Home capacity:** activity parties need ~2 m² of clear floor per child. 15 kids in a 20 m² living room doesn't work no matter how good the games are.
- **Present-opening:** deferred to home under 8; from 9+, optional by the child's own preference — ask them.
- **Allergies collected at RSVP** and labeled on the table. Nut-free default for shared candy.
