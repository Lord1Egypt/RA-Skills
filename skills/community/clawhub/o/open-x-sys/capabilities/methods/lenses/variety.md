# Variety

> `variety(x)` · Advanced lens
> **Prompt**: "Can ___ handle the variety it faces?"
> **Use it when**: a process works for the normal case and breaks on exceptions; a small team serves a very diverse set of demands; rules multiply and still do not cover what happens; an automation fails on real inputs.

## What it shows

A system that has to keep something under control must be able to respond in at least as many ways as there are different situations that can disturb it. If the situations outnumber the responses, some of them will get through. Variety compares the two sides: how many different things the system faces, and how many different things it can do about them.

When the first exceeds the second, there are only two honest remedies:

- **Reduce the variety that arrives**: narrow what is accepted, standardize inputs, segment, filter, say no.
- **Increase the variety of response**: add options, skills, discretion, tools, or people who can decide on the spot.

Hoping, working harder and adding a rule for each new case are not remedies.

## What a good use looks like

- It describes the real range of situations, including the rare ones, from evidence: the actual tickets, orders, inputs, requests.
- It describes the real range of responses available to whoever must respond, including what they are permitted to decide.
- It locates the mismatch precisely: which kinds of case are not being handled, and where they end up.
- It weighs the two remedies for this system, with their costs. Reducing incoming variety gives up some customers or cases; increasing response variety costs skill, autonomy or money.
- It notices where variety is being absorbed invisibly: by one experienced person, by overtime, by the customer.
- For an automation or an AI workflow, it asks what the workflow will meet that its design did not consider, and what happens to those cases.

## Where it goes wrong

- A count of rules mistaken for capacity to respond.
- Variety pushed onto the customer or the front line and then declared solved.
- Control tightened at the centre, which lowers the variety of response exactly where it is needed.

## It works with

Variety tests a **Build** or a **Design** before real use, explains many findings of **Bottleneck** (the constraint is where variety piles up) and pairs with **Viability**. It informs **Best Practice**: a practice fits only if it can cope with the range this person actually faces.

## Teach-back

"This was Variety: count the kinds of situation that can arrive and the kinds of response you have. If the first number is larger, either let fewer kinds in or become able to do more kinds of thing. There is no third way."

## The thinking behind it

- **W. Ross Ashby**, *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956): the law of requisite variety, by which only variety in a regulator can absorb variety in what it regulates.

OpenXSys simplifies: Ashby states the law formally in terms of the number of distinguishable states; this lens applies it qualitatively.
