# Modification Costs, Dimensions & Funding

## Tier-3 projects (plan & budget)
| Project | Typical US cost 2026 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Walk-in / curbless shower conversion | $6k–12k | Curbless needs floor recess; cheapest done during any bathroom reno |
| Stair lift (straight run) | $3k–5k | Curved runs $8k–15k; rental/used market exists |
| First-floor bedroom conversion | $8k–25k | Den/dining-room conversion + half-bath is the usual play |
| Doorway widening to 32″ clear | $800–2,500/door | Load-bearing walls add structural engineering |
| wheelchair ramp (wood, per 8′ run) | $1.5k–3k | 1:12 slope rule: 12″ of rise needs 12′ of run |
| Walk-in tub | $3k–8k | Long fill/drain times frustrate some users; chair+handheld often serves better for less |
| Comfort-height toilet swap | $300–600 installed | 17–19″ bowl |
| Stair rail second side | $80–250 | carpenter, half-day |

## Clearances (ADA-derived, private-home guidance)
- Walker passage: ≥ 610 mm (24″) clear; wheelchair: ≥ 815 mm (32″) doors, 1500 mm (60″) turning circle.
- Grab bar mounting: 33–36″ AFF; toilet side bar 39–40″ AFF.
- Ramp slope 1:12 max (1:16 gentler); handrails if rise >6″ or run >72″.
- Rocker light switches at 48″ max; lever door handles (arthritis-friendly) — $20/door, Tier-1.5.

## Funding routes (US)
- **Medicare**: does NOT pay for home modifications as such; DOES cover OT assessment and DME (raised toilet seat, shower chair, walker) with a doctor's order. Grab bars sometimes via Medicare Advantage supplemental benefits (check the plan's "home support" allowance, often $500–2,000/yr).
- **Medicaid HCBS waivers / PACE**: modifications up to ~$1k–15k by state; needs eligibility + care-plan documentation.
- **VA**: Home Improvements and Structural Alterations (HASA) up to $6,800 (special home adaptation much higher for service-connected); VASH for homeless-prevention cases.
- **Area Agency on Aging**: free assessments, minor-repair grants ($300–5,000 typical), chore services.
- **Rebuilding Together / Habitat critical repair**: volunteer labor, income-qualified.
- **Tax**: medical-expense deduction for medically-necessary modifications exceeding 7.5% AGI (needs doctor's letter).

## Renter rights (US)
Fair Housing Act: a disabled tenant may make *reasonable modifications* at their own expense (landlord can require restoration agreement; must grant permission; can't charge a modification deposit in most jurisdictions). Grab bars, ramps, lever handles qualify. Section 504 covers federally-assisted housing (landlord pays). Document the request in writing.

## The honest priority order (evidence-weighted)
1. Fix the night path + rugs + grab bars (weekend, <$150) — targets the most frequent mechanisms.
2. Get the med/balance review — targets the *cause*, not the environment.
3. Stairs strategy — rails now, first-floor/stair-lift plan documented.
4. Big-ticket wet-room conversions — only when (1)–(3) are done and the person's trajectory still points home.
