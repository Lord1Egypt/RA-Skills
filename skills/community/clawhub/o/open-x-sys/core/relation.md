# Cooperation Between Systems

A good connection lets independently maintained systems help one another on a real problem. It may begin with exchanged understanding, a tool call, a shared change in reality, or a result that does not agree. Each party retains its own state, permissions, and internal way of working. A higher-level system forms through relations that can actually produce effects.

## A relation is a first-class system

An important relation is itself a system that can observe, transform, act, confirm, and feed results back. Expand the arrow along one real effect:

```text
source system → receiving system
what difference, information, resource, or action is transferred
how the receiver interprets, transforms, or rejects it
under what conditions and for what span of time it is valid
who owns state, materials, and decision rights
what is expected to change in the receiver
how acceptance, execution, state, and effect are separately confirmed
where failure, delay, or distortion returns
how the relation can be corrected, paused, replaced, withdrawn, or exited
```

These are design semantics, not a form to generate every time. A one-off, low-risk handoff may fit in one natural-language sentence. A relation that crosses ownership boundaries, repeats, or carries a high cost of error needs more reliable sources, interfaces, versions, permissions, and feedback.

## Gaps and bridge systems

A gap exists when a difference, understanding, or capability held by one system cannot produce a usable effect in another. Typical causes include invisibility, incompatible meaning, mismatched timing, missing authority, no execution path, no confirmation, or feedback that cannot reach the party able to change the next action.

When bridging a gap, first ask whether observation, translation, action, coordination, or feedback is missing. Then organize a bridge system. It may contain a natural-language agreement, people, AI, existing tools, generated code, shared data, or a new division of work. The bridge has its own purpose, state, boundary, capabilities, and failure conditions; it can be replaced or continue to evolve.

| Relation needed | What the parties actually provide |
| --- | --- |
| **Discovery** | What the other party is, what observations or capabilities it provides, who maintains it, and whether it is currently reachable. |
| **Understanding** | Observations or explanations needed for the current problem, with sources, scale, conditions, and a way to check them. Explanations serving different purposes may coexist. |
| **Invocation** | Who makes what request, which system executes it, what authority applies, and how acceptance and effect are confirmed. |
| **Coordination** | A shared purpose, division of work, treatment of waiting and refusal, and actual results visible to both sides. |
| **Co-evolution** | Each party can see the consequences of a trial. When an important assumption fails, authorized parties can modify, replace, or stop the relation. |

## Give information to the party that can act on it

Additional neighborhood demand may matter to transit operations, yet it affects a line schedule, actual passenger routes, and cross-line coordination differently. Before sending it, determine whether the receiver has enough context, authority, and processing capability. Confirm the receiver's review, execution, and state change with whatever evidence the relevant system can return. When old data or failed delivery constrains a judgment or action, record that constraint. Reusing another party's explanation also requires knowing what new evidence would invalidate it.

### A natural-language connection agreement

> The transfer system calculates waiting from arrival and departure times provided by operations for the same stops and observation window. Operations confirms the time records; the transfer side explains which journeys it selected. Once schedule data passes its validity date, the result is marked for verification. Dispatch retains decision rights over schedule changes. After a change, actual arrivals and departures are used to test the original explanation again.

This agreement already gives an AI enough semantics to judge. A long-lived relation that carries repeated change may add an agreed data entry, execution tool, or notification channel. The host governs tool permissions. When a domain format or maintainer changes, users can locate affected connections and unfinished work.

## When purposes differ

Local on-time performance and the smoothest end-to-end journey can be different purposes. Keep each metric and observation range, then explain at a higher level how they jointly affect the result. Before a real change, put costs, viable choices, and effects on each party before the person with decision rights. A system may refuse a request; refusal and delay are also evidence about the whole. Choosing a better boundary or temporarily reducing scope can also let cooperation keep producing value.

## How a higher-level system comes into existence

A higher-level system exists when several systems produce real effects around a shared purpose, their relations can execute and receive feedback, and the whole develops a capability that none of its subsystems has alone.

The higher level preserves each subsystem's ownership, authoritative state, internal freedom, and right to exit. The shared layer maintains the semantics, sources, conditions, authority, verification, and feedback required for real cooperation. When a subsystem changes, locate the affected relations and whole-system capabilities, then send the relevant change to the participants that truly need it.
