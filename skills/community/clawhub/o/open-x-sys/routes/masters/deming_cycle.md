# Deming's cycle

> **Master route.** Plan → do → study → act.
> **Run it with**: "Run a Deming cycle on ___." · "Let's test ___ properly before we commit."
> **Fits**: any change whose effect is uncertain: a new practice, a new tool, a new schedule, a new way of selling, teaching or writing. It is the standard way to try a practice before adopting it.
> **Source**: W. Edwards Deming, *Out of the Crisis* (1986), building on Walter Shewhart.

## The four steps, in their own order

| # | Step | What it asks | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plan** | What change are we trying, what do we predict it will do, and how will we know? | Trying things with no way to tell whether they worked |
| 2 | **Do** | Carry it out, preferably on a small scale | Betting everything on an untested idea |
| 3 | **Study** | What happened, compared with the prediction? What did we learn? | Seeing what one hoped to see |
| 4 | **Act** | Adopt the change, adapt it, or abandon it. Then plan the next cycle | A test that changes nothing |

The prediction made in step 1 is what makes step 3 possible. Without it, any result can be explained afterwards.

## As OpenXSys methods

| Step | Method | How it is used here |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | **Best Practice** | Choose the change that fits this system, state the prediction and the measure, and design the cheap first test |
| Do | **Build** (as a pilot) | Make the smallest working version and run it on real work |
| Study, act | **Review** | Compare result with prediction, separate judgment from luck, then adopt, adapt or abandon, and update the practice record |

## What a good run looks like

- **Plan** writes the prediction down, with a number or an observable event and a date, in the person's system, before anything is tried.
- The measure is chosen in advance, and so is how the data will be collected, even if that is a note the person makes each day.
- **Do** is small: one week, one client, one episode, one team. The scale is chosen so that failure is cheap and the result still means something.
- What actually happened during the trial is noted, including surprises and things that had to be changed along the way.
- **Study** looks at the data before looking for an explanation, and compares it with the prediction and not with a general hope.
- It considers ordinary variation: one good week proves little. Where there are too few observations to tell, it says so and extends the trial.
- **Act** is a real decision with three possible outcomes, and the practice record is changed accordingly.
- The next cycle is named. The route is a wheel, and one turn is rarely the end.
- When the system comes back and a trial's date has passed, it asks for the result (see `system/return.md`).

## Where OpenXSys simplifies

- Deming used the word **study**, and preferred it to "check", because the step is about learning from the comparison and not only inspecting. The cycle is also widely known as PDCA.
- In *Out of the Crisis* Deming calls this the Shewhart cycle; he later expressed it as the PDSA cycle. This page uses the four words given in the design document.
- Deming's work is a whole philosophy of management, including the understanding of variation and of the system in which people work. This route takes only the cycle, and one idea from the rest: do not read meaning into ordinary variation.
- A person testing a change on their own work has few observations. The route says what can and cannot be concluded from them.
