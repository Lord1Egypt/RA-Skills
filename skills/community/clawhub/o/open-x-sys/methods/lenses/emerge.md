# Emergence

> `emerge(x)` · Advanced lens
> **Prompt**: "How did ___ arise without anyone designing it?"
> **Use it when**: a pattern exists that nobody planned (a market price, a traffic jam, a culture, a neighbourhood, a convention); people look for the person responsible and there is none; a top-down fix keeps failing against something that keeps re-forming.

## What it shows

Some orders are designed. Others arise from many participants each following local rules and responding to one another, with no one in charge of the result. The result can be stable, intricate and useful, or stable, intricate and harmful, and in either case it cannot be changed by instructing it, because nobody is holding it. Emergence explains such a pattern from below.

- **The participants**, and how they differ from one another.
- **The local rules** each follows: what each responds to, and what each can see.
- **The interactions**: how one participant's action changes the situation of the others.
- **The resulting pattern** at the level of the whole, which appears in no single rule.
- **What sustains it**, and what would make it re-form if it were removed.

## What a good use looks like

- It derives the pattern from the rules and interactions, step by step, so that the reader can see it has to appear. Calling something "emergent" explains nothing by itself.
- It shows that no participant intends or controls the whole, with evidence.
- It keeps the two levels apart: what individuals do and what the whole does are different descriptions, and each is true at its own level.
- It finds what the pattern depends on (the rule, the information, the incentive, the density) and therefore where it can be influenced: by changing what participants see or respond to, not by ordering the pattern to change.
- Where the dynamics are the insight, it builds a small simulation so that the person can watch the pattern form.

## Where it goes wrong

- "It emerged" used as an answer.
- A pattern that someone did design, described as emergent.
- A search for the culprit behind a pattern that has none.
- A simulation that looks lifelike and was never compared with the real thing.

## It works with

Emergence deepens **Loops** and **Trace** (how the pattern formed and persisted) and corrects **Incentives** when no actor is choosing the outcome. It tells **Leverage** where influence is possible: on rules and information, not on the result. Several entries in `patterns/` are emergent patterns.

## Teach-back

"This was Emergence: when a pattern has no author, look at what each participant responds to and how their actions change things for the others. To change the pattern, change what they respond to. Commanding the pattern itself does nothing."

## The thinking behind it

- **John H. Holland**, *Hidden Order: How Adaptation Builds Complexity* (1995): complex adaptive systems, in which many interacting agents that adapt to one another produce organized behavior of the whole.

OpenXSys simplifies: Holland describes specific properties and mechanisms of such systems; this lens uses the central explanation, from local rules to global pattern.
