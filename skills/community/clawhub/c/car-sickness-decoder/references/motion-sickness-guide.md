# Motion Sickness: Mechanism, Classification, Protocols, Dosing

Reference for `scripts/carsick.py`. Educational content for caregivers; dosing
lines follow published OTC label bands — a pediatrician owns actual medical
decisions.

## 1. Mechanism (why the countermeasures work)

Sensory-conflict model: the vestibular system (inner ear) registers
acceleration and turning while the eyes report a stationary cabin. The brain
receives an inconsistency it treats like a neurotoxin signal → nausea, pallor,
cold sweat, salivation. Consequences:

- **Forward gaze at the horizon** re-syncs the visual and vestibular channels
  (conflict resolved).
- **Driver never gets sick** — the driver's brain predicts motion from
  steering input; passengers suffer. Corollary: let a prone passenger
  "co-pilot" (watch the road, call turns).
- **Sleep = immunity** (vestibular input is gated during sleep).
- **Smooth driving wins**: late braking, jerky lane changes, and hard cornering
  are the inputs the conflict model hates most.
- **Reading/screens maximize conflict** (fixed near-field image + moving
  world). Audio-only entertainment is fine.

## 2. Epidemiology and natural history

- Peak prevalence ages 2–12 (~9 y worst); infants nearly immune (vestibular
  system immature); many outgrow it in adolescence.
- ~30–60% of children experience it at least occasionally; a subset (~5–10%)
  is severe.
- Hormonal factors, migraine history, and anxiety raise susceptibility.

## 3. Pattern classification (what triage separates)

### Classic kinetosis
Trigger = motion; symptoms = nausea/pallor/sweating/drooling; resolves
15–30 min after motion stops; never off-vehicles. Management = protocol
(seating/gaze/cadence/food) ± pre-trip antihistamine.

### Vestibular migraine (common in school-age kids)
Episodes may include: throbbing headache, photo/phonophobia, spinning vertigo
(sometimes at rest), pallor + vomiting, family history of migraine. Motion is
a trigger but not the only one. Management = pediatrician (diet/sleep
hygiene, sometimes prophylaxis); behavioral protocol still reduces load.

### Conditioned / anticipatory
Nausea begins before travel (at packing, in the driveway), tied to one bad
past episode or caregiver modeling. Management = calm framing, graded
exposure (short pleasant drives to fun destinations), no drama at cleanup;
meds usually unnecessary and can cement the "I'm a sick passenger" identity.

### Red flags → medical, same-day/urgent
- Vertigo or imbalance BETWEEN episodes (walking into doorframes)
- Hearing loss, tinnitus, or ear fullness
- New severe headache pattern, or headache always with episodes
- Vomiting > 2 h, signs of dehydration
- Post-head-injury onset
- First episode in adulthood with neurological signs

## 4. Trip protocol (evidence-graded)

| Measure | Grade | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Forward gaze / horizon | strong | The core fix |
| Front-most seat / center rear for kids | strong | Least head roll, best view |
| Stop every 45–60 min, walk 5 min | strong | Resets the vestibular state |
| No reading/screens | strong | Removes the conflict source |
| Sleep timing (drive at nap/night) | strong | Immunity while asleep |
| Light carb pre-meal 30–60 min | moderate | Empty-but-not stomach; avoid greasy/acidic |
| Cool airflow at face | moderate | Symptom relief |
| Ginger 0.5–1 g pre-trip | moderate | Best-studied non-drug option |
| Acupressure (P6/Neiguan band) | weak | Harmless; some trials positive |
| Lemon/citrus smell, sour candy | weak | Symptom modulation |
| Driver announcing turns, smooth speed | strong | Input predictability |

## 5. OTC pharmacology (label-band dosing)

### Dimenhydrinate (Dramamine; = diphenhydramine + chlorotheophyllinate)
- 2–6 y: 12.5–25 mg per dose, q6–8h, max 75 mg/day
- 6–12 y: 25–50 mg per dose, q6–8h, max 150 mg/day
- ≥12 y/adult: 50–100 mg per dose, q4–6h, max 400 mg/day
- Timing: **30–60 min before departure**; duration 4–6 h (short-acting — plan
  the second dose for long drives). Main side effect: drowsiness.

### Diphenhydramine (Benadryl)
Equivalent anti-motion effect bands (12.5/25/50 mg); more sedating per mg;
same pre-trip timing.

### Meclizine (Bonine; ≥12 y label)
25–50 mg once daily, less sedating, 24 h duration — good for all-day trips
in teens/adults; NOT labeled for young children.

### Scopolamine patch
Adults (≥18 label); 4 h onset — apply the night before; discuss with a
doctor; side effects (dry mouth, blurred near vision, withdrawal dizziness).

### Hard gates
- **< 2 years: no motion-sickness antihistamines** (label + pediatric
  consensus). Non-drug protocol only; frequent episodes at this age → doctor.
- Don't stack with other antihistamines/cold formulas (duplicate
  anticholinergic load).
- Regular/weekly use → pediatrician conversation (vestibular migraine workup,
  dosing plan).
- Anti-nausea agents like ondansetron do NOT prevent motion sickness and
  mask it instead — not a road-trip tool without a doctor.

## 6. Mid-episode rescue (what actually works)

Once nausea is rolling: stop the car safely, walk 5 minutes, cool air on the
face, small sips of cold water or ginger ale, sour candy. Oral meds will
likely not stay down — don't chase with pills. If the child falls asleep,
that's the finish line. Cleanup without commentary (conditioning risk).
