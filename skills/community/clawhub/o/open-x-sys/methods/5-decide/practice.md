# Best Practice

> `practice(system, goal)` · Stage 5 · Decide · **starter method**
> **Prompt**: "Given what I have, what is the best way to ___?"
> **Also heard as**: "How should I do this?" · "What do the pros do?" · "What is the right setup for me?"
> **Produces**: fitting practices compared on seven dimensions, a recommended path, and a cheap first test.

## What it is for

"What is the best way?" has no answer in general and a good answer in particular. Best Practice finds the way of doing something that fits this system: its state, its resources, its constraints and its goal. It researches what exists, studies under what conditions each option works, adapts or combines them, and recommends one path with what it costs and a small trial that would show whether it works here. It is the method that turns understanding into a way of acting.

The engine underneath it, with the full treatment, is `engines/practice.md`.

## What it produces

- **The capability gap**, stated precisely: what the system can do now against what the goal requires.
- **Candidate practices** from real sources: research, mature products, open source, professional communities, real cases, and what the person already does.
- **A comparison** on the dimensions where the candidates actually differ: fit, value, cost, evidence, system impact, what comes next, switching conditions.
- **A recommended path**, and why it fits these conditions.
- **A cheap first test**, with an observable result and a date.
- **Switching conditions**: the signals to keep, adjust or replace.
- **A practice record** in the person's system, once the path is taken.

## What a good use looks like

- The person's conditions come first, and they are real ones: hours available, budget, skills, tools already owned, tolerance for rework, the audience or customer. The system reads them from the person's system and material before asking.
- It finds how practitioners actually work, including where they failed, and does not stop at what articles recommend.
- Each candidate's mechanism is understood: why it works, and what must be true for it to work.
- **Two different people get two different answers.** If the recommendation would be the same for anyone, the person's conditions were not used.
- The evidence is graded honestly: measured here, measured somewhere comparable, reported by practitioners, argued from mechanism, or the system's own reasoning.
- It gives one recommendation, with the alternative that would win under different conditions.
- The first test is small enough to run this week and tells something real.
- When nothing existing fits, it combines or designs a practice and says why the parts are joined that way.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Generic advice** with no conditions and no cost.
- **A survey** of ten options and no judgment.
- **The most impressive tool** recommended to someone it does not fit.
- **No cheap test**, so the person pays in full to find out.
- **A script.** A practice stops at principles and the few decisions that determine success.
- **No return.** A recommendation never compared with what happened.

## It works with

Best Practice follows **Leverage** or **Bottleneck** (which say where to act) and leads to **Build** (making the practice real) and **Review** (judging it by results). It is central to *Improve how I work*, *Open my system* and *Solve a recurring problem*, and it appears in Musk's algorithm, the Toyota way, Goldratt's steps and Deming's cycle.

## Teach-back

"This was Best Practice: do not ask what is best. Ask what is best for a system like yours, with what you have. Find what others do, learn when it works, fit it to your conditions, and try the smallest version before paying for the whole."

## The thinking behind it

- **Herbert A. Simon**, *The Sciences of the Artificial* (1969): design is finding courses of action that change existing situations into preferred ones, under limits of knowledge and resources that make a satisfactory solution, found by search, the realistic aim.
- **W. Edwards Deming**, *Out of the Crisis* (1986): improvement by planned trials on a small scale, studied before being adopted.
- **Taiichi Ohno**, *Toyota Production System* (1978): the standard is the best way known today, and exists to be improved.

OpenXSys simplifies: it joins these into one stance, that a best practice is conditional, tested and temporary.
