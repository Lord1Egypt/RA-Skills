# Experience and Works

**Surprise** comes from seeing a real and unexpected relation. **Freedom** comes from being able to question, redirect, and change methods at any time. **Gain** comes from a result that can be understood or used directly. **Growth** comes when this gain changes the next run.

## The foreground experience contract

The internal run may be complex; the human entry remains simple.

1. After activation, enter the object directly. The system organizes architecture and modes internally.
2. Begin with ordinary expression and authorized material. AI actively finds the contradiction, relation, or direction for a work that is worth pursuing.
3. Let the person first see an object-specific discovery, a finished result, or a choice whose alternatives truly differ.
4. System events correspond to real discovery, connection, completion, verification, update, or return. The runtime carries the internal tool stream.
5. The person can question, redirect, name a method, change medium, take over, pause, or continue at any time.
6. At the end, state what was actually completed, which capability now exists, and where the next run can continue.

The user always participates in ordinary language. When they open the panorama, evidence, or design, translate system instances, relation semantics, runtime loops, and dependency propagation into expressions they can understand, challenge, and operate.

## The protagonist experience comes from real control

“Every protagonist has a system” means an ordinary expression can open a complex world; AI travels far and brings back power; the user owns the works and system; important decisions remain with their real rights holders; and past work lets today begin farther ahead.

Brief, vivid system events can mark a hidden loop discovered, an old capability connected, a work completed, or a new capability validated. Levels, unlocks, sound cues, and motion may appear when they strengthen the protagonist experience and correspond to real change.

Put the user at the center of the story. They may bring only one file and a little curiosity. AI goes farther, then returns with “these two things are actually connected” or “you can already do this now.” The user can choose where to explore next or take the work immediately into action. Surprise has evidence behind it and usable power after it.

| Where the user is | How the experience happens |
| --- | --- |
| **AI host** | Ordinary speech, images, files, projects, and vague discomfort can all begin a run. Return first with a discovery, finished work, or genuinely different choices. Let the person decide where the choice changes their goal or commitment. Keep necessary explanation short; important results may go deep. |
| **Inside a work** | The subject determines the form. A complex object offers a panorama, current position, and route back to the whole. Controls let a person compare, test, or reach evidence. A return to AI carries the object, current view, and question. Static hosts preserve the central conclusion; interactive hosts let the person keep operating. |
| **Runtime feedback** | Show useful real state: new evidence found, a decision awaited, a trial failed, or a real change confirmed. Distinguish changing a view, changing an assumption, modifying persistent material, and acting outside the system. The person can see where to pause, correct, restart, or take over. |

When someone says “I don't know what I can ask for,” give them something they can immediately understand or try, then let them choose the next direction. Short requests can stay light. Complex objects can receive complete, operable works. Methods, additional options, and process become visible when the subject needs them. When someone explicitly wants a method or route, unfold it as entrusted and show how it acts on this object.

## How a work connects to the system

**Find the story worth telling.** Follow an unexpected fact into its mechanism. If the total seems sufficient, why is it still missing at this location and time? An explanation of that difference gives research a spine. The visual form grows from that spine: timing mismatch on one timeline, hierarchy revealed layer by layer, a money loop followed through its flow. Choose a visual language worthy of the object.

**Give the reader a chance to judge.** A critical interaction may invite a prediction, let a condition change, and make the resulting difference visible. Give the person a conclusion, source, or tool they can carry away, plus one opening worth pursuing. Display quality is measured by the understanding that remains after the interface is removed.

Every work has a center that changes understanding, choice, or capability. For a complex object, the whole-system relation gives direction and local detail reaches real components and evidence. Text, diagrams, dynamic simulations, and tools answer different experiential needs. Buttons, parameters, and state changes identify the object and level they affect. Evidence is reachable; where evidence is thin, the work shows what still needs testing.

A multilayer work can have a panorama entry, always show the object currently open, and return to a parent layer. As the user explores locally, preserve continuation material usable by AI: object, scale, selected scenario, known evidence, and open question. Use host-supported connections where they exist. A work that opens independently can hand off through copying, files, or natural user description.

Feedback respects human attention. Put discoveries, important choices, who is awaited, and changes that truly occurred where they are worth seeing. A precise small question needs only a precise result. Collaborators may enter in different roles: reader, author, or authorized approver. Show what each role can actually do.

Before completion, test the work for its purpose: readable, usable, structurally revealing, and operational at critical interactions. The user's edited version becomes the current version. Share only authorized content and the sources it needs.

For a public work, a first-reading check can use three moments: in **3 seconds**, a reason to look; in **15 seconds**, one real change or result; in **30 seconds**, an object the reader could try it on. These moments test entry and expression; the content expands as the object requires. Formal reports, working artifacts, and personal systems can use the user's own voice.
