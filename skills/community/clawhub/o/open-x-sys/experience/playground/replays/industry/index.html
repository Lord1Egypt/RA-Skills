0 mSurface · Applications

Applications are closest to the customer, yet receive only $77.3 billion.

Discovery 02Why deeper layers earn more

Cheaper intelligence,
costlier bedrock.

Every model cost reduction sends orders to the physical layer. The price of a unit of intelligence falls, while calls, tasks and infrastructure demand grow faster.

Price per unit of intelligenceAbout 50× cheaperper year, at fixed capability
Global inference tokens14×year on year; about 30 trillion/month in mid-2026
Enterprise AI spending+35%About $407 billion in 2026
A direction rather than an equation: faster price declines bring faster growth in use and new tasks; the latter determine total spending. This is the AI industry's version of the Jevons paradox.

In 1865, Jevons observed that more efficient steam engines increased British coal consumption: cheaper power enabled new uses. AI follows a similar curve. Cheaper tokens enable agents, long contexts, multimodality and real-time interaction, ultimately returning the bill to memory, packaging, power and capital33。

Model efficiency gains therefore redistribute value toward capacity that is hardest to expand. The harder the model layer works, the more memory, packaging and power can earn.

20 mCloud & capital

Cloud providers are AI's bankers.

In 2026, four cloud providers will spend about $730 billion on capex—a sum their own free cash flow can no longer cover.

The four North American cloud providers spent approximately $208.7 billion, up 84.2% year on year; combined full-year guidance is approximately $720–744.5 billion, versus $413 billion in 202512. Including Oracle's FY2027 guidance brings the total to about $835 billion. For 2027, sell-side consensus points to $1.0–1.2 trillion13。

Where does the money come from? Hyperscalers issued approximately $182 billionof bonds in 2025, versus $92 billion in 2024. Alphabet's debt rose from about $16 billion to about $100 billion; in June 2026 it also announced an $80 billion equity raise14. JPMorgan estimates that only about 15% of large AI companies' debt and lease commitments appear on their balance sheets13。

And returns? Capex/EBITDA is approximately 90%in 2026 and approximately 94%in 2027; incremental EBITDA yield on the previous year's capex is only 20–40%13. With GPU lifetimes of 5–6 years, a low-return asset approaches retirement just as it pays back.

The question is who finances AI.Who advances the money, and for how long? Once capex exceeds operating cash flow, interest rates, credit spreads and share prices set the pace alongside demand.

60 mPower & the grid

AI's next wall is electricity.

You can build a data center in 18 months and wait five years for electricity. Power is the one input money cannot supply immediately.

Under IEA definitions, global data-center electricity use in 2024 was 415 TWh(about 1.5% of the global total), about 485 TWh in 2025 and approximately 945 TWhin 2030, approaching Japan's national consumption. AI-optimized servers account for roughly 15% of data-center electricity use, growing about 30% annually22。

US data-center load: about 23 GW in 2023, 31 GW in 2025, 41–42 GW in 2026, 66 GWin 2027 and 134 GW23in 2030. Grid-connection queues exceed 2,060 GWacross about 8,200 projects. The median wait for projects commissioned in 2025 exceeded five years; only 13% of capacity proposed in 2000–2020 was built.

Prices speak: PJM capacity prices rose from $28.92/MW-day to 269.92(+833%), hit the $329.17 ceiling in 2026/27, and reached $325 in 2028/29, with a 6,831 MW shortfall24. Large-transformer lead times are about 128 weeks, step-up transformers about 144. GE Vernova heavy-duty gas turbines are sold out through 2028, with commitments extending to 2031; prices rose about 300% in three years to roughly $600/kW. Around 50% of planned 2026 capacity may be delayed25。

Time is the constraint.Global data-center investment exceeded $550 billion in 2025 (+17%), surpassing upstream oil and gas investment. Power-purchase agreements reached 45 TWh in H1 2026 (+20%). Nuclear agreements cover 9.8 GW, 13 projects and seven buyers, but most supply starts after 2030.
Global data-center investment $550B+ 2025 (+17%), exceeding upstream oil and gas investment; major operators' 2026 capex exceeds $700 billion.
After the November 2026 US midterms 40 / 41 local data-center proposals were rejected. Opponents won 40 of 41 referendums; some elected officials favor moratoriums or limits. The November 5 sell-off priced in this political constraint for the first time26。

Permits also constrain supply. A data center needs land, grid access, air permits, water and local political consent. Each can delay a project; treating electricity only as engineering understates the constraint.

70 mBedrock · The loop

Demand is only part of this machine's fuel.

When suppliers are also shareholders, customers and guarantors, demand signals become interdependent. Circular financing postpones independent validation of demand.

OpenAI has a nominal ceiling of approximately $710 billionin chip and cloud contracts. Nvidia, Microsoft and Amazon are both suppliers and shareholders. Nvidia's proposed $100 billion OpenAI investment was never signed; its actual investment was about $30 billion at an $852 billion valuation. Nvidia also owns about 9% of CoreWeave, commits to buying unsold cloud capacity (initially $6.3 billion), and acts as supplier, shareholder and customer4。

Nvidia guarantees some leases for OpenAI's Ohio data center; AMD reportedly offers equity-for-orders arrangements to OpenAI / Meta. Anthropic's IPO makes the pattern more direct: Google and Nvidia subscribe $10 billion each, AMD $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft $1 billion each, and TSMC $0.5 billion, totaling $26 billion—with suppliers also becoming shareholders7。

These arrangements are not inherently illegal or proof of artificial demand. They finance capacity built ahead of demand. But when supplier and customer money comes from one loop, independent demand validation weakens. Supply commitments of $279B, guarantees of $108.5B and DSO of 60 days are its gauges4。

Minsky's ladder:Build from cash flow → build from the balance sheet → build from valuations. The industry is on the second rung; marginal projects are on the third. The third depends on new financing to service old obligations.

The faultNovember 2026

On November 5, the market's question changed: from “How big can it grow?” to “When does it pay back?”

The pricing framework shifted: capex changed from proof of demand to cash-flow risk; valuation changed from revenue multiples to unit economics.

That day, the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4%, the S&P 500 fell 4.4%and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 5.23% (down 11% intraday). About $2.3 trillionin market value vanished. Nvidia fell 9.3%, AMD 17.3%, Micron 17% and Tesla 10.8%; the Magnificent Seven lost about $1.04 trillion26。

“This is repricing.” Cash-rich giants remain. Marginal projects, off-balance-sheet leverage, high valuations and financing-dependent companies are being reassessed. AnticipateFour futures

Four futures, defined by conditions.

Two variables shape the future: end-demand growth and capital availability. Two variables, four combinations.

Acceleration needs demand to keep beating expectations and willing capital markets. Digestion needs demand to catch supply as capital becomes selective. A bubble has continuing funding but lagging revenue. An air pocket sees capital withdraw before demand proves itself.

These scenarios can form a path: acceleration → digestion → air pocket → bubble → renewed acceleration. The order depends on policy and credit cycles rather than the technical roadmap.

Subjective probabilities:Digestion 40%, acceleration 25%, air pocket 20%, bubble 15%. These prioritize monitoring rather than predict outcomes. Two signals turning together trigger a scenario change.
Capital availability: tight ↓ / loose ↑
End-demand growth: low ← / high →

LeverageWhere to stand

Three positions, three ways to act.

An observation checklist for three positions. Each has one recommendation, one trade-off, and one low-cost first step.

Capital allocators

Remove capex growth from the investment thesis.

Watch return on capital and contracted capacity. Capex growth tells a story; free cash flow and payback tell the accounts. Memory's 80% gross margins are cyclical rents.

First step:Build a six-line monitor: capex/EBITDA, FCF, HBM contract prices, supplier-financing share, credit spreads and PJM prices. Update weekly; record rather than forecast.

Trade-off: you may miss a melt-up, but avoid being passive at the turn.

When to change:Turn defensive when capex guidance cuts, falling HBM prices and widening credit spreads occur together.

Product builders

Go to applications. Sell outcomes.

Applications receive $77.3 billion versus $1.5 trillion for infrastructure; coding and chatbots take 76%. The next opportunity lies in verifiable results in healthcare, legal work and financial back offices.

First step:Choose a workflow with measurable results and charge for outcomes. Start with 10 real customers rather than 100 demos. Treat the model as a supplier.

Trade-off: lower margins than infrastructure; distribution and trust are scarcer than model capability.

When to change:When model companies bundle your features, deepen proprietary data, compliance and workflow integration; avoid a price contest with the model layer.

People building AI in China

Advanced manufacturing is the constraint.

Open models, domestic inference, vertical applications and the Global South create opportunities. Rising token prices signal demand above supply, but per-card performance, power efficiency and ecosystem migration remain hard constraints.

First step:Run your own workload on a domestic inference stack. Measure throughput, latency and unit cost. Begin with inference and fine-tuning.

Trade-off: export controls, per-card performance and power consumption, and software migration can reduce the short-term experience.

When to change:Reassess owned training clusters when HBM/advanced-packaging availability improves or open models reach the frontier.

Use:Each scenario names the conditions that make it hold and the signals that would show a transition. Carry those signals into the next observation.

One quarter of operating profit at three memory makers exceeds one year's revenue at two leading model companies. The AI industry sells scarcity.

In 2026, AI infrastructure spending reaches $1.48 trillion, versus $28.3 billion for generative model software. For every $52 building roads, $1 goes to the driver.

When suppliers are also shareholders, customers and guarantors, demand signals become interdependent. AI's next wall is power; its next fault line is cash flow.
