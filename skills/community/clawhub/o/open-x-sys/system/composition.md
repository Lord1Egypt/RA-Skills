# System Composition: How Independent Systems Form Higher-Level Capability

Whole-system capability comes from real, runnable relations among independent parts. A person, AI host, file system, tool, domain application, and outside organization may complete something together while each retains its own state, boundary, authority, and exit path.

## Physical containment and actual effects

In a computer environment, hardware and operating system host files, networks, and the AI host. The host carries conversation, LLMs, tools, permissions, and events. OpenXSys interprets natural-language systems and organizes effects within it.

Actual effects form a network: a person offers intent, correction, and decisions; AI forms understanding and works; tools change files or external state; feedback from reality changes the model again. Physical containment says where a part lives. Effect relations say who owns state, how it changes, and whether understanding and feedback exist between systems.

## Conditions for a higher-level system

Several systems form a higher-level system when:

- they share a whole-system capability or result to produce;
- critical relations transfer and transform the necessary meaning, state, and effects;
- each subsystem's ownership, permissions, and authoritative state remain clear;
- the timescales of action and feedback connect;
- success, failure, correction, stopping, and exit each have a destination;
- whole-system results feed back into affected subsystems.

Relations generate emergent capability at the higher level while subsystem identity, ownership, and internal freedom remain alive. When a relation is complex, unfold it as a bridge system responsible for observation, translation, action, confirmation, and feedback.

## A person's own system

A root system connects the projects, works, capabilities, relations, and choices a person owns or participates in. It lets past gains enter new reality while each domain system continues to hold authoritative state. See the [root system](root.md) and [relations between systems](../core/relation.md).
