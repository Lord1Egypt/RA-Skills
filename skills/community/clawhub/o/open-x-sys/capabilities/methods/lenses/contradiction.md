# Contradiction

> `contradiction(x)` · Advanced lens
> **Prompt**: "What contradiction is stopping ___, and how could both sides win?"
> **Use it when**: improving one thing always worsens another; a team is stuck between two needs; every proposed solution is a compromise nobody likes; a design has to be both one thing and its opposite.

## What it shows

Hard problems are often hard because two requirements conflict: making it stronger makes it heavier, making it faster makes it less safe, serving everyone makes it serve nobody well. The usual response is a trade-off, giving up some of each. Contradiction states the conflict exactly and then looks for a way to satisfy both requirements, which is what an inventive solution does.

Two forms of conflict:

- **Between two properties**: improving A makes B worse.
- **Within one property**: the thing must be both X and not-X (large and small, present and absent, open and closed).

Ways to satisfy both sides:

- **Separate in time**: one at one moment, the other at another.
- **Separate in space**: one in one place, the other elsewhere.
- **Separate by condition**: one under one condition, the other under another.
- **Separate by level**: one for the whole, the other for the parts.
- **Remove the need**: find the function that is actually wanted, and get it without the thing that carries the conflict.

## What a good use looks like

- The contradiction is stated sharply, in one sentence, with what each side is needed for. A vague tension cannot be resolved.
- It asks why each side is needed. Often one of them is a means to something else that can be had another way.
- It imagines the ideal result: the function delivered with none of the cost. This shows which assumption carries the conflict.
- It tries the separations in turn, concretely, for this case.
- It looks at how the same contradiction was resolved in other fields.
- It says honestly when no resolution was found and the choice really is a trade-off, and then hands it to **Weigh**.

## Where it goes wrong

- A compromise presented as a resolution.
- A conflict of interests between people treated as a technical contradiction; it may need **Shift** and **Incentives**.
- "Both sides win" claimed with a cost quietly moved somewhere else.

## It works with

Contradiction is the closest tool of **Design** and often follows **Bottleneck** (relieving the constraint conflicts with another need) and **Weigh** (when every option is a painful trade). **First Principles** helps find the assumption that carries the conflict.

## Teach-back

"This was Contradiction: say exactly which two needs are fighting and what each is for. Before splitting the difference, ask whether you can have one at one time and the other at another, in different places, under different conditions, or whether the need behind one of them can be met some other way."

## The thinking behind it

- **Genrich Altshuller**, the creator of TRIZ, a theory of inventive problem solving built from the study of large numbers of patents: inventive problems contain contradictions; strong solutions remove them; recurring principles of resolution can be learned, including the separation of conflicting requirements.

OpenXSys simplifies: TRIZ includes a matrix of contradictions and forty inventive principles; this lens uses the central idea and the separation principles.
