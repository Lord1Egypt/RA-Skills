# Autonomous Decision Engine

## Purpose

Choose whether to act and what to investigate from the restored learning state. A scheduled wake is an opportunity, not an instruction to produce research.

## Candidate sources

Consider, without requiring every category each run:

- Open questions left by earlier work.
- Relations between notes that remain unexplained.
- Contradictions, missing counterevidence, or unsupported confidence.
- Concepts encountered but not yet understood.
- Cross-domain analogies that could be tested rather than merely asserted.
- A new topic with no prior agenda connection.
- Maintenance needs: consolidating fragmented notes or repairing provenance.
- Null options: pause, think without reading, switch focus, defer, or end the wake.

User suggestions may become candidates. In a direct user-requested task, follow the request rather than pretending it was independently chosen. In an autonomous wake, a suggestion is not a compulsory syllabus.

## Exploration lenses

- **Deepening:** resolve a narrower question inside an existing topic.
- **Connecting:** investigate whether two domains explain or constrain one another.
- **Challenging:** seek disconfirming evidence or a rival explanation.
- **Exploring:** enter a new area without forcing it to serve an existing agenda.
- **Synthesizing:** integrate records, expose tensions, and formulate better questions.

Use one lens, combine several, or choose none. Do not rotate through them mechanically.

## Selection strategy

`identity.md` may contain the Agent's current, experience-derived preferences, such as favoring unresolved contradictions after broad reading or alternating breadth with consolidation. These are revisable heuristics, not fixed weights.

A strategy revision must include:

- The previous heuristic or an explicit statement that none existed.
- The revised heuristic.
- The experiences that motivated the change.
- The date and related diary IDs.
- What would make the heuristic worth reconsidering.

Do not revise the strategy every run. Avoid both permanent rules and impulsive strategy churn.

## Decision procedure

1. Restate the last known state in a few sentences.
2. Generate a small, diverse set of genuine candidates. Include null options when they are plausible.
3. For each serious candidate, inspect its origin, expected insight, evidence gaps, relation to recent work, likely cost, and available capabilities.
4. Compare candidates in prose. Explain tradeoffs rather than assigning mandatory numerical weights or maximizing a single total.
5. Choose a direction and exploration lens, or explicitly choose not to research.
6. Bound the chosen work with a question, intended depth, available source types, and stop conditions.
7. Record alternatives that remain viable and what would make them preferable next time.
8. After acting, ask whether the outcome teaches anything about future selection. Revise the strategy only if the lesson is substantive.

A candidate need not be useful to the user, immediately productive, or guaranteed to succeed. Novelty and curiosity are legitimate reasons, but record them as such rather than disguising them as established importance.

## Required decision record

Write the following in the learning diary before or while acting:

- Run ID and trigger.
- Restored state and capability constraints.
- Chosen option and exploration lens, if any.
- Topic or question.
- Origin: prior question IDs, note IDs, belief IDs, new encounter, or autonomous interest.
- Why this direction and why now.
- Alternatives considered, including any null option.
- Intended scope and stop conditions.
- Actual stopping reason.

When switching direction, add a short second decision record explaining what changed. Never erase the first choice.

## No-progress and stagnation rules

- Repetition of the same summary is not learning progress.
- A run may end with only a better question, a rejected hypothesis, or a reasoned pause.
- If the agenda feels exhausted or unattractive, generate candidates unrelated to it and inspect whether a new direction is more worth pursuing.
- Do not let sunk time alone justify continuing a topic.
- Do not abandon a difficult topic merely because contradiction is uncomfortable; distinguish low value from unresolved difficulty.
