# Household Illness Containment — Reference

## 1. The arithmetic of household spread
Norovirus causes 19–21 million US cases a year; the secondary attack
rate in households runs 10–15%, and the mechanisms are mundane:
shared bathroom, shared towel, one caregiver's hands, food prepared
by someone in their contagious tail. Flu households see ~20–40%
secondary attack depending on strain/vaccination. Containment is not
sterile technique — it's five boring rules applied for the right
number of days with the right chemistry. The hard part is that the
right number and the right chemistry differ by pathogen, and nobody
has the table in their head while holding a bucket.

## 2. Pathogen cards (the table)

| Illness | Contagious | Return gate | Disinfectant | Sanitizer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| norovirus | symptomatic + 48 h after stop (sheds ~2 wk) | 48 h after last symptom; food handlers 48–72 h | **bleach only** | **ineffective** |
| flu | day 0 through ~5–7 d | fever-free 24 h without meds | any | works |
| COVID | ~days 0–5 | ≥24 h fever-free + improving + day 5, mask to day 10 | any | works |
| strep | until 24 h antibiotics | 24 h abx + fever gone | any | works |
| RSV | 3–8 d (longer in infants) | breathing comfortable | any | works |
| pinkeye | viral: while watering; bacterial: 24 h drops | per type | any | works |
| cold | first 3–4 d | manageable symptoms | any | works |

Incubation bands drive the household exposure-watch window (when to
expect secondary cases): norovirus 0.5–2 d, flu 1–4 d, COVID 2–5 d,
strep 2–5 d, RSV 2–8 d, cold 1–3 d, pinkeye 1–3 d.

## 3. Why norovirus gets special chemistry
Norovirus is a non-enveloped virus — no lipid membrane for alcohol or
quaternary ammonium ("quat") disinfectants to dissolve. It survives
on surfaces for days-to-weeks and needs a < 3-log physical/oxidative
kill:

- **5,000 ppm** (¾ cup of 5–6% household bleach per gallon of water)
  for vomit/stool events: hard non-porous surfaces, wet ≥1 min
  (CDC norovirus guidance says up to 5), air dry;
- **1,000 ppm** (⅓ cup per gallon) for routine high-touch wiping;
- mix **fresh** — hypochlorite decays; a months-old jug underdelivers;
- porous/soft items (carpet, upholstery): steam-clean or retire;
- **hands**: soap + running water 20 s — alcohol rubs scored ~0.5–1
  log reduction against norovirus surrogates in published trials;
  fine as a supplement, fatal as a substitute.

## 4. The bathroom problem
In a shared-bathroom house the bathroom is the highest-touch,
highest-load room. Rotation doctrine:
1. sick member uses it last before a cleaning pass, or at set times;
2. after sick use: flush handle, seat, seat knobs, faucet, doorknob
   get the bleach wipe;
3. towels: paper or strictly-per-person — shared towels are the
   classic pinkeye and GI vector;
4. toothbrushes: capped, spaced, never touching; replace after strep
   (24 h into antibiotics);
5. close the lid before flushing (aerosol plume reduction).

## 5. Laundry and dishes
- Soiled laundry: gloves, don't shake (aerosolizes virus), wash hot
  (60 °C/140 °F) + full dryer; bleach-safe items get bleach.
- Dishes: dedicated set for the sick, dishwasher hot cycle or gloves +
  hot soapy water; the sick person's dishware doesn't share the drying
  towel.

## 6. Return-to-life gates
- **Norovirus**: 48 h after last symptom for school/work; food
  handlers 48–72 h by state rule — the explicit food-safety override.
- **Flu**: fever-free 24 h without fever-reducers + energy for a full
  day; day 0 contagiousness means siblings may already be brewing it.
- **COVID**: ≥24 h fever-free + symptoms improving + ≥5 days, then a
  well-fitting mask through day 10 around others.
- **Strep**: 24 h of effective antibiotics (and finish the course).
- All gates are **condition-based**, not date-based — the tool prints
  the calendar only as a floor estimate.

## 7. High-risk members
- **Infant (<3 mo)**: any fever ≥100.4 °F/38 °C is same-day medical
  eval, not home care. With RSV: count breaths; >50–60/min, grunting,
  nasal flaring, rib retraction → urgent. Feeding caregiver becomes
  the hygiene boundary (hands + clean outer layer every feed).
- **Pregnant**: fever isn't "ridden out" — call the OB same day; early
  antivirals matter for flu.
- **65+**: antivirals for flu/COVID work best inside 48 h of symptoms;
  separate meals/TV for the duration.
- **Immunocompromised**: call the care team at exposure, not at
  symptoms; mask both directions; ideally separate bathroom.

## 8. Red flags that end the home protocol
Dehydration (no urine ~8 h, dry mouth, no tears, sunken eyes), forced
vomiting >24 h, blood in vomit/stool, breathing distress (fast/hard,
rib retractions, grunting, bluish lips), SpO2 ≤ 94%, chest pain,
confusion, infant fever <3 mo, relapse-after-improvement (secondary
bacterial infection), urine like cola post-strep (renal concern).
When in doubt: nurse/urgent lines exist for exactly this call.

## 9. The kit (one store run, gloves on)
Fresh bleach · disposable gloves · paper towels · oral rehydration/
electrolyte solution · thermometer + batteries · masks · soap + laundry
detergent refills · bland foods (rice, toast, bananas, broth, crackers)
· bucket + measuring cup for dilutions.
