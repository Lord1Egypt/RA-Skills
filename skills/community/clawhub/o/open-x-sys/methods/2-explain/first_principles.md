# First Principles

> `first_principles(x)` · Stage 2 · Explain · **starter method**
> **Prompt**: "Break ___ down to what must be true, and rebuild it."
> **Also heard as**: "Why does ___ cost that much?" · "Does it have to be this way?" · "What is ___ actually made of?"
> **Reveals**: what is physics or fact, what is only habit or assumption, and what a rebuild from fundamentals looks like.

## What it is for

Most things are the way they are partly for reasons that must hold and partly because that is how they have been done. Reasoning by analogy ("others do it this way") cannot tell the two apart. First Principles does: it breaks the subject down to what must be true, sets aside what is merely customary, and rebuilds from the fundamentals to see what is actually possible. The gap between the rebuild and the present state is where the insight is.

## What it reveals

- **What must be true**: physics, mathematics, hard facts, the irreducible needs of the people involved.
- **What is convention**: practices inherited from history, industry habit, analogy with something else.
- **What is assumption**: things believed and never checked.
- **The rebuild**: what the thing would look like, or cost, if built from the fundamentals alone.
- **The gap**, and why it exists.

## What a good use looks like

- The three kinds are visibly separated, and each item has its basis. "Must be true" is reserved for what really cannot be otherwise.
- There is a quantity wherever one is possible. For a cost: the price of the materials, the energy, the irreducible labor, then everything stacked above that floor, each layer named.
- The rebuild is actually carried out, not only gestured at, and it is compared with what exists.
- The gap is explained. Conventions often exist for good reasons that are not physical: safety, regulation, trust, coordination among many parties. Find the reason before calling a convention unnecessary.
- It says which parts of the gap could be closed, by whom, and at what cost.
- Sources and dates accompany the facts; where a number is estimated, the estimate is shown (see `methods/1-see/estimate.md`).

## Where it goes wrong

- **A slogan for an opinion.** "From first principles, this is obviously wasteful", with no decomposition.
- **Breaking down without rebuilding.** A list of components is not yet an insight.
- **Dismissing a convention without learning why it exists.** Some fences are there for a reason.
- **Treating only physics as real.** Law, trust and human limits are constraints too, even if they can change.
- **Assumptions smuggled into the "must be true" pile.**

## It works with

First Principles follows **Open** or **X-ray**, uses **Size It** for its numbers, and leads to **Leverage** and **Design** (a rebuild is the start of a design). It is the first step of **Musk's algorithm**, where it appears as questioning every requirement, and the core of *Learn something deeply*.

## Teach-back

"This was First Principles: list what must be true, set aside what is only habit, and rebuild from there. Then ask why the real thing differs from your rebuild, because some of those reasons are good ones."

## The thinking behind it

- **Aristotle**, *Metaphysics* and *Posterior Analytics*: a first principle is the first basis from which a thing is known; knowledge is built from principles that are not themselves derived.
- **Elon Musk** has described reasoning from first principles rather than by analogy in interviews, with the cost of battery packs reasoned from the price of their constituent materials as a well-known example. He popularized the phrase for a wide audience.

OpenXSys simplifies: in Aristotle the term belongs to a theory of knowledge; here it names a practical habit. The specifics of any quoted example are checked against the source before being repeated.
