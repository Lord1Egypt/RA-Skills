# Loops

> `loops(x)` · Stage 1 · See
> **Prompt**: "What keeps ___ going, or holding it back?"
> **Also heard as**: "Why does ___ keep growing?" · "Why can't we get past ___?" · "Why does every fix wear off?"
> **Reveals**: feedback loops, and the known pattern at play.

## What it is for

Behavior that persists (steady growth, a plateau, a decline, an oscillation, a problem that returns after every fix) is almost always produced by feedback: a result that acts back on its own cause. Loops finds those circles of cause and effect. It explains why a system keeps doing what it does when nobody intends it, and it is where many reveals come from, because people usually see the straight line and miss the circle.

## What it reveals

- **Reinforcing loops**: more leads to more (or less to less). Growth, collapse, lock-in.
- **Balancing loops**: a gap between a state and a goal produces action that closes the gap. Stability, resistance, limits.
- **Delays**: the time between an action and its return, which is what turns a balancing loop into overshoot and oscillation.
- **Dominance**: which loop is in charge now, and what would make another take over.
- **The known pattern**, when the structure matches one in `patterns/`.

## What a good use looks like

- Every link in a loop is a real causal relation with evidence, stated as a mechanism: "more drivers shorten waiting times" and not "drivers relate to growth".
- It finds the balancing loop as well as the reinforcing one. Nothing grows forever; the question is what stops it and when.
- Delays are marked, with a rough length.
- It says which loop dominates at present and names the condition under which dominance shifts.
- A pattern is named only when its structure is actually there, with where that pattern usually breaks.
- It ends with where the loop could be strengthened, weakened or broken.

## Where it goes wrong

- **A diagram of everything.** Twenty arrows explain nothing. One or two loops that carry the behavior do.
- **"A virtuous cycle"** claimed with no mechanism for any of its links.
- **Only the growth loop**, with the limit left out.
- **Loops with no delays**, which cannot explain why things overshoot.
- **A pattern name used as an explanation.** "It is a flywheel" is a label until each turn of it is shown.

## It works with

Loops follows **X-ray** and **Flow**, and feeds **Leverage** (the strongest places to push are usually in the loops) and **What-if** (loops are what make futures diverge). It sits in *Understand it* and *Solve a recurring problem*. Read the relevant page in `patterns/` before naming a pattern.

## Teach-back

"This was Loops: when something keeps going or keeps coming back, look for the circle. Find what feeds itself, find what pushes back, mark the delays, and ask which one is in charge right now."

## The thinking behind it

- **Norbert Wiener**, *Cybernetics* (1948): feedback as the common principle of control in machines and living things.
- **Peter Senge**, *The Fifth Discipline* (1990): system archetypes, recurring loop structures that produce recognizable behavior.
- **Jay W. Forrester** and **Donella Meadows**: feedback structure as the source of a system's behavior over time.
