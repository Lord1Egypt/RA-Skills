# Flywheel

> A reinforcing pattern. Several steps arranged in a circle, each making the next easier, so that effort accumulates into momentum.

## The structure

A closed chain of causes: A makes B easier, B makes C easier, C makes A easier. Each turn leaves something behind (customers, data, reputation, lower cost, skill) that makes the next turn cheaper or stronger. No single push produces the result. Many pushes in a consistent direction do.

## How to recognize it

- You can write the chain as a circle of three to six links, and **each link is a real mechanism** you can evidence in this subject.
- Something **accumulates** with each turn: a stock that persists and makes the next turn easier.
- The behavior over time is a slow start followed by acceleration, with no single moment of breakthrough.
- The organization keeps doing the same few things for a long time, and declines distractions.

## Where it usually breaks

- **A link that is weak or imagined.** The circle looks complete on a slide, and one arrow has no mechanism behind it. The whole is as strong as that link.
- **A limit.** Every reinforcing loop meets a balancing one: a saturated market, a capacity, a regulator, management attention. See [limits to growth](limits_to_growth.md).
- **The wrong thing accumulating.** The loop spins a number that is easy to grow and does not carry the next step.
- **It runs in reverse.** The same links that built momentum drain it when one of them turns negative: fewer customers, less data, worse product, fewer customers.
- **Inconsistency.** Changing direction repeatedly resets the accumulation.
- **Imitation.** If every link can be copied, the advantage lasts only as long as the head start.

## Where to push

Strengthen the weakest link. Remove friction between two steps. Protect the stock that accumulates. Find the coming limit before reaching it.

## What to check before naming it

Draw the circle and write the mechanism on every arrow. Find the accumulating stock. Ask what would stop it. If one arrow says only "leads to", it is not yet a flywheel.

## Source

- **Jim Collins**, *Good to Great* (2001): the flywheel effect, sustained results that come from the cumulative effect of many consistent pushes, contrasted with a "doom loop" of reactive changes of direction. Collins later developed the idea of mapping a specific organization's flywheel as a sequence of components.

The underlying structure is a reinforcing feedback loop (see `methods/1-see/loops.md`).
