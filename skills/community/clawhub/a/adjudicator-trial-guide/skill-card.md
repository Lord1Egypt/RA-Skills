## Description:

This Chinese-language skill helps judges, arbitrators, judicial assistants, and law students apply Zou Bihua's Nine-Step Method of Elements Trial to civil and commercial adjudication or arbitration workflows.

This skill is ready for commercial/non-commercial use.

## Publisher:

### License/Terms of Use:

MIT-0

## Use Case:

Legal professionals and law students use this skill to structure civil and commercial case handling, including pre-trial claim and legal-basis preparation, trial issue management, fact finding, judgment-writing consistency checks, and judicial explanation boundaries. It is intended as a Chinese-language adjudication methodology aid, not a substitute for current law, jurisdiction-specific rules, formal legal authority, or professional judgment.

### Deployment Geography for Use:

Global

## Known Risks and Mitigations:

Risk: Legal guidance can become incorrect if applied without checking current law, local procedure, or forum-specific rules.

Mitigation: Verify all outputs against current statutes, judicial interpretations, arbitration rules, and authoritative local practice before use.

Risk: Non-professional users may over-rely on adjudication guidance outside its intended legal-professional context.

Mitigation: Use the skill only as a study or workbench aid for qualified legal professionals, judicial staff, arbitrators, researchers, or supervised law students.

Risk: Unreviewed guidance about explanation rights, evidence guidance, or value judgments could affect neutrality or procedural fairness.

Mitigation: Apply outputs through independent professional review, preserve party equality, document explanations in the case record, and avoid replacing the adjudicator's own reasoning.
