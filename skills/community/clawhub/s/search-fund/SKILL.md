## 1. What a search fund is

One or two entrepreneurs raise a small pool of search capital from a group of aligned investors, spend one to three years identifying and acquiring a privately held company, and then run it as CEO, typically for six to ten years.

The difference from PE is the sequence: the person comes first, the deal comes later. Capital enters in two stages - search capital covering salary and diligence costs, then acquisition capital at closing. If nothing is acquired, there is no deal and the search capital is largely lost.

Four common variants: traditional funded search, self-funded, accelerator, single-sponsor. A fifth, long-duration or committed-capital vehicles, holds for ten years or more and is used to build a platform through successive acquisitions.

## 2. 2026 data (two scopes, never mix them)

US and Canada - Stanford GSB, 2026 Search Fund Study (Case E-967), as of 31 December 2025:

  862 core search funds since 1984
  Aggregate IRR 33.9%, ROI 4.75x across all funds, including failed searches
  Acquired and exited: IRR 39.3%, ROI 5.98x
  Acquisition rate 58% of concluded funds; 48% for the 2021-24 cohort
  2024-25 cohort: median purchase price USD 16.0m at 6.2x EBITDA; median CEO first-year base USD 208,000
  Median 13 investors; median USD 550,000 raised per principal
  Excluding funds returning 10x or more: ROI 2.8x, IRR 27%. Excluding the top decile: ROI 2.1x, IRR 20%

Outside the US and Canada - IESE, International Search Funds 2026 (ST-689-E), as of 31 December 2025:

  492 search funds across 48 countries and regions since 1992
  Aggregate ROI 2.8x, IRR 30%
  2024 set a record with 96 new funds; 2025 saw 82, exceeding US and Canada for the first time
  Of 216 acquisitions, 167 still operating, 38 exited at a gain, 11 at a loss

Two qualifications must always be stated. First, both datasets are non-Chinese markets whose model depends on available acquisition debt, seller willingness to accept deferred consideration, and a mature investor pool. Second, returns are extremely concentrated: the mean is not the expectation for any single fund.

## 3. China: the honest version

One hard number: in the same international count, mainland China has one identified international core search fund, Taiwan, China has one, India has 59, Japan 53, Spain 98. China does not lack SMEs; it lacks the conditions under which this model works.

Regulation: the Commercial Bank M&A Loan Measures (Jinguifa 2025 No. 27), effective 2026, raised controlling-stake acquisition loans to 70% of transaction value with a term of up to ten years, and minority-stake loans to 60% over seven years. But Article 8 requires a high degree of industrial relevance or strategic synergy between acquirer and target. A purely financial acquisition by an individual with no industry link rarely passes. The binding constraint is not loan capacity but borrower eligibility.

Two further structural obstacles. Seller preferences: the dominant cohort in mainland China ranks succession to children first, sale to a peer second, a full-cash exit third; "sell to a younger operator, stay on, take deferred consideration" ranks last. Exit: the mid-market (RMB 30-500m revenue) has thin secondary liquidity.

So only three forms work in China today: an industrial partner acquiring jointly with an operating principal; incumbent management acquiring alongside an external investor; a professional manager taking over with a financial investor holding a minority. Individual leveraged buyouts by a young searcher are not a viable form here.

Demand side is not in doubt: roughly RMB 18 trillion of family wealth is expected to transfer across the next decade, and many sound SMEs have no internal successor.

## 4. If you own a business: a self-check

This is a filter, not a valuation service and not an offer.

  Is the business genuinely ready for a handover within two years
  Net profit in the range of RMB 5m to 30m, positive for three consecutive years
  Customers are diversified; no single customer above 30%
  The owner is willing to stay on for 6 to 12 months after closing

Any of the following usually disqualifies: a demand for full cash upfront with no deferred consideration; profit dependent on a single customer, licence or relationship; heavy-asset or highly cyclical industries; no successor management and no willingness to stay; unresolved litigation, tax exposure, or unpaid social insurance.

Why to think about it early: the value of a founder-dependent business depreciates with the founder's age. Delay costs customers, equipment and key staff.

## 5. If you want to buy a company

Six steps in order:

  1 Understand why you want to buy rather than start from scratch
  2 Budget two to three years of search, accepting that it may produce nothing
  3 Raise search capital from a small group of people who back you
  4 Source proactively and industry-first, not by waiting for brokers
  5 Raise acquisition capital, and solve leverage and seller notes
  6 Take over as CEO, and hold for six to ten years

Empirical reference points (US and Canada): median 20 months from search to acquisition; 48% acquisition rate for the 2021-24 cohort; 74% acquisition rate among those signing a letter of intent within six months; 58% for partnered searches versus 43% for solo. The three most cited reasons an LOI fails: discovery in due diligence, valuation disagreement with the seller, and insufficient investor support.

In China the constraint is different: an individual cannot readily obtain acquisition debt, so the path is either a joint acquisition with an industrial partner or an entry through a management buyout.

## 6. If you are an investor: why this is treated as an asset class

Four tests, all of which must hold: a repeatable process, a measurable return distribution, an identifiable correlation profile, and a supporting intermediary ecosystem.

  Scale: median acquisition USD 16.0m in 2024-25, too small for most institutions, which makes it a natural fit for family offices and private investors
  Duration and liquidity: one to three years of search plus six to ten years of ownership, seven to twelve years in total, with little interim liquidity
  Return and risk: see section 2; note that 42% of concluded funds acquired nothing, rising to 52% for the 2021-24 cohort, and search capital is largely lost on failure
  Concentration: excluding the top decile, ROI falls from 4.75x to 2.1x
  Correlation: the underlying cash flows are private SME operating cash flows, with low correlation to public equities

Two consequences follow. First, the distribution of single-fund outcomes is very wide, so the mean is not a forecast for any one fund. Second, the asset class only works in aggregate, and the real work in building a portfolio is selecting operators rather than selecting industries. Operator assessment should weigh diversity of background, professional reputation, learning speed, ability to build trust, and integrity - not financial modelling skill.

In the US this is already an institutionalised asset class. In China it is not; China is at the ecosystem-building stage. Both sentences must be said together.

## 7. If you are a professional adviser

What these deals look like: median USD 16m at 6.2x EBITDA, but process complexity close to a large-cap transaction - fund formation documents, due diligence, valuation, tax structuring, seller notes, management incentive plans.

Why the demand persists: a successful search fund generates several transactions over seven to ten years (the acquisition, add-ons, the exit), so it is a long-term client rather than a one-off. And with 58% and 48% acquisition rates, roughly half of all searches fail, so a pipeline needs volume.

Where the value sits: the three most cited failure reasons - due diligence findings 79%, valuation disagreement 45%, insufficient investor support 40% - map directly onto diligence, valuation, and deal structuring.
