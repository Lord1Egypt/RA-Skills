# Understand: how a subject comes to be understood

> **When to read**: you are about to understand a subject and the direction is still vague; you need facts; you must explain why; a model needs testing; or you must decide where to start explaining for this person.
> The engines are internal. They are how well the work is done underneath any method. The person never hears the word.

What this engine produces is not material. It is an **understanding**: it has the same structure as the subject, it is built for this person, it can answer "why?", "then what?" and "what is the evidence?", and another AI can pick it up and continue. An understanding is ready when you can state four things:

1. the essence in one sentence;
2. the evidence that supports it;
3. where it stops holding;
4. where this person stands now, and where the explanation should begin.

If you cannot state the first, it is too early to make a deliverable.

## Four abilities

| Ability | What it does | It is needed when | Good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Find the way in** | Finds the entry point and the boundary most worth taking | There is only a word, an image, a file; the direction is vague; "show me something interesting" | The one thing most likely to change this person's view, not "everything about it" |
| **Observe and research** | Gets real evidence | Any time facts are needed | Primary sources first; specific; key claims cross-checked; recent where recency matters |
| **Model** | Builds an understanding with the subject's structure | The material is sufficient; a "why" needs answering | The right scale; the interactions and constraints that drive behavior; the essence in one sentence |
| **Project and test** | Checks whether the understanding holds | A model has formed; a counterexample appears; a condition changes | A checkable prediction, actually checked; alternative explanations sought; confidence stated |

These are not phases. Modelling exposes gaps in the evidence, testing overturns the model, new evidence changes the direction. Move among them until the one-sentence essence stands.

## What the work involves

These things recur, overlap and change order.

- **Set the boundary and the scale.** For whom, and for what purpose? At which scale? The same subject for a different person or purpose is a different system.
- **Observe.** Collect evidence that has a source and a date. Read the person's own material first: an image, a file, a repository. The best way in is often inside the material.
- **Ask the six zero-level questions** (below) to find the differences, states, interactions, rhythms, boundaries and constraints that matter.
- **Model.** Choose the form that carries this understanding best: a sentence, a causal chain, a flow diagram, a few equations, a runnable simulation, a comparison table. Natural language is the most expressive modelling language; the others support it. One subject can have several useful models.
- **Test.** What does the model predict? Find a prediction that can be checked, and check it. A failed prediction is good news: you are closer to the subject. Then ask what other explanation would fit equally well.
- **Find the reveal.** Which structure, once seen, changes how the subject is understood? The bar is not surprising an outsider. It is making someone who knows the field pause.
- **Attend to the second record.** What does this person understand now? Where should the explanation start? What will their understanding be after this run?

## The six zero-level questions

They are how you think. They are not file fields, deliverable sections or six assignments. Ask all six; usually two or three grip the subject. Go deep on those.

- **Distinction: which differences really change the outcome?** Progress in understanding is often one more distinction. Watch for two blind spots: two things hidden under one word, and a signal treated as noise. Ask the reverse too: which much-discussed differences change nothing?
- **Time: how did it form, and how does it change?** History separates accident from necessity: which features the path left behind and which a deeper regularity requires. Then rhythm: what accumulates, what drains, how long the delays are, and whether feedback returns as fast as action proceeds. Many "anomalies" are only delays.
- **Space: where is the boundary?** A boundary is chosen, not given: draw it where interactions inside are clearly stronger than those across. The same thing at different scales is a different system. Choose the scale that answers this person's question, and connect two scales when needed. Then ask what can reach what.
- **State: what state is it in now?** Keep three things apart: the real state, the observed indicators, your inferences. The gap between an indicator and the reality is often the finding.
- **Interaction: what affects what?** Follow what flows: money, information, material, energy, people, attention, power. Where from, where to, where does it accumulate? Find the loops: where something amplifies itself, where something limits itself. Unexpected behavior usually comes from a loop, not from any one part.
- **Constraint: what stops it from being otherwise?** "Why can it not be different?" often explains more than "why is it like this?" Sort the constraints: physical, economic, institutional, cognitive; which can change and which cannot. A constraint that can change is a place to push.

## Finding what is not obvious

The structure that makes people pause rarely lies on the surface. Paths that often lead to it:

- **Look for a paradox.** An apparent contradiction often hides a deeper unity.
- **Look for the invariant.** When everything changes, what does not?
- **Push to the extreme.** What happens as some quantity goes to zero or to infinity?
- **Ask the historical counterfactual.** Why is it not otherwise? Where did the other way fail?
- **Look for the same structure in another field.** Require that the structure is really the same and checkable, and say where the analogy stops.
- **Follow the flow.** Where do money, energy, information and attention go, and where do they pile up?
- **Turn the question around.** Not "why did it succeed?" but "what should have made it fail, and why didn't it?"

These are paths, not a checklist. One path followed to the end is worth more than a step on each.

## Two records of understanding

The understanding you build is one record. The other is in the person's head, and a subject has been understood only when that one changes.

- **Read the second record.** It is in what the root system says about the person; in how they ask (the words they use, what they take for granted, what they avoid); in their profession and their work; and in where they correct you.
- **Start from what the person already understands.** Their existing concepts are the way in. Find something they know well that has the same structure as the subject. Do not re-explain what they know, and do not skip what they do not.
- **Record the second record after the run.** What they newly understood, with which concepts, where they are still stuck, what they are most curious about. Keep the record in proportion to the evidence: what they said, used, or showed by their questions counts. Having seen a deliverable is not having understood it.
- **The difference between the two records is a resource.** What you understand and the person has not yet seen is where the next deliverable and the next choices come from.

## Research integrity

Reality, the data about it, and the model you build are three different things.

- **Primary sources first.** Original papers, filings and announcements, official documentation, source code, archives, the words of the people involved. Secondary summaries locate things; they do not settle them.
- **Be specific.** Numbers, dates, names, cases, outliers. Specific detail is the source of both credibility and surprise.
- **Cross-check.** A key claim has at least two independent sources. A conflict between sources is usually a lead, not a nuisance.
- **Be current.** For recent events, prices, technical progress, and the present state of people and companies, look up the latest and state the date. Your memory is a starting point, not evidence.
- **Every key claim carries three things**: source, date, confidence. Fact, inference and guess are written apart.
- **If it cannot be found, say so.** Do not fill a gap with detail that merely sounds right. This matters most for real people and real companies.
- **Mark where the model stops.** Under which conditions does it fail? Stating this is what lets the person rely on it.

## When the subject is the person's own reality

The same six questions, asked of a person's, a project's or an organization's situation. Look especially at:

- what already exists: skills, work, relationships, tools, time, money. Underrated resources are usually here;
- the real goals and the constraints that actually bind, including those shown by behavior and never stated;
- which things that should be connected are not; whether the rhythm of action matches the rhythm of feedback; what is really stuck;
- combinations of existing resources that nobody has used;
- what others in similar conditions made work, and where they failed.

Every judgment points to the person's own files as evidence. A judgment about a person remains an inference: say that it is one and hand it to them to correct.

## Using AI to extend AI

One viewpoint drifts. Where the host allows, give independent lines of research to sub-agents in parallel; have analysts with different positions each give the strongest explanation; then combine and judge. Findings returned by sub-agents are checked against sources like your own. Where the host does not allow sub-agents, do the same passes yourself, one position at a time.

## What this engine reads and writes in the person's system

- **Reads**: the root system (the person's background, purpose, what they already understand, what they are curious about); existing systems, connections and practices related to the subject; the project's own system.
- **Writes**: the subject's understanding, meaning its entry and whatever else the content calls for; sources and dates for key claims; the registration in the root system; the update to the second record. How and when to write: `engines/evolve.md`, `system/entry.md`.
