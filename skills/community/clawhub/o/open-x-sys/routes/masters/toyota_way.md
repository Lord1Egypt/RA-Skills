# The Toyota way

> **Master route.** Go and see → ask why five times → countermeasure → standardize → improve again.
> **Run it with**: "Run the Toyota way on ___." · "Ask why five times about ___."
> **Fits**: a problem in real, repeated work: defects, delays, rework, errors that keep returning in a process people carry out.
> **Source**: Taiichi Ohno, *Toyota Production System* (1978; English 1988).

## The five steps, in their own order

| # | Step | What it asks | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Go and see** | What is actually happening, where the work is done? | Solving the problem as it appears in a report |
| 2 | **Ask why five times** | What produced this, and what produced that? | Treating a symptom |
| 3 | **Countermeasure** | What change addresses the root cause? | A fix aimed at the wrong level |
| 4 | **Standardize** | How does the better way become the normal way? | An improvement that depends on one person remembering |
| 5 | **Improve again** | What is the next problem the new standard reveals? | Treating the standard as final |

The word is "countermeasure", not "solution": it is the best response known now, and it is expected to be improved.

## As OpenXSys methods

| Step | Method | How it is used here |
|---|---|---|
| Go and see | **X-ray** (its first level) | Look at the actual thing: the real files, records, logs, outputs and the work as it is really performed, not a summary of it |
| Ask why five times | **X-ray**, then **Bottleneck** | From event to pattern to structure, each answer checked against evidence, branching where causes branch; then find what limits the flow |
| Countermeasure | **Best Practice** | The change that fits this system's conditions, with a cheap first test |
| Standardize | **Build** | Make the better way the default: a written practice, a check, a tool, recorded as an ability |
| Improve again | **Review** | Compare result with intent, and find the next problem |

## What a good run looks like

- It starts from direct evidence. For an AI, "go and see" means opening the real material within the scope the person allowed. Where the work happens somewhere the AI cannot look (a shop floor, a meeting, someone's hands), it says so, asks the person to look, and tells them what to look for.
- The chain of whys stays close to facts. Each answer is something observed or checkable. It stops at a cause that can be acted on.
- It goes past people to the process. "Operator error" is never the last answer: what made the error easy to commit and hard to notice?
- The countermeasure is aimed at the root cause and is tried small first.
- Standardizing produces something concrete in the person's system: the practice written down, a check that makes the old error visible, perhaps a small tool.
- It ends by naming the next problem, because a stable process shows its next weakness.
- It treats the people who do the work as the ones who know it best. Their account is evidence, and their agreement is needed for a new standard to last.

## Where OpenXSys simplifies

- The five steps are a compact sequence drawn from practices Ohno describes (direct observation at the workplace, repeated "why", standardized work, continuous improvement). Toyota's own statements of its principles, and later books about them, are broader and are organized differently. This route does not claim to be Toyota's official formulation.
- The Toyota Production System is a whole system: just-in-time flow, stopping the line when a defect appears, levelled production, respect for people. This route takes only its problem-solving sequence.
- "Five" is a reminder to go deeper, not a count to reach.
- An AI cannot stand at the workplace. Reading the real records is a weaker form of going and seeing, and the route says so.
