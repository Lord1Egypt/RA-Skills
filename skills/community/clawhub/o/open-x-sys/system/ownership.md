# Ownership: Who a System Truly Belongs To

“The user owns the system” is an architectural fact. Materials, state, works, accounts, tools, relations, and decisions each belong to their real owners. OpenXSys lets those rights act together clearly and preserves them when higher-level systems form.

## Five rights make real effects possible

- **Observation right**: who may see what;
- **Interpretation and recommendation right**: who may form models, recommendations, or candidate plans;
- **Decision right**: who chooses goals, boundaries, and irreversible direction;
- **Execution right**: who may actually modify, publish, spend, or contact outside parties;
- **Correction and exit right**: who may reject an inference, stop a relation, withdraw authority, or carry away the result.

The model forms recommendations and action plans. Real owners and the host determine decision rights, tool rights, and authorized scope. Authorization may be one-time or cover continuing work in a defined scope. Systems retain internal freedom and exit rights after joining a higher-level system.

## Understanding a person

A person's goals, experience, preferences, and situation exist as evidence-backed, correctable inferences. Current user statements and corrections govern. Labels, diagnoses, and unconfirmed motives do not become facts. Credentials and payment secrets remain in appropriate security systems; sensitive state remains with its authoritative owner.

## Action and sharing

Research, making, and execution advance within authorization. Publication, spending, contacting others, expansion into private scope, persistent execution, and irreversible change follow the actual rights supplied by the user and host. A public work carries only what its purpose needs and its owner has chosen to share.
