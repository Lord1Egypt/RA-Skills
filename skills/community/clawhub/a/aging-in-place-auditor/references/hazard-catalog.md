# Hazard Catalog — the scored items, the why, the fix

The audit scores each item 0–3 (absent / mild / notable / severe) and multiplies by a weight reflecting fall-injury contribution. Weights favor bathroom transfer points, stairs, and night-path lighting — the three killers.

## Bathroom (highest-yield room)
| Hazard | Why it matters | Fix | Cost | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No toilet grab bar | Sit→stand transfer is the most common single fall | Stud-mounted 250-lb-rated bar | $25–50 | 1 |
| No shower grab bars | Towel bars & glass doors are not anchors | Two bars, entry + long wall | $40–80 | 1 |
| No non-slip surface | Soap film turns a tub into ice | Strips or suction mat | $10–25 | 1 |
| Toilet too low (15″ std) | Weak quadriceps can't launch | Raised seat / frame, 17–19″ | $25–60 | 1 |
| Standing shower only | 10 min of wet standing balance is optional | Handheld head + chair | $30–80 | 2 |
| No night light | 2AM dark trips = most common nighttime fall | Dusk-sensor LED | $8–15 | 1 |

**Grab bar law:** a real grab bar is anchored in studs/blocking and rated ≥250 lb (18″–32″ sizes; ADA angles as guidance, not law, in private homes). Suction-cup bars are for balance *cues* only. Towel bars hold 5–10 lb — they are the "grabbed-but-failed" story in half of bathroom fall ER narratives.

## Stairs & levels
- **Rail both sides** (34–36″ height, extending the tread's depth past top/bottom). One rail = zero help when the wrong side slips. $80–250, carpenter, Tier 2.
- **Switch at both ends** — descending into darkness or leaning across the void for a switch is a designed-in hazard. Three-way circuit or motion lights.
- **Contrast tape** on the first/last tread and worn nosings — presbyopia + depth-perception loss hides the final step.
- **The first-floor question:** if the only bedroom/bath is upstairs, plan a Tier-3 conversion or stair lift ($3–5k) *before* a fall makes the decision urgent.

## Bedroom & night path
- Lit path bed→bathroom (motion-sensor plugs beat wired nightlights for renters).
- Lamp/phone reachable without standing; tap- or clap-activated lamps exist for arthritic hands.
- Bed height: knees ~90° when seated (≈20–23″ mattress top). Too-low beds trap people on the floor.
- A firm chair with arms in the bedroom beats a soft sofa for dressing.

## Kitchen
- Daily items waist-to-shoulder; nothing daily above head or below knee.
- Step stool WITH a handle, 300-lb rated. No kitchen chairs as stools. Ever.
- Counter seating height 36″ is hard to rise from — a 30″ table chair with arms nearby.

## Floors & thresholds
- Loose throw rugs = the most-cited trip object in home-fall studies. Remove or tape (dual-sided carpet tape ages — replace yearly).
- Thresholds >13 mm (½″): bevel/ramp. Fanned by walker feet constantly.
- Cord crossings: reroute or covered ramps.
- Stair carpet with worn nosings is slick: replace or add nosing strips.

## Lighting physics (why "brighter" is medical, not cosmetic)
A healthy 60-year-old's retina delivers roughly **one-third** of the light to the brain as a 20-year-old's; by 85 it's worse. Practical targets:
- General room: 1600+ lumens (100W-equivalent LED) on ceiling fixtures.
- Task/kitchen counter/reading: 800+ lumens dedicated.
- Night path: warm, motion-sensor, low-glare (amber) — enough to see furniture edges, not enough to fully wake.
- Glare is the hidden enemy: frosted shades and indirect uplight beat bare bulbs for aging eyes with cataract beginnings.

## The medication layer (say it explicitly)
Four drug classes roughly double fall risk: sedative-hypnotics (benzos, “Z-drugs”), opioids, anticholinergics, and antihypertensives that cause orthostatic drops. Bifocal/progressive lenses misjudge stair depth — single-distance glasses for stairs are a real recommendation. The audit's `meds_vision` item routes this to a doctor's visit, because hardware can't fix pharmacology.

## Escalation doctrine
- **2+ falls in 12 months, living alone** → medical alert with fall detection + PT balance program (Otago / Stepping On have RCT evidence for ~30% fall reduction).
- **Walker/wheelchair + multilevel home** → OT assessment and a first-floor plan; hardware alone won't carry it.
- **Score ≥50%** → professional OT home assessment (Medicare covers when ordered; also check Area Agency on Aging free assessments).
