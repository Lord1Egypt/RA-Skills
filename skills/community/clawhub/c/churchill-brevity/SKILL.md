# Churchill Brevity

Make the essential points easy to find. Brevity is a discipline of clearer thinking, not a word-count contest.

## Procedure

1. Identify the reader's purpose: what must they understand, decide, or do? Preserve the requested genre and length; infer the purpose when evident.
2. Choose the lightest adequate form:
   - Short, crisp paragraphs for a report or written briefing.
   - An aide-mémoire of informative headings when the reader needs speaking prompts and can supply the explanation. Do not use bare headings for a standalone document that must explain itself.
   - A short main brief plus an appendix when analysis or statistics are substantial. Keep decision-changing evidence, uncertainty, and caveats in the main brief; move supporting derivations and tables, not inconvenient facts.
3. Put the main points where the reader will encounter them first. For decision work, make the recommendation or question explicit when supported by the material. Do not invent a recommendation merely to fill a template.
4. Remove padding and indirect formulations. Prefer a short, expressive phrase—even a conversational one—when it fits the audience. Replace vague abstractions with the actual actor, action, and object where the source identifies them.
5. Check the revision against the source: retain material facts, numbers, units, dates, scope, attribution, uncertainty, and required actions. A shorter sentence must not assert more than the original.

## Editing decisions

- “It is also of importance to bear in mind the following considerations…” → state the considerations directly.
- “Consideration should be given to the possibility of carrying into effect…” → name the proposed action and its actual status. Do not silently convert a possibility into an instruction or commitment.
- Delete a phrase if it contributes no meaning; replace it with one word only when that word preserves the meaning.
- Prefer plain precision over polished official jargon. Do not replace necessary technical terms with vague simpler words.
- Do not force bullets, sentence-length limits, fixed templates, a reduction percentage, or telegraphic fragments.
- Preserve a person's voice, deliberate rhythm, and genre-specific obligations. Apply narrowly to narrative or personal prose only when requested.

## Delivery and verification

Return the finished text, not a lecture about brevity. Include an appendix only when needed or requested. Briefly flag unresolved ambiguity or a material tradeoff; omit routine edit commentary unless asked.

Before returning, check: Can the reader find the essential point without hunting? Does each retained passage do useful work? Can the main brief stand alone? Did compression change any claim or hide a decision-relevant qualification?
