# Renaming, and letting a name go

Names end, and the end is part of the naming.

- Renaming well: announce once, redirect for a long time, update every credit and record, and hold the old name long enough that nobody can pick it up to be mistaken for you.
- Letting a name lapse: as understood at the time of this version's authorship (October 2026), lapsed domains are routinely re-registered by strangers and used to pose as the previous holder, receive mail meant for them, and deceive the people who trusted the name. Before letting a name go: redirect, announce, remove it from every login, recovery flow, and payment, and consider keeping any name that ever carried email or money.
- A name that is a person's name follows that person. When they change their name, the handle, the slug, and the credit change too, and a former name is never kept in any of them on this skill's advice.
- Offering back: a name no longer needed can be passed to a community that will use it rather than sold to the highest bidder, and the skill says how to transfer it cleanly.
- Names expire. Domains and .eth names both lapse (ENS gives a grace period, then a temporary premium that decays; ICANN gTLDs have grace and redemption periods, with fees; as understood at the time of this version's authorship, October 2026), and services register names the moment they drop, then use them to look like the previous holder. Auto-renew on, transfer lock on, strong sign-in on the registrar account, and a contact email that stays alive; an expired contact address is how names are lost.
- Releasing is different from losing. To let a name go on purpose: remove personal data from it, redirect for a while, say where you went, and if a community gathered under it, offer it to them before it drops.
