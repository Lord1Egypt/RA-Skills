# The guided tour

[Chinese edition](https://github.com/gengshupeng/open-x-sys/blob/main/skills/open-x-sys.zh-CN/experience/playground/tour.md)

> **For the agent.** Ten minutes along the journey on one ready subject. It is the recommended first experience for someone who has never opened a system. Run it in learn mode: name each method in one line, ask for a prediction before each reveal, and keep every stop short.

The tour is not a lecture about OpenXSys. It is one real run, slowed down so the person can see each move, on a subject whose materials are in this folder. The person should finish with one thing they did not know about the subject and the feeling that they could do this on something of their own.

## Choosing the subject

Offer two or three ready subjects and recommend one. **Why wider roads fill up** (`subjects/road-congestion.md`) is the default: almost everyone has an opinion about traffic, the common intuition is definite and is not what the evidence shows, and every stage of the journey has something to say about it.

If the person prefers another ready subject, or brings their own, run the same stops on that.

## Eight stops

Each stop is one method, a question from the person's side, a prediction, and one finding. Keep each to a few sentences and one small visual if the host can show one. Tell the person at the start that there are eight short stops and that they can skip ahead at any time.

| Stop | Stage | Method | The question | What the person should take away |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | See | **X-ray** | "What is really going on?" | Events have a structure beneath them |
| 2 | Explain | **Loops** or **First Principles** | "Why does it keep happening?" | The cause is a loop, or an assumption nobody checked |
| 3 | Anticipate | **What-if** | "What if we changed this?" | Consequences arrive late, and others respond |
| 4 | Focus | **Leverage** | "Where would a push matter most?" | Most interventions do little, a few do a lot |
| 5 | Decide | **Best Practice** | "What would actually fit here?" | A good practice depends on conditions, and comes with a cheap test |
| 6 | Create | **Make** | "Turn this into something." | A finished page with a reveal, a route strip and sources |
| 7 | Improve | **Review** | "How would we know it worked?" | A prediction made now is what makes learning possible later |
| 8 | (any) | **Swap** | "What if we had used a different method?" | A different method shows a different side of the same thing |

### How each stop goes

1. **Name the method** in one line: what it is and why it comes now.
2. **Ask for a prediction**, one question, answerable in a sentence. For stop 1 on the default subject: "A city widens its busiest highway. A few years later, what do you expect has happened to travel times?"
3. **Show what the materials say**, with the source and its date, and set it beside the prediction. If the prediction was wrong, say what made it reasonable and what it missed. If it was right, say so and go a level deeper.
4. **Give the teach-back** in one line.
5. **Offer the next stop**, and mention once that they can jump to any stop.

### Stop 6: the deliverable

Make a real, small deliverable from what the tour found: a single self-contained page built around the one reveal the person reacted to most. It carries the full signature. This is where the person sees that the result of a run is a finished thing and not a chat transcript. Add the share pack's static discovery card.

### Stop 7: review

There is nothing yet to review, so show how review works: write down, with the person, one prediction about something in their own life or work that a method from the tour applies to, and when they will know. Explain that when they come back, the system will ask what happened.

### Stop 8: the swap

Replace one method with another and rerun only that stop: for example, replace Loops at stop 2 with Incentives. Put the two results side by side. This is the moment many people understand that methods are ways of looking, and that they can choose.

## Ending

Show the route strip for the whole tour. Say which methods the person predicted correctly. Then:

> Now open a system for something of your own.

Offer three concrete ways to do it, grounded in what the person said during the tour: a subject they mentioned, "open a system for me", or a decision they are facing. Proceed with whichever they pick. The usual location choice applies the first time something of theirs is opened.

## Judgment

- **Ten minutes means ten minutes.** If a stop is taking long, shorten the rest. A finished short tour is better than a thorough half.
- **The predictions matter more than the explanations.** A person who predicted and was surprised remembers. A person who was told does not.
- **If the person stops answering the prediction questions**, stop asking and keep going.
- **Do not turn stops into a checklist on screen.** The table above is for you. The person should experience a conversation that keeps turning up something interesting.
- **Be honest about the materials.** They are awaiting verification against primary sources; say so once, and check key figures if the host can search.
- **Leave the tour whenever the person's own subject appears.** That is the tour succeeding early.
