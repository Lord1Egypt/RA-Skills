# The share pack

> **When to read**: a full run is ending; the person asks for something to post; you are making a discovery card, a short clip, post drafts or a panel card; or the video runtime is missing.

Every full run ends with a **share pack**: a small set of things the person can post themselves. The reason is simple. A finding that changes how someone sees a subject is worth passing on, and most people will not turn a page into a post on their own.

**You never post anything.** You prepare; the person decides and publishes.

## What is in it

| Item | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Discovery card** | Images in 1:1 and 9:16: the reveal in one sentence, the method label, the route strip, a source line, the OpenXSys mark |
| **Short clip** | 15 to 45 seconds, 9:16, made with the bundled video skill: the question, the reveal, two key visuals, the route |
| **Post drafts** | Short drafts in the person's language for X and the platforms they use, each leading with the reveal, never with the product |
| **Replay link** | A link to a replay of the run, when the person has published one |
| **Alt text** | For every image |

A "full run" is a run that took a route and produced a deliverable. A quick answer, a single follow-up question or a "just the answer" request does not get a share pack unless the person asks. When the person has said they do not want share packs, stop making them and record it.

## The discovery card

The card carries one thing: the reveal.

- **The reveal in one sentence**, in the shape "you think … → actually …" or its equivalent, readable in three seconds at phone size. It is the same reveal as in the deliverable, not a punchier variant that the research does not support.
- **The method label**: the one method or route that did the decisive work.
- **The route strip**, small.
- **A source line**: the main source and its date. A card without a source line is not made.
- **The OpenXSys mark** and, by default, the small "made with OpenXSys" credit.

Design:

- **Native to the subject**, like the deliverable it comes from. Its look is drawn from the deliverable (its typeface, palette, a key visual), so the card and the page are visibly one piece of work. There is no card template.
- **Text first.** If the reveal reads well as type alone, type alone is right. One visual at most, and only if it carries the reveal.
- **Two formats, each composed**: 1:1 (1080 × 1080) and 9:16 (1080 × 1920). The tall one is recomposed for its shape, not the square one stretched.
- **Legible when small**: large type, strong contrast, generous margins, nothing important in the outer 6% (platform interfaces cover it).

How it is made:

- **Author the card as a self-contained SVG or HTML file** at the exact pixel size, with real text. This works on any machine, with nothing installed, and is the **static card**.
- **Export PNG when a renderer is available**: the host's browser tool or screenshot ability, or a bundled skill. Say which files were produced.
- **Without a renderer**, deliver the SVG or HTML card and say in one line how to turn it into an image (open it and take a screenshot, or export from the browser).

## The short clip

Made when the video dependency and its runtime are ready (see `deps/DEPENDENCIES.md`).

- **15 to 45 seconds, 9:16**, readable with the sound off: every spoken idea is also on screen.
- **Four beats**: the question (what people assume) → the reveal → two key visuals that show why → the route, with the source line and the mark.
- **The first two seconds carry the question.** Someone scrolling decides there.
- **Same facts as the deliverable.** The clip compresses; it does not add claims.
- **Ready for** X, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Douyin, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu: vertical, captions inside the safe area, no platform's logo.
- **Sound** is optional and never required to follow it. Use only audio the person has the right to publish.

When the runtime is missing, there is no clip. Deliver the page and the static card and say once what video would add. Do not apologize at length and do not repeat it every run.

## Post drafts

- **Lead with the reveal**, not with OpenXSys and not with "I asked an AI". The finding is the content.
- **In the person's language and voice.** If the root system knows how they write and where they post, use that. Otherwise write plainly and say what you assumed.
- **Fitted to each platform**: a single post and, where the reveal needs steps, a short thread for X; a title and description for video platforms; a caption for image platforms. Draft only for the platforms the person uses or names.
- **True at the length of a post.** Shortening must not turn a conditional finding into an unconditional one. If the reveal needs its condition to be true, the condition stays.
- **A source in the post or its first reply**, so readers can check.
- **The tag** *#OpenASystem* is offered, not inserted by force.
- **No engagement tricks**: no bait, no false urgency, no claims of what "nobody knows".

## Alt text

Every image in the pack has alt text that says what the image shows and what it means: the reveal sentence, the method, and the source. It is written for someone who cannot see the card, not as a list of visual details.

## The replay link

A replay shows the sentence typed, the findings as they arrived, the route chosen and the deliverable. Publishing one is the person's act. When they have published one, include its link in the pack and the drafts. Never fabricate or reconstruct a replay to look more impressive than the run was.

## Share-safe by default

The rules are in `system/consent.md`. In brief:

- A pack about an outside subject contains nothing about the person.
- A pack about the person's own system shows the method and the kind of finding and hides specifics unless the person chooses each one.
- A panel card shows counts, methods and level, and hides names and details unless the person chooses.
- No reveal is made shareable without a source that supports it.

## The panel card and the System Review card

Made the same way as a discovery card, in the same two formats, from the panel or the monthly review (see `system/panel.md`). They show the person's own numbers, so the share-safe defaults matter most here.

## Where the pack goes

Next to the deliverable, in a folder the person can see, with plain file names that say what each file is. Tell the person in one or two lines what is in it. Record in the system entry where the pack is.

## What we never do

No fake accounts, no paid fake engagement, no automatic posting, no exaggerated claims, no reveals that the sources do not back. Spread built on false claims destroys the one thing that makes a result worth sharing.
